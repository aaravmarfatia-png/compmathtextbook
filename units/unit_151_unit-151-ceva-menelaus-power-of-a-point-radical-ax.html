Reading

1. Ceva and Menelaus

Ceva. If cevians AD, BE, CF of a triangle are concurrent, then (AF/FB)(BD/DC)(CE/EA) = 1, and conversely. Menelaus. If a line meets lines BC, CA, AB at D, E, F, then (AF/FB)(BD/DC)(CE/EA) = −1 with signed lengths. Example: if BD : DC = 1 : 2 and CE : EA = 3 : 1 in a concurrent configuration, then AF/FB = (2 · 1)/(1 · 3) = 2/3. For the angle bisectors, BD/DC = AB/AC, so Ceva immediately proves the bisectors are concurrent.

Try it

Concurrent cevians with BD/DC = 2 and CE/EA = 3. What is AF/FB?

Answer. 1/6.

Try it

Menelaus with BD/DC = 1/2, CE/EA = 2, what is |AF/FB|?

Answer. 1/(1/2 · 2) = 1.

2. Power of a point, radical axis and inversion

The power of a point P with respect to a circle is PA·PB for any line through P meeting the circle at A, B; it equals PT² for a tangent length PT from outside. Two circles have a radical axis, the line of points of equal power; it is found by subtracting the equations (for x² + y² = 25 and (x − 8)² + y² = 25 it is x = 4). Three circles have a radical centre. Inversion about O with radius k sends P to P′ on ray OP with OP·OP′ = k²; a circle through O becomes a line not through O.

Try it

PA = 3, PB = 12 for an outside secant; PT?

Answer. 6.

Try it

Inversion radius 6, OP = 9; OP′?

Answer. 4.

3. A worked problem

Problem. Circles x² + y² = 25 and (x − 8)² + y² = 25 meet at two points. Find the common chord. Solution. Subtract: x² − (x − 8)² = 0 gives 16x − 64 = 0, x = 4, so y² = 25 − 16 = 9 and the chord has length 6.

Check. (4, 3) is 5 from (0, 0) and from (8, 0) ✓.

Problem p1. In triangle ABC, D lies on BC with BD : DC = 1 : 2, and E lies on CA with CE : EA = 3 : 1. The cevians AD and BE meet at P, and line CP meets AB at F. What is AF : FB, as a fraction AF/FB?

HintCeva's theorem: (AF/FB)(BD/DC)(CE/EA) = 1.
Answer2/3
Method: Ceva

(AF/FB)·(1/2)·(3/1) = 1, so AF/FB = 2·1/(1·3) = 2/3.

Method: Mass points

Mass points: choose mass(C)=1·... the balancing masses give F dividing AB with AF : FB = mass(B) : mass(A) = 2/3 (checked with coordinates).

Source: Technique from Kedlaya ch. 5, 6, 10 (original problem)

Problem p9. Two chords AB and CD of a circle meet at an interior point P with PA = 6, PB = 4 and PC = 3. What is PD?

HintPower of a point: PA·PB = PC·PD.
Answer8
Method: Power of a point

PD = PA·PB/PC = 6·4/3 = 8.

Method: Similar triangles

Triangles PAC and PDB are similar, so PA/PD = PC/PB and PD = 8.

Problem p16. Circle 1 has centre (0, 0) and radius 5; circle 2 has centre (8, 0) and radius 5. They meet at two points. What is the length of their common chord?

HintThe common chord lies on the radical axis: subtract the two circle equations.
Answer6
Method: Radical axis

Subtracting x² + y² = 25 from (x − 8)² + y² = 25 gives x = 4; then y² = 25 − 16 = 9, so y = ±3 and the chord is 6.

Method: Right triangles

The chord is perpendicular to the line of centres at distance 4 from the first centre, so half-chord = √(5² − 4²) = 3 and the chord is 6.

Source: Technique from Kedlaya ch. 5, 6, 10 (original problem)

Problem p19. Circle 1 is x² + y² − 6x − 4y − 12 = 0 and circle 2 is x² + y² + 2x − 8y + 4 = 0. The radical axis meets the x-axis at the point (a, 0). What is a?

HintSubtract the two equations to get the line of equal power.
Answer-2
Method: Subtraction

Circle 1 − circle 2 gives −8x + 4y − 16 = 0. At y = 0: x = −2.

Method: Power

The point (a, 0) has equal powers: a² − 6a − 12 = a² + 2a + 4, so −8a = 16 and a = −2.

Problem p20. The circles x² + y² − 4x + 2y − 4 = 0, x² + y² + 2x − 4y − 5 = 0 and x² + y² − 2x − 2y − 8 = 0 have a common radical centre (h, k). What is 6(h + k)?

HintThe radical centre is where the three pairwise radical axes meet.
Answer-21
Method: Two axes

Subtracting the first two gives −6x + 6y + 1 = 0; subtracting the first and third gives −2x + 4y + 4 = 0. Solving gives (h, k) = (-5/3, -11/6), so 6(h + k) = -21.

Method: Equal power

The point (h, k) has the same power 185/36 with respect to all three circles (computed from each equation), so it lies on every axis.

Source: Technique from Kedlaya ch. 5, 6, 10 (original problem)

Problem p21. Inversion about O with radius 6 sends P to P′ on ray OP with OP · OP′ = 6². If OP = 4, what is OP′?

HintThe definition of inversion.
Answer9
Method: Definition

OP′ = 6²/4 = 9.

Method: Fixed circle

P is inside the circle of radius 6 exactly when P′ is outside it; here OP′ = 9.

Problem p23. A circle passes through O and has diameter OA = 4. Under inversion about O with radius 6, its image is a line perpendicular to OA. How far is that line from O?

HintInvert the far end A of the diameter.
Answer9
Method: Invert A

A is at distance 4, so A′ is at distance 6²/4 = 9 along the same ray; the image line passes through A′ perpendicular to OA.

Method: Coordinates

Points of the circle are (D cos²t, D cos t sin t); their images all have x-coordinate k²/D = 9.

Problem p24. The incircle of triangle ABC with BC = 13, CA = 14, AB = 15 touches BC at D, CA at E and AB at F. What is AF?

HintTangent lengths from a vertex are equal; AF = s − a.
Answer8
Method: Tangent lengths

s = 21 and AF = AE = s − BC = 8.

Method: System

Let AF = x, BF = y, CD = z; then x + y = 15, y + z = 13, z + x = 14 and x = 8.
