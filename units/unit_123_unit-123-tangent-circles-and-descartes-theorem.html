Book: Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 5 (inversion applications) (25 problems)
Coxeter & Greitzer, Geometry Revisited, chapter 5, with Descartes' circle theorem as an application.

Reading

1. Tangent circles: distances and tangent lengths

Two circles with radii r₁ and r₂ are externally tangent when the distance between centres is r₁ + r₂ and internally tangent when it is |r₁ − r₂|. For two externally tangent circles, the length of a common external tangent segment between the points of contact is 2√(r₁r₂).

Worked example. Circles of radii 4 and 9 are externally tangent. The centres are 13 apart and the radii differ by 5, so the tangent has length √(13² − 5²) = 12 = 2√(4 × 9) ✓.

The circle between. A small circle tangent to both circles and to the common tangent line has radius r with 1/√r = 1/√r₁ + 1/√r₂. For radii 4 and 9: 1/2 + 1/3 = 5/6, so r = 36/25 = 1.44.

2. Descartes' circle theorem

For four mutually tangent circles with curvatures k = 1/r (negative if the circle encloses the others), (k₁ + k₂ + k₃ + k₄)² = 2(k₁² + k₂² + k₃² + k₄²). Given three circles, this is a quadratic for k₄ with two solutions k₄ = k₁ + k₂ + k₃ ± 2√(k₁k₂ + k₂k₃ + k₃k₁).

Worked example 1. Three unit circles mutually tangent: k = 1, 1, 1. Then k₄ = 3 ± 2√3. The plus sign gives k₄ = 6.464 (radius 0.1547), the small circle in the gap; the minus sign gives k₄ = −0.464, the large circle that encloses all three (radius 2.155).

Worked example 2. The curvatures (−1, 2, 2, 3) satisfy the theorem: the sum is 6 and its square is 36, and 2(1 + 4 + 4 + 9) = 36 ✓. This is a circle of radius 1 containing two circles of radius ½ and one of radius ⅓.

3. Using the theorem

After finding one new circle you can feed any three of the four into the formula to find yet another, which produces an infinite packing (the Apollonian gasket). Starting from (−1, 2, 2, 3), the next curvature from (−1, 2, 3) is 4 ± 2√(−2 + 6 − 3) = 4 ± 2: so 6 or 2 (the 2 already present). The new circle has curvature 6.

Reducing to lines. A line is a circle with curvature 0. Two tangent circles of radius 1 and a common tangent line: k = 0, 1, 1 gives k₄ = 2 ± 2√(1) = 4 or 0. So the circle in the gap has radius ¼. ✓ (Check with the earlier formula for a circle in the gap: 1/√r = 1/√1 + 1/√1 = 2, so r = 1/4.)

Integer packings. Starting from integer curvatures, all further curvatures stay integers when the discriminant is a perfect square.

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. Forgetting the negative sign for an enclosing circle. Mixing up radius and curvature. Using the theorem when the circles are not mutually tangent.

Checks. Plug the final four curvatures into the original equation.

Practice. For every figure, first list curvatures, signs included.

Worked examples with Try-its: Descartes' circle theorem

For four mutually tangent circles with curvatures k = 1/r (negative for a circle enclosing the others) we have (k₁ + k₂ + k₃ + k₄)² = 2(k₁² + k₂² + k₃² + k₄²). With three unit circles k = 1, 1, 1 the fourth circle has k₄ = 3 ± 2√3. The small inner circle has k₄ = 3 + 2√3, so r = 1/(3 + 2√3) = (2√3 − 3)/3 ≈ 0.1547.

Try it

Three mutually tangent circles have radii 2, 3 and 6 (curvatures 1/2, 1/3, 1/6). What is the curvature k₄ of the small circle between them?

Answer. The curvatures sum to 1 and the pairwise products sum to 1/6 + 1/18 + 1/12 = 11/36, so k₄ = 1 + 2√(11/36) = 1 + √11/3.

Worked examples with Try-its: Tangent circles and lengths

Two circles of radii r and R tangent externally have centre distance r + R, and their common external tangent has length 2√(rR): for radii 2 and 3 that is 2√6. Chains of tangent circles and the line they touch often use the right triangle with hypotenuse r + R and one leg R − r.

Try it

Two externally tangent circles of radii 4 and 9 sit on a line. How far apart are their contact points with the line?

Answer. 2√36 = 12.

Worked examples with Try-its: A longer problem

Problem. Two circles of radii 1 and 4 touch externally and both touch a line. A third circle touches both circles and the line. Find its radius. Solution. In terms of curvature, 1/√r₃ = 1/√1 + 1/√4 = 3/2, so r₃ = 4/9.

Reflection. The relation 1/√r₃ = 1/√r₁ + 1/√r₂ is Descartes' theorem with a straight line (curvature 0).

More worked examples: Worked examples

Curvature. Take three mutually tangent circles of radii 1, 1 and 1/4 (curvatures 1, 1, 4). Descartes' theorem gives k₄ = k₁ + k₂ + k₃ ± 2√(k₁k₂ + k₂k₃ + k₃k₁) = 6 ± 2√9 = 12 or 0. The value 0 is a straight line tangent to all three circles.

Try it

Two circles of radii 1 and 3 touch externally. How long is their common external tangent segment?

Answer. 2√(1 · 3) = 2√3.

Try it

Write the curvature of a circle of radius 1/5.

Answer. 5.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. Two circles of radii 2 and 8 touch externally and a third small circle touches both and their common tangent line. Find its radius. Solution. 1/√r = 1/√2 + 1/√8 = 1/√2 + 1/(2√2) = 3/(2√2), so r = 8/9.

Check. √r = 2√2/3 gives r = 8/9.

Problem p1. Three mutually tangent circles have curvatures 2, 3 and 6. A fourth circle tangent to all three (in the gap) has curvature k. Find k.

Hintk₄ = k₁ + k₂ + k₃ + 2√(k₁k₂ + k₂k₃ + k₃k₁).
Answer23
Method: Formula

k = 11 + 2√(6 + 18 + 12) = 11 + 2 · 6 = 23, so the circle has radius 1/23.

Method: Other root

The other root is 11 − 12 = −1, the circle of radius 1 enclosing all three.

Source: Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 5 (inversion applications)

Problem x18. A point is 20 from the centre of a circle of radius 12. How long is a tangent segment from the point?

HintThe radius is perpendicular to the tangent.
Answer16
Method: Pythagoras

√(20² − 12²) = √256 = 16.

Method: Power of a point

The power of the point is 20² − 12² = 256, which is the tangent length squared, so 16.

Problem x19. Find the circumradius of the triangle with sides 6, 25 and 29.

HintR = abc/(4K).
Answer145/8
Method: Formula

K = 60, so R = 4350/(4 · 60) = 145/8.

Method: Law of sines

The altitude to side 29 is 120/29, so sin of the adjacent angle is 24/145; then 2R = 6/24/145 gives R = 145/8.
