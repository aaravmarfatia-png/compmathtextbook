1. Rotation as a tool

A rotation around a point preserves lengths and angles. Rotating part of a figure often turns scattered lengths into the sides of one triangle.

Worked example. P is inside square ABCD with PA = 1, PB = 2, PC = 3. Rotate the figure 90° about B so that C goes to A. Then P goes to a point P′ with BP′ = 2 and AP′ = CP = 3. Triangle BPP′ is right isosceles with PP′ = 2√2. In triangle APP′ the sides are 1, 2√2, 3, and 1² + (2√2)² = 9 = 3², so angle APP′ = 90°. Then angle APB = 90° + 45° = 135°, and by the law of cosines AB² = 1 + 4 − 2 × 1 × 2 × cos 135° = 5 + 2√2. The side is about 2.80.

2. Similarities and spiral similarity

A similarity multiplies all lengths by a fixed k. A spiral similarity combines a rotation about a point with a dilation from the same point; it sends A to A′ and B to B′ when the triangles OAA′ and OBB′ are similar. If two circles meet at O and X, a line through X meets them again at A and B; O is the centre of the spiral similarity sending one circle's figure to the other.

Worked example. A dilation with centre the origin and factor 3 followed by a rotation of 90° maps (1, 2) to (−6, 3). In complex form: multiply z = 1 + 2i by 3i: 3i − 6 = −6 + 3i ✓.

Fixed point. Every spiral similarity (not a translation) has exactly one fixed point.

3. Translations, glides, composition

Translating a segment can bring two separated lengths together, making a triangle or parallelogram. Worked example. In a trapezoid with parallel sides 10 and 4 and both legs equal to 5, translate one leg to form a triangle with sides 6, 5, 5 (the difference of the bases 10 − 4 = 6). Its area by Heron: s = 8, √(8 × 2 × 3 × 3) = 12, so the height is 2 × 12/6 = 4, and the trapezoid area is (10 + 4)/2 × 4 = 28.

Composition. Two rotations compose to a rotation (or translation if the angles add to 360°). For example, a rotation by 60° about one vertex of an equilateral triangle followed by a rotation by 60° about another gives a rotation by 120°, whose centre can be found by following one point.

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. Rotating in the wrong direction. Rotating about a point that does not make the lengths line up. Forgetting that a similarity scales areas by k².

Checks. After the rotation, check that the lengths you want are now sides of one triangle.

Practice. Try the same problem with a translation, a rotation and a reflection before choosing.

Worked examples with Try-its: Types of transformation

Isometries preserve distance: translations, rotations, reflections and glide reflections. Similarities also allow dilation. A linear map with matrix M multiplies areas by |det M|: the stretch [[2, 0], [0, 3]] has determinant 6, so areas grow six-fold, while the shear [[1, 1], [0, 1]] has determinant 1 and preserves area.

Try it

A map has matrix [[3, 1], [2, 4]]. By what factor does it scale areas?

Answer. det = 12 − 2 = 10.

Worked examples with Try-its: Composition

Two reflections in parallel lines compose to a translation; two reflections in intersecting lines compose to a rotation about their intersection by twice the angle between the lines. A homothety followed by another with the same centre multiplies the ratios: ratios 2 and 3 give ratio 6.

Try it

Which single transformation equals a rotation of 40° followed by a rotation of 50° about the same point?

Answer. A rotation of 90° about that point.

Worked examples with Try-its: A longer problem

Problem. The triangle with vertices (0, 0), (2, 0), (0, 1) is mapped by (x, y) ↦ (2x + y, x + y). Find the image area. Solution. The determinant of [[2, 1], [1, 1]] is 1 and the original area is 1, so the image has area 1. The image vertices are (0, 0), (4, 2), (1, 1), whose triangle area ½|4 · 1 − 2 · 1| = 1 ✓.

Reflection. Always compute the determinant first, then check with the vertices.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Reflection. The matrix [[0, 1], [1, 0]] reflects in y = x, and applying it twice gives the identity. Dilation. The matrix [[2, 0], [0, 2]] doubles lengths and multiplies areas by det = 4. Composition. A rotation by 60° followed by a rotation by 30° is a rotation by 90°.

Try it

What does the matrix [[1, 0], [0, −1]] do to the point (3, 4)?

Answer. Sends it to (3, −4): reflection in the x-axis.

Try it

A transformation has determinant −1. What can you say about orientation?

Answer. It reverses orientation (like a reflection) and preserves area.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. The unit square is mapped by [[2, 1], [0, 3]]. Find the area of the image and the image of the vertex (1, 1). Solution. The determinant is 6, so the area is 6. The point (1, 1) goes to (2 + 1, 0 + 3) = (3, 3).

Check. The image is the parallelogram with vertices (0, 0), (2, 0), (3, 3), (1, 3), whose area is 2 · 3 = 6 ✓.

Problem p1. Reflect the point (3, 1) across the line 1x + 1y = 0. Find the sum of the coordinates of the image.

HintThe segment from the point to its image is perpendicular to the line, and its midpoint lies on the line.
Answer-4
Method: Formula

Subtract twice the signed distance along the normal (1, 1): d = (1·3 + 1·1 − 0)/2 = 2, so the image is (-1, -3), with sum -4.

Method: Midpoint check

The midpoint of the point and the image is (1, -1), which gives 0 on the left of the line equation, equal to 0 as required.

Problem p5. A point P moves on the x-axis. For A = (1, 3) and B = (7, 5), the smallest possible value of (AP + PB)² is?

HintReflect A across the x-axis.
Answer100
Method: Reflection

Reflect A to A′ = (1, -3). Then AP = A′P, and A′P + PB is smallest when P lies on segment A′B. So the minimum is A′B = √(6² + 8²), squared is 100.

Method: Coordinates

Let P = (t, 0). The sum √((t − 1)² + 9) + √((t − 7)² + 25) is minimised where the two lines meet at equal angles, which is the same point as on A′B, giving the same minimum 100.

Source: Andreescu & Gelca, 1.6

Problem p8. Rotate the point (5, 2) by 90° counterclockwise about (1, 1). Find the product of the coordinates of the image.

HintSubtract the centre, rotate (x, y) to (−y, x), then add the centre back.
Answer0
Method: Vector

Relative to the centre the point is (4, 1). Rotating gives (-1, 4). Adding the centre back gives (0, 5), with product 0.

Method: Check

The image is 17 from the centre in each case: distance² = 17, same as before, and the dot product of the two relative vectors is 0, so the angle is 90°.

Source: Andreescu & Gelca, 1.6

Problem p14. The translation by (3, 4) is followed by the translation by (-2, 5). The combined translation moves every point a distance d. Find d².

HintAdd the vectors.
Answer82
Method: Add

The combined vector is (1, 9), so d² = 1² + 9² = 82.

Method: Follow a point

Start at the origin: it goes to (3, 4) and then to (1, 9), at distance² 82.

Problem p16. A triangle of area 3 is enlarged by a scale factor of 2. What is the area of the image?

HintAreas scale by the square of the ratio.
Answer12
Method: Square rule

3 · 2² = 12.

Method: Example

A right triangle with legs 2s and 2 · 1 enlarged by k has legs multiplied by 2, so area multiplies by 4: 12.

Problem p20. Rotating 180° about (1, 2) and then 180° about (4, 6) is a translation. By how much does it move each point? Give d².

HintFollow a single point.
Answer100
Method: Follow point A

The point A goes to itself in the first rotation, and then to 2B − A in the second. So the translation is 2(B − A) = (6, 8), with d² = 100.

Method: Formula

A half-turn about C sends P to 2C − P. Applying it twice sends P to 2B − (2A − P) = P + 2(B − A), giving d² = 100.

Source: Andreescu & Gelca, 1.6
