1. Distance, midpoint and slope

Coordinates turn geometry into algebra. The distance between (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂) is √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²), the Pythagorean theorem in disguise: from (1, 2) to (4, 6) it is √(9 + 16) = 5. The midpoint is the average of the coordinates: ((1 + 7)/2, (2 + 10)/2) = (4, 6) for the segment from (1, 2) to (7, 10). The slope of a line through two points is rise over run, (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁); the slope from (1, 2) to (5, 6) is 1.

Parallel and perpendicular. Parallel lines have equal slopes; perpendicular lines have slopes whose product is −1 (m and −1/m). The perpendicular bisector of the segment from (0, 0) to (6, 2) passes through the midpoint (3, 1) with slope −3, so its equation is y − 1 = −3(x − 3), i.e. 3x + y = 10.

Equations of lines. Slope-intercept y = mx + b; point-slope y − y₁ = m(x − x₁); standard form ax + by = c. The line 2x + 3y = 12 meets the axes at (6, 0) and (0, 4), forming a triangle of area ½ · 6 · 4 = 12.

Try it

Find the equation of the line through (1, 2) and (5, 10).

Answer. Slope 8/4 = 2, so y − 2 = 2(x − 1), i.e. y = 2x.

2. Distance from a point to a line

The distance from the point (p, q) to the line ax + by = c is |ap + bq − c|/√(a² + b²). For the point (3, 4) and the line 3x + 4y = 5: |9 + 16 − 5|/5 = 4. For two parallel lines 3x + 4y = 5 and 3x + 4y = 20, the distance is |20 − 5|/5 = 3. The formula follows from projecting onto the normal direction (a, b).

Area of a triangle from coordinates. For a triangle with vertices (0, 0), (6, 0), (2, 5) the area is ½ · 6 · 5 = 15. For general vertices use the shoelace formula. A triangle has area zero exactly when its three vertices are collinear, a useful test.

Reflections. Reflecting (3, 1) over the line y = x swaps the coordinates, giving (1, 3); over the x-axis gives (3, −1). To reflect over a general line, drop a perpendicular, find its foot, and double the displacement.

Try it

Find the distance from (2, 1) to the line x + y = 7.

Answer. |2 + 1 − 7|/√2 = 4/√2 = 2√2.

3. Circles

The circle with centre (h, k) and radius r has equation (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r². The circle (x − 2)² + (y + 1)² = 25 has centre (2, −1) and radius 5, and passes through (5, 3) since 9 + 16 = 25. The tangent at (5, 3) is perpendicular to the radius, whose direction is (3, 4), so its equation is 3x + 4y = 27; the distance from the centre to this line is |6 − 4 − 27|/5 = 5, equal to the radius, as a tangent requires.

Circle through three points. The circle through (0, 0), (4, 0), (0, 2) has centre (2, 1) (found as the intersection of the perpendicular bisectors x = 2 and y = 1) and r² = 5; each of the three points is at squared distance 5 from the centre. A right triangle's circumcentre is the midpoint of its hypotenuse: for (0, 0), (6, 0), (0, 8) the circumcentre is (3, 4) and the radius 5.

Line meets circle. Substitute: for y = x + 1 and x² + y² = 13 we get x² + (x + 1)² = 13, so 2x² + 2x − 12 = 0, x² + x − 6 = 0, and x = 2 or x = −3: the points (2, 3) and (−3, −2). The discriminant tells how many intersection points: positive gives two, zero a tangent, negative none.

Try it

Find the centre and radius of x² + y² − 6x + 4y − 12 = 0.

Answer. Complete the squares: (x − 3)² + (y + 2)² = 25. Centre (3, −2), radius 5.

4. A longer problem

Problem. Triangle ABC has A = (0, 0), B = (8, 0), C = (2, 6). Find (a) the area, (b) the circumcentre and circumradius, (c) the distance from C to the line through A and B, and (d) the equation of the median from A.

Solution. (a) Base 8, height 6: area 24. (b) The perpendicular bisector of AB is x = 4. The perpendicular bisector of AC: midpoint (1, 3), slope of AC is 3 so perpendicular slope −1/3: y − 3 = −(x − 1)/3. At x = 4, y = 3 − 1 = 2. The circumcentre is (4, 2) and R = √(16 + 4) = √20; check with C: (2 − 4)² + (6 − 2)² = 20 ✓. (c) 6. (d) The midpoint of BC is (5, 3), so the median has slope 3/5 and equation y = 3x/5.

Reflection. Every step came from one of the tools above. If an answer can be checked by substituting a given point (as C into the circle), do it.

5. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. (1) Forgetting the square root in the distance formula. (2) Using slope m instead of −1/m for perpendicular lines. (3) Sign errors when completing the square. (4) Dividing by zero for vertical lines (their slope is undefined; use x = c).

Checks. Substitute known points into the equation. Check that the circumcentre is equidistant from all three vertices. Draw a quick sketch.

How to practise. Sketch every problem. In Unlimited practice, rotate the line, distance and circle families.
