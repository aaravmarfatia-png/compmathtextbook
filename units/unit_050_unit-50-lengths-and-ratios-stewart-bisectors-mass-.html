1. Stewart's theorem and the length of a cevian

Let D lie on side BC of triangle ABC with BD = m, DC = n, BC = a = m + n, and let d = AD. Stewart's theorem: b²m + c²n = a(d² + mn), where b = CA and c = AB. For the 13-14-15 triangle with BD = 6, DC = 8 (so m = 6, n = 8, b = 15, c = 13): 225 · 6 + 169 · 8 = 14(d² + 48), giving d² = 2702/14 − 48 = 145. So AD = √145 (matches the coordinate computation in Unit 33). Medians: m = n = a/2 gives the median-length formula; here d² = 148. Angle bisector: BD : DC = c : b = 13 : 15, and its length satisfies d² = bc(1 − a²/(b + c)²) = 195(1 − 196/784) = 146.25.

The angle bisector theorem. The bisector of angle A divides the opposite side in the ratio of the adjacent sides: BD/DC = AB/AC. In the 13-14-15 triangle BD = 14 · 13/28 = 6.5.

Try it

In a triangle with AB = 5, AC = 7, BC = 8, the bisector of angle A meets BC at D. Find BD.

Answer. BD = 8 · 5/12 = 10/3.

2. Mass points

Mass points turn ratio problems into balance problems. Assign masses to the vertices so that on each cevian the point where it meets the side balances the masses at its ends: if BD : DC = 2 : 3, put mass 3 at B and mass 2 at C, so D has mass 5. If CE : EA = 1 : 2 then masses at C and A are in the ratio 2 : 1 — consistent with C = 2 if A = 1. Scale to make C agree: B = 3, C = 2, A = 1.

The intersection P of AD and BE has mass 6 (1 + 3 + 2), and on the cevian AD the ratio AP : PD = (mass of D) : (mass of A) = 5 : 1, so AP = (5/6)AD. A coordinate computation with A = (0, 0), B = (10, 0), C = (0, 10) gives AP/AD = 5/6 ✓.

When it works. Mass points handle ratios on cevians that are concurrent; for a transversal, use Menelaus. The method is Ceva's theorem in physical clothing.

Try it

In triangle ABC, D on BC with BD : DC = 1 : 2 and E on AC with AE : EC = 3 : 1. Cevians AD and BE meet at P. Find BP : PE.

Answer. Give B mass 2 and C mass 1 so that BD : DC = 1 : 2 (D has mass 3). For AE : EC = 3 : 1 the masses at A and C are in the ratio 1 : 3, so scale the first assignment by 3: B = 6, C = 3, D = 9, and A = 1, E = A + C = 4. Then BP : PE = (mass of E) : (mass of B) = 4 : 6 = 2 : 3, which a coordinate check confirms.

3. A longer problem

Problem. In triangle ABC, AB = 9, AC = 12, BC = 15. D is the foot of the angle bisector from A. (a) Find BD and DC. (b) Find AD. (c) Is the triangle right-angled?

Solution. (c) 9² + 12² = 225 = 15², so yes, right-angled at A. (a) BD = 15 · 9/21 = 45/7, DC = 60/7. (b) AD² = bc(1 − a²/(b + c)²) = 108(1 − 225/441) = 108 · 216/441 = 23328/441, so AD = 108√2/21 = 36√2/7.

Reflection. Because the angle at A is right, the bisector makes a 45° angle with each leg and AD = (bc√2)/(b + c) = 108√2/21 ✓, an easy check.

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. (1) Writing Stewart's theorem with the wrong pairing of sides and segments. (2) Mixing up which segment is m and which is n. (3) Reversing the mass assignment (the longer segment gets the smaller mass). (4) Using mass points when the cevians are not concurrent.

Checks. Test Stewart's theorem on a median or an altitude where you know the answer.

How to practise. Write m, n, a, b, c on a diagram first. In Unlimited practice, rotate the Stewart and mass-point families.

More worked examples: Worked examples

In triangle ABC with AB = 6, AC = 9, BC = 10, the angle bisector from A meets BC at D with BD : DC = 6 : 9, so BD = 4 and DC = 6. Stewart's theorem gives AC² · BD + AB² · DC = BC(AD² + BD · DC): 81 · 4 + 36 · 6 = 540 = 10(AD² + 24), so AD² = 30. The bisector-length formula bc(1 − a²/(b + c)²) = 54(1 − 100/225) = 30 agrees.

Try it

In triangle ABC, AB = 4 and AC = 6, BC = 5. Where does the angle bisector from A meet BC (distance from B)?

Answer. BD = 5 · 4/10 = 2.

Try it

A cevian divides BC in the ratio 2 : 3. What masses at B and C balance the point?

Answer. Mass 3 at B and 2 at C.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. In triangle ABC, D lies on BC with BD : DC = 1 : 2 and E is the midpoint of AD. The line BE meets AC at F. Find AF : FC using mass points. Solution. Give C mass 1 and B mass 2 so D has mass 3. E is the midpoint of AD, so A has mass 3. Then F on AC has mass 3 + 1 = 4 and AF : FC = 1 : 3.

Check with coordinates. Take B = (0, 0), C = (3, 0), A = (0, 3), so D = (1, 0), E = (1/2, 3/2). The line BE is y = 3x, meeting AC (x + y = 3) at x = 3/4, y = 9/4. Then AF/AC = (3/4)/3 = 1/4, so AF : FC = 1 : 3 ✓.

Practice problems (25)

Problem p1. In triangle ABC, AB = 7, AC = 9, BC = 8. D is on BC with BD = 3 and DC = 5. Find AD².

HintStewart's theorem.
Answer46
Method: Stewart

AB²·DC + AC²·BD = BC(AD² + BD·DC): 49·5 + 81·3 = 488 = 8(AD² + 15), so AD² = 61 − 15 = 46.

Method: Coordinates

Put B = (0,0), C = (8,0), A = (x, y) with x² + y² = 49 and (x − 8)² + y² = 81. Subtracting: 16x − 64 = −32, so x = 2 and y² = 45. D = (3, 0), so AD² = 1 + 45 = 46.

Source: Chen, Euclidean Geometry in Mathematical Olympiads, ch. 3

Problem p2. In triangle ABC, AB = 6, AC = 3 and BC = 5. Find the square of the length of the bisector from A, as a fraction.

HintLength² = bc(1 − a²/(b + c)²).
Answer112/9
Method: Formula

bc = 18 and a²/(b + c)² = 25/81, so length² = 18·56/81 = 112/9.

Method: Bisector theorem and Stewart

BD = 5·6/9 = 10/3, DC = 5/3; Stewart gives 36·(5/3) + 9·(10/3) = 90 = 5(AD² + 50/9), so AD² = 18 − 50/9 = 112/9.

Source: Chen, Euclidean Geometry in Mathematical Olympiads, ch. 3

Problem p3. In triangle ABC, D is the midpoint of BC and E lies on AC with AE : EC = 1 : 2. AD and BE meet at P. Find AP / PD.

HintMass points: B = C = 1.
Answer1
Method: Mass points

B and C have mass 1, so D has mass 2. For AE : EC = 1 : 2 give C mass 1 and A mass 2. Then AP : PD = (mass of D) : (mass of A) = 2 : 2 = 1.

Method: Coordinates

With A = (0,0), B = (2,0), C = (0,3): D = (1, 1.5), E = (0,1). BE: x/2 + y = 1, AD: y = 1.5x. They meet at x = 0.5, y = 0.75, the midpoint of AD, so AP/PD = 1.

Source: Chen, Euclidean Geometry in Mathematical Olympiads, ch. 3

Problem p4. In a trapezoid with parallel sides 12 and 18, one diagonal is 20 long. The diagonals cross at a point. How long is the longer part of that diagonal?

HintThe diagonals divide each other in the ratio 12 : 18.
Answer12
Method: Similar triangles

The two triangles formed with the parallel sides are similar with ratio 12 : 18 = 2 : 3. The diagonal of length 20 is cut into 8 and 12, so the longer part is 12.

Method: Ratio

Parts 2/5 and 3/5 of 20 are 8 and 12.

Problem p5. In a trapezoid with parallel sides 12 and 18, a segment parallel to them passes through the intersection of the diagonals with endpoints on the legs. How long is it? (As a fraction.)

HintIt is the harmonic mean of the bases.
Answer72/5
Method: Harmonic mean

The length is 2ab/(a + b) = 2·12·18/30 = 72/5.

Method: Similar triangles

The diagonals meet in the ratio 2 : 3, so the parallel segment through that point is 12·3/5 + 18·2/5 = 36/5 + 36/5 = 72/5.

Answer5

BD/DC = 10/14 = 5/7 and BD + DC = 12, so BD = 12·5/12 = 5.

Method: Check

DC = 7 and 5/7 = 10/14.

Problem p7. Triangle ABC has area 60. D is the midpoint of AB and E lies on BC with BE : EC = 1 : 2. Find the area of triangle BDE.

HintMultiply the ratios BD/BA and BE/BC.
Answer10
Method: Ratio of sides

BD/BA = 1/2 and BE/BC = 1/3, so area BDE = 60·(1/2)(1/3) = 10.

Problem p8. A right triangle has legs 6 and 8. The altitude to the hypotenuse divides it into two segments. How long is the shorter one? (As a fraction.)

HintLeg² = hypotenuse segment × hypotenuse.
Answer18/5
Method: Leg theorem

The hypotenuse is 10, and 6² = 10·s gives s = 3.6 = 18/5, the shorter segment (the other is 6.4).

Method: Similar triangles

The small triangle with hypotenuse 6 is similar to the big one with ratio 6/10, so its short side is 6·6/10 = 18/5.

Problem x11. In triangle ABC, D is the midpoint of BC and E lies on AC with AE : EC = 1 : 2. AD and BE meet at P. Find AP/PD.

HintMass points.
Answer1
Method: Masses

A = 2, C = 1 balance E; B = C = 1 balance D with mass 2. Then AP/PD = 2/2 = 1.

Method: Coordinates

Take A = (0, 0), B = (6, 0), C = (0, 3). Then E = (0, 1) and D = (3, 3/2). The line BE is x/6 + y = 1 and the point t · D on AD satisfies t/2 + 3t/2 = 1, so t = 1/2 and P is the midpoint of AD.

Problem x17. In triangle ABC, BC = 10, CA = 7 and AB = 9. D lies on BC with BD = 4. Find AD².

HintStewart's theorem.
Answer221/5
Method: Stewart

AD² = (CA² · BD + AB² · DC)/BC − BD · DC = (49 · 4 + 81 · 6)/10 − 24 = 221/5.

Method: Law of cosines

cos B = 11/15. Then AD² = AB² + BD² − 2 · AB · BD · cos B = 81 + 16 − 264/5 = 221/5.

Problem x18. A triangle has sides 5, 5 and 6. Find the square of the median to the side of length 5.

Hintm² = (2b² + 2c² − a²)/4.
Answer97/4
Method: Median formula

(2 · 25 + 2 · 36 − 25)/4 = 97/4.

Method: Parallelogram law

Doubling the median completes a parallelogram with diagonals 5 and 2m; the law gives 25 + 4m² = 2(25 + 36), so m² = 97/4.
