1. Similarity and the right triangle

Two triangles are similar if their angles are equal; then corresponding sides are proportional. Tests: AA, SAS (two sides proportional with the included angle equal), SSS. In a right triangle with legs a, b, hypotenuse c and altitude h to the hypotenuse, the altitude splits the triangle into two triangles similar to the original. This gives: h² = p q, a² = p c, b² = q c where p and q are the pieces of the hypotenuse.

Worked example 1. In a 3-4-5 triangle the altitude to the hypotenuse is h = 12/5. The pieces are p = 9/5 (next to the leg 3) and q = 16/5: check pq = 144/25 = h² ✓ and 3² = 5 × 9/5 ✓.

Worked example 2. In the 5-12-13 triangle: h = 60/13, with pieces 25/13 and 144/13. Check 25 × 144/169 = 3,600/169 = (60/13)² ✓.

2. Homothety

A homothety (dilation) with centre O and ratio k sends every point P to P′ on line OP with OP′ = k · OP. It maps every line to a parallel line and every circle to a circle, multiplying lengths by |k| and areas by k². Two similar figures with parallel corresponding sides are related by a homothety.

Worked example 1. The medial triangle (of the midpoints of the sides) is the image of the original triangle under a homothety centred at the centroid with ratio −1/2.

Worked example 2. A circle of radius 2 and a circle of radius 6 are externally tangent. The tangent point is the homothety centre (ratio −3) sending one onto the other.

Centres of similitude. Two circles with different radii have an external centre of similitude (on the line of centres, dividing it externally in the ratio of the radii) and an internal one. Common tangents pass through these points.

3. Useful similar-triangle arguments

Thales's theorem. A line parallel to one side of a triangle divides the other two sides proportionally. If DE ∥ BC with D on AB and E on AC, then AD/AB = AE/AC = DE/BC.

Worked example. In triangle ABC, DE ∥ BC with AD = 4, DB = 6, BC = 15. Then DE = 15 × 4/10 = 6 and the area ratio of ADE to ABC is (4/10)² = 4/25.

Shadows and heights. A pole of 3 m casts a 2 m shadow while a tree casts a 14 m shadow: tree height 21 m.

The geometric mean. In the right triangle, the altitude is the geometric mean of the two pieces of the hypotenuse, and each leg is the geometric mean of the hypotenuse and its adjacent piece.

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. Matching the wrong vertices in a similarity. Squaring ratios for lengths. Forgetting that a homothety with negative ratio flips the figure.

Checks. Check the ratios of all three pairs of sides.

Practice. Write the similarity in order: △ABC ∼ △DEF.

Worked examples with Try-its: The right triangle altitude

In a right triangle with legs a, b and hypotenuse c, the altitude h to the hypotenuse splits it into p and q (p + q = c) and creates two triangles similar to the original. Hence h² = pq, a² = pc, b² = qc and h = ab/c. For the 3-4-5 triangle h = 12/5, p = 9/5, q = 16/5 and indeed (12/5)² = 144/25 = (9/5)(16/5). For 5-12-13, h = 60/13.

Try it

In a right triangle the altitude to the hypotenuse divides it into pieces 4 and 9. How long is the altitude?

Answer. √36 = 6.

Worked examples with Try-its: Similarity and homothety

If two figures are similar with ratio k, lengths scale by k, areas by k² and volumes by k³. A homothety (dilation) with centre O and ratio k sends each point P to the point P′ on ray OP with OP′ = k · OP. The medial triangle is the image of the original under a homothety with ratio −1/2 about the centroid, so its area is a quarter of the original.

Try it

Two similar triangles have areas 18 and 50. What is the ratio of their sides?

Answer. 3 : 5.

Worked examples with Try-its: A longer problem

Problem. A tree casts a 12 m shadow when a 2 m pole casts a 3 m shadow. How tall is the tree? Solution. The triangles are similar, so the height is 12 · 2/3 = 8 m.

Reflection. Identify the matching vertices before writing the proportion.

More worked examples: Worked examples

In a right triangle with legs 6 and 8 the altitude to the hypotenuse is 6 · 8/10 = 24/5 = 4.8. If DE ∥ BC in triangle ABC with AD = 3 and DB = 5, then DE/BC = AD/AB = 3/8. A dilation with ratio 3 multiplies lengths by 3 and areas by 9.

Try it

Triangles ABC and DEF are similar with AB = 6, DE = 9. If the area of ABC is 20, what is the area of DEF?

Answer. 20 · (9/6)² = 45.

Try it

A 2 m pole casts a 3 m shadow while a tree casts a 15 m shadow. How tall is the tree?

Answer. 10 m.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. In right triangle ABC with right angle at C, CD is the altitude to AB, AD = 4 and DB = 9. Find CD, AC and BC. Solution. CD² = 4 · 9 = 36, so CD = 6. AC² = AD · AB = 4 · 13 = 52 and BC² = 9 · 13 = 117, so AC = 2√13 and BC = 3√13. Check: AC² + BC² = 169 = 13².

Reflection. The three similar triangles give all of these relations.

Problem p6. A trapezoid has parallel sides 6 and 10. A segment through the intersection of the diagonals, parallel to the bases, has endpoints on the legs. Find its length as a fraction.

HintIt is the harmonic mean 2ab/(a + b).

Method: Similar triangles

The diagonals divide each other in ratio 6:10, so the segment through the intersection has half-length (6·10)/(6 + 10) = 15/4 on each side, total 15/2.

Problem x19. Find the area of the triangle with sides 9, 12 and 15.

HintHeron's formula.
Answer54
Method: Heron

s = 18, so K = √(18 · 9 · 6 · 3) = 54.

Method: Altitude

The altitude to the side 15 is 2K/15 = 36/5, so the area is ½ · 15 · 36/5 = 54.
