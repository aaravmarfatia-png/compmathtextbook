1. Arithmetic functions

If n = ∏ p^e then τ(n) = ∏(e + 1), σ(n) = ∏(p^(e+1) − 1)/(p − 1) and φ(n) = n∏(1 − 1/p). For n = 12: τ = 6, σ = 28, φ = 4. The number of ordered pairs with lcm(a, b) = n is ∏(2e + 1); for n = 12 this is 5 · 3 = 15.

Legendre. v_p(n!) = Σ ⌊n/pᵏ⌋; so 25! ends in 5 + 1 = 6 zeros. Kummer. v_p of C(a + b, a) is the number of carries when adding a and b in base p. In particular v₂(C(2n, n)) is the number of 1's in the binary expansion of n.

Answer. 28.

Answer. 6.

2. Gcd identities

gcd(2^a − 1, 2^b − 1) = 2^gcd(a,b) − 1 because Euclid's algorithm acts on the exponents: for a = 12, b = 18 the answer is 2⁶ − 1 = 63. The same pattern holds for aⁿ − 1 and for Fibonacci numbers.

Try it

gcd(2⁸ − 1, 2¹² − 1)?

Answer. 2⁴ − 1 = 15.

3. A worked problem

Problem. How many positive divisors of 360 are perfect squares? Solution. 360 = 2³ · 3² · 5. A square divisor has even exponents: 2⁰ or 2², 3⁰ or 3², 5⁰, giving 2 · 2 · 1 = 4: 1, 4, 9, 36.

Check. 1, 4, 9, 36 all divide 360 and are squares ✓.

4. In the books

Khurmi, ch. 3. 3.1 number of divisors, 3.2 sum of divisors, 3.3 Euler's totient with Gauss's identity Σ φ(d) = n, 3.4 multiplicative functions (Theorem 3.4.1: if f is multiplicative so is Σ_{d|n} f(d)), Möbius inversion (Theorem 3.4.5), 3.5 floor and ceiling functions with Hermite's identity.

Ch. 6. Legendre's formula v_p(n!) = Σ ⌊n/pⁱ⌋, equivalent to (n − s_p(n))/(p − 1) with s_p the base-p digit sum. That second form is what gives the exponent of 2 in C(2n, n), the digit count of n in binary.

Problem p5. How many positive divisors of 2^6·3^4·5^2 are perfect squares?

HintA divisor is a square iff every exponent is even.
Answer24
Method: Even exponents

Exponents of 2, 3, 5 can be chosen in 4, 3, 2 ways: 24 divisors.

Method: Square root

Divisors that are squares correspond to divisors of √(largest square divisor) = 2^3·3^2·5^1: 24 of them.

Problem p15. Find gcd(2^30 − 1, 2^42 − 1).

Hintgcd(2^a − 1, 2^b − 1) = 2^gcd(a,b) − 1.
Answer63
Method: Exponent gcd

gcd(30, 42) = 6 so the answer is 2^6 − 1 = 63.

Method: Euclid

Reduce 2^30 − 1 modulo 2^42 − 1 as in the Euclidean algorithm on exponents; this ends at 2^6 − 1 = 63.

Source: Technique from Khurmi ch. 3, 6.2 and the IMO Compendium §2.4 (original problem)

Problem p18. How many zeros does 100! end with?

HintCount factors of 5 using Legendre's formula.
Answer24
Method: Legendre

⌊100/5⌋ + ⌊100/25⌋ + ⌊100/125⌋ + … = 24.

Method: Count fives

Count multiples of 5, then extra factors from multiples of 25, 125, …: 24.

Problem p22. What is the exponent of 2 in C(2000, 1000)?

HintKummer: it is the number of carries adding 1000 + 1000 in binary.
Answer6
Method: Carries

Adding 1000 to itself in binary produces 6 carries, the number of 1's in 1000.

Method: Legendre

v₂((2n)!) − 2v₂(n!) = 6.

Problem p25. What is the smallest positive integer n for which n! is divisible by 10^12?

HintYou need at least 12 factors of 5 (there are always enough 2's).
Answer50
Method: Count fives

⌊n/5⌋ + ⌊n/25⌋ ≥ 12 first holds at n = 50.

Method: Check neighbours

49! has 10 + 1 = 11 factors of 5 and 50! has 12.
