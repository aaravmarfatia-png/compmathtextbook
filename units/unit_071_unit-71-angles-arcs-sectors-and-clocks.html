1. Degrees, radians, arcs and sectors

A full turn is 360° or 2π radians, so 180° = π radians and 1 radian ≈ 57.3°. For a circle of radius r and an angle θ in radians, the arc length is rθ and the sector area is ½r²θ. A sector of angle 60° = π/3 in a circle of radius 6 has arc length 6 × π/3 = 2π and area ½ × 36 × π/3 = 6π, one sixth of the circle's area 36π ✓.

Worked example. A circular pizza of diameter 12 is cut into 8 equal slices. Each slice has area (π × 36)/8 = 4.5π ≈ 14.14 and arc length 12π/8 = 1.5π ≈ 4.71.

Angles in a circle. The inscribed angle is half the central angle on the same arc. A 40° arc subtends 20° at the circumference.

2. Clock problems

The minute hand turns 360° in 60 minutes, so 6° per minute. The hour hand turns 360° in 12 hours, so 30° per hour or 0.5° per minute. At h hours and m minutes, the hour hand is at 30h + 0.5m degrees and the minute hand at 6m degrees from 12.

Worked example 1. The angle at 3:20. Hour hand: 90 + 10 = 100°. Minute hand: 120°. The angle between them is 20°.

Worked example 2. When do the hands first overlap after 12:00? The minute hand gains 6 − 0.5 = 5.5° per minute on the hour hand, and must gain 360°, so 360/5.5 = 65 5/11 minutes: 1:05:27. The hands overlap 11 times every 12 hours.

Worked example 3. At what time between 4 and 5 are the hands at a right angle? At 4:m the hour hand is at 120 + 0.5m and the minute at 6m. The difference is 120 − 5.5m = ±90, so m = 30/5.5 = 60/11 ≈ 5.45 minutes (4:05:27) or m = 210/5.5 = 420/11 ≈ 38.18 minutes (4:38:11).

3. Arcs, chords and sector problems

The chord of a sector of angle θ in a circle of radius r has length 2r sin(θ/2). For θ = 60° it equals r (the triangle is equilateral). The segment cut off by the chord has area ½r²(θ − sin θ). For r = 6 and θ = π/3: ½ × 36 × (1.0472 − 0.8660) = 3.262.

Rolling circles. A wheel of radius 1 rolls without slipping along a flat road; after one turn its centre moves 2π. A circle of radius 1 rolling around the outside of a circle of radius 3 makes 4 turns by the time it returns to its start (the circumference ratio is 3 plus the extra one turn from the rotation around the centre).

Worked example. A cone is made from a sector of radius 10 and angle 216°. The arc length is 10 × (216π/180) = 12π, which becomes the circumference of the base, so the base radius is 6 and the height is √(100 − 36) = 8.

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. Using degrees in the formulas rθ and ½r²θ. Forgetting that the hour hand moves between the hour marks. Using the wrong angle (reflex versus acute) in clock problems.

Checks. Check that a full circle gives the whole circumference or area.

Practice. Convert to radians first for arc formulas.

Worked examples with Try-its: Arcs, sectors and segments

In a circle of radius r, an angle of θ radians at the centre cuts an arc of length rθ and a sector of area ½r²θ. With r = 6 and θ = 60° = π/3, the arc is 2π and the sector is 6π. The segment (the sector minus its triangle) has area ½r²(θ − sin θ) = 18(π/3 − √3/2) = 6π − 9√3. An inscribed angle is half the central angle on the same arc, so an arc of 80° is seen from the circle at 40°.

Degrees and radians. 180° = π, so multiply degrees by π/180. Keep answers in terms of π unless asked otherwise.

Try it

A sector has radius 10 and arc length 5π. What is its angle in degrees and its area?

Answer. θ = 5π/10 = π/2 = 90°; area = ½ · 100 · π/2 = 25π.

Worked examples with Try-its: Clock problems

Think of the hands as runners. The minute hand moves 6° per minute and the hour hand 0.5° per minute, so the minute hand gains 5.5° per minute. At h:m the angle between them is |30h − 5.5m|, and if it exceeds 180° you subtract it from 360°. At 3:40: |90 − 220| = 130°. At 2:30: |60 − 165| = 105°.

Overlaps. The hands meet when the minute hand has gained a full multiple of 360° on the hour hand: every 360/5.5 = 720/11 minutes, so 11 times in 12 hours, at times 12 · k/11 hours. The hands are at right angles 22 times in 12 hours and opposite 11 times.

Try it

What is the smaller angle between the hands at 7:20?

Answer. |210 − 110| = 100°.

Worked examples with Try-its: A longer problem

Problem. Between 4:00 and 5:00, at what times do the hands form a right angle?

Solution. The angle is |120 − 5.5m|. Setting this equal to 90 gives 5.5m = 30 or 5.5m = 210, so m = 60/11 ≈ 5.45 or m = 420/11 ≈ 38.18. Both lie between 0 and 60, so the times are about 4:05:27 and 4:38:11.

Reflection. Always solve both signs of an absolute value, then check that each answer lies in the allowed window.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Clock angle. At 8:15 the hour hand is at 8 · 30 + 15 · 0.5 = 247.5° and the minute hand at 90°, so the angle is 157.5° (the formula |30h − 5.5m| gives |240 − 82.5| = 157.5). Sector. A sector of radius 9 and angle 40° = 2π/9 has arc length 9 · 2π/9 = 2π and area ½ · 81 · 2π/9 = 9π.

Try it

Answer. |150 − 275| = 125°.

Try it

What fraction of a circle's area is a sector of angle 72°?

Answer. 72/360 = 1/5.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. At what time between 2:00 and 3:00 do the hands overlap? Solution. Setting |60 − 5.5m| = 0 gives m = 60/5.5 = 120/11 ≈ 10.91 minutes, i.e. about 2:10:55.

Check. At that moment the minute hand is at 6 · 120/11 ≈ 65.45° and the hour hand at 60 + 0.5 · 120/11 ≈ 65.45° ✓.

Problem p1. What is the angle between the hands at 3:40 (in degrees, less than 180)?

HintHour hand: 30h + 0.5m. Minute hand: 6m.
Answer130
Method: Positions

Hour hand 3·30 + 40·0.5 = 110°. Minute hand 40·6 = 240°. The difference is 130°.

Method: Check

The minute hand points at the 8 (240°). The hour hand is two thirds of the way from the 3 to the 4, which is 90° + 20° = 110°.
