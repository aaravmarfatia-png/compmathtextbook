Reading

1. Points as complex numbers

Place the plane's point (x, y) at z = x + yi. Then the distance between points is |z₁ − z₂|, the midpoint is (z₁ + z₂)/2 and the centroid of a triangle is (z₁ + z₂ + z₃)/3. Three points z₁, z₂, z₃ are collinear exactly when (z₁ − z₂)/(z₃ − z₂) is real: for 0, 1 + i, 3 + 3i the ratio is 1/3. The area of the triangle with vertices 0, a, b is ½|Im(ā b)|; for 0, 3, 4i it is ½ · 12 = 6.

Rotation and similarity. Multiplying by e^{iθ} rotates about the origin by θ; multiplying by a real k scales. Rotating z about the point c by 90° gives c + i(z − c). Rotating 3 + 4i by 90° about 1 + i: (2 + 3i)i + 1 + i = −3 + 2i + 1 + i = −2 + 3i.

Equilateral triangles. The vertices 0, 1, w form an equilateral triangle when w = e^{iπ/3} = (1 + i√3)/2 (or its conjugate). In general, z₁, z₂, z₃ form an equilateral triangle iff z₁² + z₂² + z₃² = z₁z₂ + z₂z₃ + z₃z₁.

Try it

Rotate the point 5 by 60° about the point 2 and find the result.

Answer. 2 + 3 e^{iπ/3} = 2 + 3(1/2 + i√3/2) = 3.5 + (3√3/2)i.

2. Lines, circles and angles

The unit circle is |z| = 1, and for such z, z̄ = 1/z. A circle with centre c and radius r is |z − c| = r. The angle between two segments from a point is the argument of a quotient: ∠ABC is arg((c − b)/(a − b)). Four points are concyclic iff the cross-ratio (z₁ − z₃)(z₂ − z₄)/((z₁ − z₄)(z₂ − z₃)) is real.

Example (square on a side). If AB is a side, the other two vertices of the square are B + i(B − A)·(±1) and A + i(B − A)·(±1). For A = 0, B = 2, we get the vertices 2i and 2 + 2i on one side.

Try it

Find the circumcentre of the triangle with vertices 0, 4, 4i in complex form.

Answer. The right angle is at 0, so the circumcentre is the midpoint of the hypotenuse from 4 to 4i: 2 + 2i.

3. A longer problem

Problem. Triangle ABC has A = 0, B = 6, C = 3 + 3√3 i. Show it is equilateral and find the centroid and the area.

Solution. |AB| = 6; |AC| = √(9 + 27) = 6; |BC| = |−3 + 3√3 i| = √(9 + 27) = 6. Centroid: (9 + 3√3 i)/3 = 3 + √3 i. Area: ½|Im(b̄c)| = ½ · 18√3 = 9√3, which agrees with the formula (√3/4) · 36.

Reflection. Complex numbers made the equilateral check a matter of three modulus computations.

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. (1) Rotating about the origin instead of the correct centre (subtract, rotate, add). (2) Using the wrong conjugate in the area formula. (3) Mixing degrees and radians.

Checks. Rotations preserve distances.

How to practise. Translate each geometry problem into 'move the centre to 0'. In Unlimited practice, rotate the complex-geometry families.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Distance and area. For the triangle with vertices 0, 4 and 3i the sides are 4, 3 and |3i − 4| = 5, and the area is ½ · 4 · 3 = 6. Rotation. Rotating z about 1 by 90° gives 1 + i(z − 1); the point 3 + 4i goes to 1 + i(2 + 4i) = −3 + 2i. Collinearity. Three points z₁, z₂, z₃ are collinear exactly when (z₃ − z₁)/(z₂ − z₁) is real. Equilateral test. Points a, b, c form an equilateral triangle exactly when a + ωb + ω²c = 0 or a + ω²b + ωc = 0, where ω = e^{2πi/3}.

Try it

Are 1 + i, 2 + 2i and 4 + 4i collinear?

Answer. (4 + 4i − 1 − i)/(2 + 2i − 1 − i) = (3 + 3i)/(1 + i) = 3, real, so yes.

Try it

Find the midpoint of 3 + 2i and −1 + 6i.

Answer. 1 + 4i.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. Show that 0, 1 and e^{iπ/3} form an equilateral triangle. Solution. With a = 0, b = 1, c = e^{iπ/3}: the sides are |b − a| = 1, |c − a| = 1 and |c − b| = |e^{iπ/3} − 1| = |−1/2 + i√3/2| = 1.

Reflection. Rotating the segment from 0 to 1 by 60° about 0 produces the third vertex.

Problem p6. The three cube roots of 8 form a triangle in the plane. Find its area as k√3.

HintThey lie on a circle of radius 2.
Answer3√3
Method: Side length

They are 2, 2e^(2πi/3), 2e^(4πi/3), equally spaced on a circle of radius 2, so the triangle is equilateral with side 2√3 and area (√3/4)·12 = 3√3.
