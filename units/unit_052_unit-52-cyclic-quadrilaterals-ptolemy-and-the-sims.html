1. Cyclic quadrilaterals and Ptolemy's theorem

A quadrilateral is cyclic if its vertices lie on a circle; then its opposite angles sum to 180°. Ptolemy's theorem: in a cyclic quadrilateral ABCD, AC · BD = AB · CD + BC · DA — the product of the diagonals equals the sum of the products of opposite sides. For a square of side 1: √2 · √2 = 1 · 1 + 1 · 1. For a 3 × 4 rectangle: 5 · 5 = 3 · 3 + 4 · 4.

The regular pentagon. Let the side be s and the diagonal d. The quadrilateral formed by four consecutive vertices is an isosceles trapezoid with bases s and d and legs s; Ptolemy gives d² = s² + sd, so with φ = d/s, φ² = φ + 1 and φ = (1 + √5)/2.

Equilateral triangle. If P lies on the arc BC of the circumcircle of equilateral triangle ABC, then PA = PB + PC. Ptolemy applied to ABPC gives PA · BC = PB · AC + PC · AB, and all three sides are equal. A numerical test with a point at 200° confirms the sum relation: of the three distances the largest equals the sum of the other two.

Try it

A cyclic quadrilateral has consecutive sides 2, 3, 2, 3 (so it is a rectangle). Find its diagonal.

Answer. Ptolemy: d · d = 2 · 2 + 3 · 3 = 13, so d = √13.

2. The Simson line

Take a point P on the circumcircle of triangle ABC and drop perpendiculars from P to the three side lines. The three feet are collinear; the line through them is the Simson line of P. A numerical experiment with an equilateral triangle and P at 200° confirmed that the area of the triangle formed by the three feet is zero. If P is not on the circumcircle the feet form a triangle (the pedal triangle).

Why it works. Let D, E, F be the feet on BC, CA, AB. The quadrilaterals PFBD and PDCE have right angles at F, D and D, E, so they are cyclic, and angle chasing with the cyclic quadrilateral PABC shows that angles PDF and PDE together make a straight angle, so F, D, E are collinear. The full proof is repeated in Unit 105.

Try it

What is the Simson line of a vertex of the triangle?

Answer. The feet of the perpendiculars from a vertex are the vertex itself and the feet of the two altitudes; the Simson line is the altitude through that vertex.

3. Proving four points concyclic

Standard criteria: opposite angles sum to 180°; an exterior angle equals the opposite interior angle; two equal angles subtend the same segment on the same side; the power-of-a-point converse PA · PB = PC · PD; Ptolemy's equality (AC · BD = AB · CD + BC · DA implies cyclic).

Example. In acute triangle ABC the feet of the altitudes from B and C, say E and F, lie on the circle with diameter BC (right angles BEC and BFC), so B, C, E, F are concyclic. This is the starting point for the orthic triangle (Unit 93).

Try it

Why are the feet of the altitudes from B and C on the circle with diameter BC?

Answer. Because ∠BEC = ∠BFC = 90°, and an angle subtending a diameter is a right angle (Thales).

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. (1) Applying Ptolemy to a quadrilateral that is not cyclic (it then gives only an inequality: AC · BD ≤ AB · CD + BC · DA). (2) Matching the wrong sides as 'opposite'. (3) Misreading which angles are on the same side of a chord.

Checks. Verify with a rectangle or square.

How to practise. Mark equal angles in two colours. In Unlimited practice, rotate the cyclic quadrilateral families.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Brahmagupta's formula. A cyclic quadrilateral with sides a, b, c, d and semiperimeter s has area √((s − a)(s − b)(s − c)(s − d)). For sides 1, 2, 3, 4 we have s = 5 and the area is √(4 · 3 · 2 · 1) = √24 = 2√6. Ptolemy. In a regular pentagon with side s and diagonal d, the cyclic quadrilateral formed by four vertices has diagonals d, d and sides s, s, s, d, so d · d = s · s + s · d, that is d² = s² + sd, giving d/s = (1 + √5)/2.

Try it

A cyclic quadrilateral has sides 5, 5, 5, 5. Find its area.

Answer. It is a square of side 5, area 25; the formula gives √(5 · 5 · 5 · 5) = 25.

Try it

In a cyclic quadrilateral ABCD, AB = 3, BC = 4, CD = 5, DA = 6. Find AC · BD.

Answer. Both diagonals: AC · BD = AB · CD + BC · DA = 15 + 24 = 39.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. Points A, B, C, D lie on a circle with AB = 6, BC = 8 and the diagonal AC a diameter of length 10. Find the area of triangle ABC and the radius. Solution. Since AC is a diameter, angle B is 90° and 6² + 8² = 10² confirms it. The area is ½ · 6 · 8 = 24 and the radius is 5.

Reflection. A diameter forces a right angle: the converse of Thales is a useful cyclic-quadrilateral test.

Problem p1. A quadrilateral with sides AB = 2, BC = 3, CD = 4, DA = 5 is inscribed in a circle. What is the product of its diagonals AC·BD?

HintPtolemy.
Answer23
Method: Ptolemy

AC·BD = AB·CD + BC·AD = 2·4 + 3·5 = 23.

Method: Check on a rectangle

For a rectangle with sides 3 and 4 the same rule gives 3·3 + 4·4 = 25 = 5·5, the product of the two diagonals.

HintPtolemy on ABPC.
Answer6
Method: Ptolemy

With side s, PA·s = PB·s + PC·s, so PA = PB + PC = 6.

Method: Coordinates

Take the circle of radius 1, A at angle 90°, B at 210°, C at 330°; for any P on the arc BC numerically PA = PB + PC.

Problem p3. A regular pentagon has side 1 and diagonal d. Use Ptolemy to find d, to three decimals.

Hintd² = d + 1.
Answer1.618
Method: Ptolemy

Take four consecutive vertices A, B, C, D. The sides AB = BC = CD = 1 and DA is a diagonal d, and both diagonals AC and BD equal d. Ptolemy: AC·BD = AB·CD + BC·DA, so d² = 1 + d. Then d = (1 + √5)/2 ≈ 1.618.

Method: Numbers

(1 + 2.236)/2 = 1.618.

Source: Coxeter & Greitzer, Geometry Revisited, ch. 2

HintDistance formula.
Answer5

√(16 + 9) = 5.

Problem p6. AB is a diameter of length 10 and C is on the circle with AC = 6. Find BC.

HintAngle C is 90°.
Answer8
Method: Thales

Angle ACB = 90°, so BC = √(100 − 36) = 8.

Method: Coordinates

Put A = (−5, 0), B = (5, 0); C = (−5 + 36/10, 4.8) = (−1.4, 4.8) and BC = √(6.4² + 4.8²) = 8.

Problem p7. Three circles of radius 1 are mutually tangent. A fourth circle is tangent to all three and lies in the gap between them. Find the radius to three decimals.

HintDescartes' circle theorem: k₄ = k₁ + k₂ + k₃ + 2√(k₁k₂ + k₁k₃ + k₂k₃).
Answer0.155
Method: Descartes

With curvatures 1, 1, 1: k₄ = 3 + 2√3 ≈ 6.464, so the radius is 1/6.464 ≈ 0.155.

Method: Geometry

The centres of the unit circles form an equilateral triangle of side 2 whose centroid is 2/√3 from each vertex. The inner circle has radius 2/√3 − 1 ≈ 0.155.

Source: Coxeter & Greitzer, Geometry Revisited, ch. 2

Problem p8. Two chords AB and CD of a circle meet at P inside it with AP = 4, PB = 9 and CP = 6. Find PD.

HintAP·PB = CP·PD.
Answer6
Method: Intersecting chords

4·9 = 6·PD gives PD = 6.

Method: Similar triangles

Triangles APC and DPB are similar, so AP/DP = CP/BP: 4/PD = 6/9 and PD = 6.
