Reading

1. Inversion

Inversion in a circle with centre O and radius r sends a point P ≠ O to the point P′ on ray OP with OP · OP′ = r². Points inside go outside and vice versa; points on the circle stay fixed; applying inversion twice returns the original point.

Worked example 1. With r = 2, a point at distance 4 from O goes to distance 4/4 = 1; a point at distance ½ goes to distance 8.

What it does to lines and circles. A line through O is sent to itself. A line not through O is sent to a circle through O (and back). A circle through O is sent to a line not through O. A circle not through O is sent to another circle not through O.

Worked example 2. With r = 2, the circle through O with diameter 8 (the far end of the diameter at distance 8) is sent to the line at distance r²/8 = 4/8 = ½ from O, perpendicular to that diameter.

2. Using inversion

Inversion preserves the angle between curves, so it keeps tangency. Choose a centre at the point where many circles touch; they become lines, and the figure often turns into something simple (parallel lines, a strip).

Worked example. Two circles tangent at O invert to two parallel lines (both tangent circles pass through O and have a common tangent at O); a third circle tangent to both becomes a circle between the lines, with diameter equal to the gap. This is how the figure of circles in a tangent chain is analysed.

Distances. The inverse points satisfy P′Q′ = r² · PQ/(OP · OQ).

3. Cross ratio and polars

The cross ratio of four collinear points A, B, C, D is (A, B; C, D) = (AC · BD)/(AD · BC), using signed lengths. It is unchanged by projections and inversions.

Worked example. For the points 0, 1, 2, 3 on a number line: (0, 1; 2, 3) = ((0 − 2)(1 − 3))/((0 − 3)(1 − 2)) = ((−2)(−2))/((−3)(−1)) = 4/3. If the cross ratio is −1 the four points are harmonic. Example: A = 0, B = 4, C = 1, D = −2 gives ((0 − 1)(4 + 2))/((0 + 2)(4 − 1)) = −6/6 = −1.

The polar. For a circle x² + y² = r² the polar of the point (x₀, y₀) is the line x x₀ + y y₀ = r². For the pole (4, 0) and r = 2 the polar is the line 4x = 4, i.e. x = 1, which is at distance r²/4 = 1 from the centre ✓, as inversion predicts: the polar passes through the inverse point of the pole.

La Hire. A lies on the polar of B iff B lies on the polar of A.

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. Inverting in a circle centred at the wrong point. Forgetting that the image of a circle through O is a line. Dropping signs in the cross ratio.

Checks. Take two points, compute their inverses, check OP · OP′ = r².

Practice. Choose O at a tangency point and draw the inverted picture.

Worked examples with Try-its: Inversion and polars

Inversion in a circle of centre O and radius r sends P to the point P′ on ray OP with OP · OP′ = r². With r = 6, a point at distance 2 goes to distance 18. Circles through O become lines not through O, and lines not through O become circles through O. The polar of a point (x₀, y₀) with respect to x² + y² = r² is the line x₀x + y₀y = r²: for (5, 0) and r = 3 the polar is x = 9/5.

Try it

With r = 4 where does the point at distance 8 from O go?

Answer. OP′ = 16/8 = 2.

Worked examples with Try-its: Tangents and cross ratio

If P is outside the circle, the polar of P passes through the two points of tangency. For P = (5, 0) and r = 3 these points are (9/5, ±12/5): they lie on the circle (81 + 144)/25 = 9, and the tangent length is √(25 − 9) = 4. Inversion preserves the cross ratio of four points on a circle or line, which links it to the harmonic ideas of earlier units.

Try it

Find the polar of (4, 0) with respect to x² + y² = 4.

Answer. 4x = 4, so x = 1.

Worked examples with Try-its: A longer problem

Problem. Under inversion in the unit circle, find the image of the line x = 2. Solution. A line not through O maps to a circle through O; the foot of the perpendicular (2, 0) maps to (1/2, 0), so the circle has diameter from (0, 0) to (1/2, 0): centre (1/4, 0), radius 1/4.

Reflection. Find the image of the closest point to O; it is the far end of the circle's diameter through O.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Inversion with radius 5. The point 3 + 4i lies on the circle |z| = 5, so it is fixed by inversion in that circle; the point 10 goes to 25/10 = 5/2. Polar. The polar of (3, 4) with respect to x² + y² = 25 is 3x + 4y = 25, which passes through the point (3, 4) itself because the point lies on the circle (the polar of a point on the circle is its tangent).

Try it

Invert the point at distance 2 from O in a circle of radius 4.

Answer. Distance 16/2 = 8 on the same ray.

Try it

Find the polar of (0, 5) with respect to x² + y² = 4.

Answer. 5y = 4, so y = 4/5.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. The tangent lengths from P = (5, 0) to the circle x² + y² = 9 are 4. Show that the chord of contact is x = 9/5. Solution. The chord of contact is the polar of P: 5x = 9, so x = 9/5. The contact points have y² = 9 − 81/25 = 144/25, so y = ±12/5, and the distance from P to (9/5, 12/5) is √((16/5)² + (12/5)²) = 4 ✓.

Reflection. The polar of an outside point is its chord of contact.

Problem p1. Inversion in a circle of radius 6 centred at O sends a point P with OP = 4 to P′. Find OP′.

HintP′ lies on ray OP with OP · OP′ = r².
Answer9
Method: Definition

OP · OP′ = 6² = 36, so OP′ = 36/4 = 9.

Method: Similar triangles

For a point Q on the circle both P and P′ are related by triangles OPQ and OQP′ being similar, since OQ² = OP · OP′; this gives OP′ = 9.

Problem p5. Under inversion in a circle of radius 6 centred at O, a line at distance 4 from O maps to a circle through O. What is the diameter of that circle?

HintThe foot of the perpendicular from O maps to the far end of the diameter.
Answer9
Method: Foot of the perpendicular

The foot F is at distance 4 from O and maps to F′ with OF′ = 6²/4 = 9. Since the image circle passes through O and F′ with its diameter along OF, the diameter is 9.

Method: Reverse

Inverting back, the circle with diameter D through O becomes a line at distance r²/D; setting r²/D = 4 gives D = 9.

Source: Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 5-6

Problem p10. Inversion in a circle of radius 6 centred at O sends a circle of radius 2 whose centre is 10 from O (not through O) to a circle. Find the radius of the image circle.

HintInvert the two points of the circle on the line through O.
Answer3/4
Method: Two points

The nearest and farthest points of the circle are at 10 − 2 = 8 and 10 + 2 = 12 from O. They map to 9/2 and 3 on the same line, so the image circle has diameter 9/2 − 3 and radius 3/4.

Method: Formula

The image radius is r²·a/|d² − a²| = 36 · 2/|100 − 4| = 3/4.

Problem p13. Four points on a line have coordinates A = 0, B = 2, C = 3, D = 7. The cross ratio (A, B; C, D) is (CA/CB) ÷ (DA/DB), using signed lengths. Find its value.

HintUse signed differences.
Answer15/7
Method: Direct

CA = -3, CB = -1, DA = -7, DB = -5, so the cross ratio is (-3/-1) ÷ (-7/-5) = 15/7.

Method: Rearranged

The same ratio is (CA · DB)/(CB · DA) = (-3 · -5)/(-1 · -7) = 15/7.

Source: Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 5-6

Problem p16. Points A = 0, B = 6 and C = 2 lie on a line. Find the coordinate of the harmonic conjugate D of C with respect to A and B, i.e. the point with CA/CB = −DA/DB.

HintPut the equation into one unknown.
Answer-6
Method: Solve

(2 − 0)/(2 − 6) = −(D − 0)/(D − 6) gives 2(D − 6) = −-4(D − 0), so D = -6.

Method: Check

DA/DB = (-6)/(-12) = 1/2, which is the negative of CA/CB = -1/2.

Source: Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 5-6
