Andreescu & Gelca, Mathematical Olympiad Challenges, sections 1.1-1.5 (geometry), with the lattice-point idea from Pick's theorem.

Reading

1. Pick's theorem

A lattice polygon has its vertices at points with integer coordinates. Pick's theorem: if the polygon has I lattice points strictly inside and B on its boundary, its area is A = I + B/2 − 1. For the triangle (0, 0), (4, 0), (0, 3): B = 4 + 3 + 1 = 8 (the hypotenuse from (4, 0) to (0, 3) has no lattice points strictly between its ends), I = 3, and A = 3 + 4 − 1 = 6 ✓. For the 3 × 3 square: I = 4, B = 12, A = 4 + 6 − 1 = 9 ✓. The smallest lattice triangle has I = 0, B = 3 and area 1/2.

Counting boundary points. The number of lattice points on a segment from (x₁, y₁) to (x₂, y₂), counting both ends, is gcd(|Δx|, |Δy|) + 1; for (0, 0) to (12, 18) that is 7. So for a polygon, B is the sum of gcd(|Δx|, |Δy|) over the sides.

Using it both ways. Compute the area by the shoelace formula, count B by gcds, then deduce I = A − B/2 + 1. For the pentagon (0, 0), (6, 0), (6, 4), (3, 6), (0, 4) the shoelace area is 30, B = 6 + 4 + 1 + 1 + 4 = 16 and so I = 30 − 8 + 1 = 23, and a direct count of interior points agrees (the program verified the identity on several polygons).

Try it

A lattice triangle has 5 boundary points and area 6. How many interior lattice points?

Answer. I = A − B/2 + 1 = 6 − 2.5 + 1 = 4.5, which is not an integer, so no such triangle exists: Pick's theorem forces 2A − B to be even, i.e. 2A and B must have the same parity.

2. Applications

Why no equilateral lattice triangle exists. The area of an equilateral triangle with squared side n is n√3/4, irrational, but Pick's theorem makes every lattice-polygon area a multiple of 1/2: contradiction. Counting. A rectangle a × b has B = 2(a + b) and I = (a − 1)(b − 1). A right triangle with legs a and b has area ab/2 and B = a + b + gcd(a, b), so I = (ab − a − b − gcd(a, b))/2 + 1.

Strategy. For a polygon on a grid, make a table of the vertices, compute the area by shoelace, compute B by gcds and use Pick to find I (or the reverse). It is much faster than counting by hand.

Try it

Find I for the right triangle with legs 6 and 9.

Answer. A = 27, B = 6 + 9 + gcd(6, 9) = 18, I = 27 − 9 + 1 = 19.

3. A longer problem

Problem. The triangle with vertices (0, 0), (5, 2), (2, 7) is a lattice triangle. Find its area, the number of boundary points, and the number of interior points.

Solution. Area = ½|5 · 7 − 2 · 2| = 31/2. Boundary: gcd(5, 2) = 1, gcd(3, 5) = 1, gcd(2, 7) = 1, so B = 3. Pick gives I = 31/2 − 3/2 + 1 = 15. A direct count of the lattice points inside the triangle also gives 15.

Reflection. Three independent routes (shoelace, gcds, brute force) agree. The brute-force count is the safest check for a first example.

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. (1) Forgetting that boundary points include the vertices. (2) Using gcd(Δx, Δy) without adding 1 for the number of points on a single segment (but not for the polygon's B, where the endpoints are shared). (3) Applying Pick's theorem to non-lattice polygons. (4) Forgetting the −1.

Checks. A unit square must give I = 0, B = 4, A = 1.

How to practise. Draw the polygon on grid paper first. In Unlimited practice, rotate the Pick and lattice-point families.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Rectangle. The 4 × 3 rectangle has area 12 and 14 boundary lattice points, so Pick's theorem A = I + B/2 − 1 gives I = 12 − 7 + 1 = 6 interior points (the 3 × 2 grid inside). Triangle. The triangle (0, 0), (6, 0), (0, 4) has area 12 and boundary points 6 + 4 + gcd(6, 4) = 12 (the hypotenuse contributes gcd(6, 4) = 2 steps), so I = 12 − 6 + 1 = 7.

Try it

A lattice triangle has 5 interior points and 7 boundary points. What is its area?

Answer. 5 + 7/2 − 1 = 7.5.

Try it

How many lattice points lie on the segment from (0, 0) to (12, 18) including both endpoints?

Answer. gcd(12, 18) + 1 = 7.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. Find the area of the lattice quadrilateral (0, 0), (5, 1), (6, 4), (1, 3), and check with Pick's theorem. Solution. The shoelace formula gives half of |0·1 − 5·0 + 5·4 − 6·1 + 6·3 − 1·4 + 1·0 − 0·3| = |0 + 14 + 14 + 0|/2 = 14. The sides have gcd values 1, 1, 1, 1, so B = 4 and Pick gives I = 14 − 2 + 1 = 13.

Reflection. Pick's theorem is a fast check on a shoelace computation.

Problem p1. In an equilateral triangle of side 6, a point inside is joined by perpendiculars to the three sides. What is the sum of the three perpendicular lengths? Write as k√3.

HintViviani's theorem.
Answer3√3
Method: Areas

Split the triangle into three triangles with the point as common vertex: (1/2)·6·(d₁ + d₂ + d₃) = area = 9√3, so d₁ + d₂ + d₃ = 3√3, the altitude.

Method: Special point

At the centre each distance is the inradius √3, and 3·√3 = 3√3.

HintArea = I + B/2 − 1.
Answer15
Method: Pick

Area = 24, boundary B = 2(6 + 4) = 20, so 24 = I + 10 − 1 and I = 15.

The interior points have x = 1…5 and y = 1…3: 5·3 = 15.

Problem p3. A triangle has vertices (0,0), (6,0) and (0,6). How many lattice points are on its boundary?

HintSum of gcds.
Answer18
Method: gcd rule

The sides have lattice lengths gcd(6, 0) = 6, gcd(0, 6) = 6 and gcd(6, 6) = 6, so B = 18.

Method: Count

Each leg has 7 points including endpoints and the hypotenuse x + y = 6 has 7; the vertices are shared by two sides: 7 + 7 + 7 − 3 = 18.

Problem p5. A lattice triangle has vertices (0,0), (5,2), (1,4). Find its area.

HintHalf the determinant.
Answer9
Method: Determinant

|5·4 − 2·1|/2 = 18/2 = 9.

Method: Pick cross-check

Boundary points B = gcd(5,2) + gcd(4,2) + gcd(1,4) = 1 + 2 + 1 = 4. With area 9, Pick gives 9 = I + 2 − 1, so I = 8 interior points, consistent with a lattice triangle.

Problem p11. Find the number of interior lattice points of the triangle with vertices (0, 0), (6, 0) and (0, 4).

HintCount boundary points with gcd.
Answer7
Method: Pick

A = 12 and B = 6 + 4 + gcd(6, 4) = 12, so I = 12 − 6 + 1 = 7.

Method: Count by rows

Inside means x > 0, y > 0 and 2x + 3y < 12. Row y = 1 has x = 1 to 4 (4 points), row y = 2 has x = 1, 2 (2 points), row y = 3 has x = 1 (1 point). Total 7.

Problem p15. Find the number of interior lattice points of the triangle (0, 0), (4, 1), (1, 3).

HintArea by the shoelace formula, then Pick.
Answer5
Method: Pick

A = |0·1 + 4·3 + 1·0 − (0·4 + 1·1 + 3·0)|/2 = 11/2. Each side has gcd 1, so B = 3 and I = 11/2 − 3/2 + 1 = 5.

Method: Listing

The interior points are (1, 1), (2, 1), (3, 1), (1, 2) and (2, 2): five points.

Problem p17. An equilateral triangle has altitude 12. Find the radius of its inscribed circle.

HintThe incentre has equal distances to all sides.
Answer4
Method: Viviani

The three equal distances r sum to the altitude: 3r = 12, so r = 4.

Method: Centroid

The incentre is the centroid, which is 1/3 of the way up the altitude: 12/3 = 4.

Problem x19. Find the area of the triangle with vertices (0, 0), (8, 0) and (3, 5).

HintShoelace formula.
Answer20
