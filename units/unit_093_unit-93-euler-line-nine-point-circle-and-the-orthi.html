Book: Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 1 and 5; Chen ch. 1 (25 problems)
Coxeter & Greitzer, Geometry Revisited, chapter 1 (the Euler line and nine-point circle) and Chen, Euclidean Geometry in Mathematical Olympiads, chapter 1, checked in coordinates on the 13-14-15 triangle with B = (0,0), C = (14,0), A = (5,12).

Reading

1. The orthic triangle

The feet of the three altitudes of a triangle form the orthic triangle. In an acute triangle ABC, the orthic triangle has angles 180° − 2A, 180° − 2B, 180° − 2C, and the orthocentre H is the incentre of the orthic triangle. So the altitudes are the angle bisectors of the orthic triangle. In an obtuse triangle H lies outside and becomes an excentre.

Why. The quadrilateral with vertices B, C and the feet of the altitudes from B and C is cyclic (it has a diameter BC with right angles at the feet). Angle chasing in these cyclic quadrilaterals gives the angle at each foot.

Worked example. A triangle with angles 50°, 60°, 70° has an orthic triangle with angles 180 − 100 = 80°, 180 − 120 = 60°, 180 − 140 = 40°, which sum to 180° ✓.

2. Distances to the orthocentre

Let M be the midpoint of BC, R the circumradius. Then the distance from the circumcentre O to BC is OM = R cos A, and the distance from vertex A to the orthocentre H is AH = 2·OM = 2R cos A. Also, the reflections of H over the sides and over the midpoints of the sides lie on the circumcircle.

Worked example (13-14-15). The angle A opposite the side 14 has cos A = 33/65 and R = 65/8. Then OM = (65/8)(33/65) = 33/8, which also equals √(R² − (7)²) = √(4,225/64 − 49) = √(1,089/64) = 33/8 ✓. And AH = 2 × 33/8 = 33/4 = 8.25.

Euler line. O, G, H are collinear with OG : GH = 1 : 2 (Unit 27). So OH = 3 OG, and OH² = 9R² − (a² + b² + c²).

3. The nine-point circle

The midpoints of the sides, the feet of the altitudes and the midpoints of AH, BH, CH lie on one circle, with centre N the midpoint of OH and radius R/2. It is the circumcircle of the orthic triangle and of the medial triangle.

Why. The medial triangle is the image of ABC under the homothety centred at G with ratio −1/2, so its circumcircle has radius R/2 and its centre is the image of O. That centre N satisfies N = G + (−1/2)(O − G), the midpoint of OH.

Worked example. A triangle has circumradius 10. The nine-point circle has radius 5. Its area is 25π.

Feuerbach's theorem. The nine-point circle is tangent to the incircle and the three excircles. For the 13-14-15 triangle the radii are R/2 = 65/16 = 4.0625 and r = 4, so the distance between the nine-point centre and the incentre is R/2 − r = 1/16: the circles are tangent internally.

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. Assuming the orthic triangle formulas for an obtuse triangle. Mixing up AH and OM (AH = 2 OM). Forgetting that N is the midpoint of OH, not of OG.

Checks. Test with a right triangle: H is the right-angle vertex.

Practice. Build a coordinate example and verify each claim.

Worked examples with Try-its: The Euler line

In any triangle the orthocentre H, centroid G and circumcentre O are collinear with HG = 2 · GO. In the 13-14-15 triangle with B = (0, 0), C = (14, 0), A = (5, 12) we have O = (7, 33/8), G = (19/3, 4) and H = (5, 15/4), and H − G = 2(G − O) exactly. The nine-point centre N is the midpoint of OH, here (6, 63/16).

Try it

If O = (0, 0) and H = (6, 3), where is G?

Answer. G = (2, 1), one third of the way from O to H.

Worked examples with Try-its: The nine-point circle

One circle passes through the three midpoints of the sides, the three feet of the altitudes and the three midpoints of AH, BH, CH. Its radius is R/2, which is 65/16 for the 13-14-15 triangle. Check: the foot of the altitude from A is (5, 0), and its squared distance from N = (6, 63/16) is 1 + (63/16)² = (65/16)². The triangle formed by the three feet is the orthic triangle.

Try it

A triangle has circumradius 10. What is the radius of its nine-point circle?

Answer. 5.

Worked examples with Try-its: A longer problem

Problem. In the 13-14-15 triangle, find the distance between the orthocentre and the circumcentre. Solution. OH = √((7 − 5)² + (33/8 − 15/4)²) = √(4 + 9/64) = √265/8. Euler's formula OH² = 9R² − (a² + b² + c²) gives 9 · 4225/64 − 590 = (38025 − 37760)/64 = 265/64 ✓.

Reflection. Two independent routes agreeing is a strong check.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Right triangle. In the 3-4-5 triangle with A = (0, 0), B = (4, 0), C = (0, 3) the orthocentre is the right-angle vertex H = (0, 0), the circumcentre is the midpoint of the hypotenuse O = (2, 3/2), and the centroid is G = (4/3, 1). Then H − G = (−4/3, −1) and G − O = (−2/3, −1/2), so HG = 2 · GO ✓. The nine-point circle has radius R/2 = 5/4 and centre at the midpoint of OH, (1, 3/4).

Try it

In a right triangle the circumradius is 6.5. What is the hypotenuse?

Answer. 13.

Try it

Triangle with H = (0, 0) and O = (3, 6): where is G?

Answer. (1, 2).

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. The orthocentre of a triangle with circumcentre O = (0, 0) and vertices A = (5, 0), B = (0, 5), C = (−3, 4), all on a circle of radius 5. Find H. Solution. For a circumcentre at the origin, H = A + B + C = (2, 9).

Check. The altitude from A is perpendicular to BC: BC = (−3, −1), AH = (−3, 9), dot product 9 − 9 = 0 ✓.

Problem p1. Triangle with B = (0,0), C = (14,0), A = (5,12). The orthocentre is H = (x, y). Find x + y as a fraction.

HintThe altitude from A is the line x = 5; use the altitude from B.
Answer35/4
Method: Altitudes

The altitude from A is x = 5. The altitude from B is perpendicular to AC (direction (9, −12)), so it is 9x − 12y = 0. At x = 5, y = 15/4, so H = (5, 15/4) and x + y = 35/4.

Method: Euler line

With O = (7, 33/8), H = A + B + C − 2O = (19 − 14, 12 − 33/4) = (5, 15/4).

Source: Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 1 and 5; Chen ch. 1

Problem p2. For the same triangle, the circumcentre is (7, y). Find y as a fraction.

HintEqual distances to B and A.
Answer33/8
Method: Equal distances

On the perpendicular bisector of BC, x = 7, and 49 + y² = 4 + (y − 12)². So 49 = 148 − 24y, giving y = 99/24 = 33/8.

Method: Check

R² = 49 + (33/8)² = 4225/64, so R = 65/8, matching abc/(4K).

Source: Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 1 and 5; Chen ch. 1

Problem p3. Find OG²/GH² for this triangle as a fraction.

HintG divides OH in the ratio 1 : 2.
Answer1/4
Method: Euler line

OG : GH = 1 : 2, so the squares have ratio 1 : 4, and OG²/GH² = 1/4.

Method: Coordinates

O = (7, 33/8), G = (19/3, 4), H = (5, 15/4). Each coordinate step from O to G is half the step from G to H, so the squared lengths are in ratio 1/4.

Source: Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 1 and 5; Chen ch. 1
