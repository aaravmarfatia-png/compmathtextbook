1. Ratios and proportions

A ratio compares two quantities of the same kind: 3 : 5. To split 150 in the ratio 3 : 5 : 7, divide by the total number of parts, 15, and multiply: 30, 50, 70. A proportion says two ratios are equal: 4/x = 6/9 gives 6x = 36, x = 6 (cross-multiplication). Chaining ratios: if a : b = 2 : 3 and b : c = 4 : 5, rewrite so that b matches (multiply the first by 4, the second by 3) to get a : b : c = 8 : 12 : 15.

Direct and inverse variation. If y is proportional to x, then y/x is constant. If y is inversely proportional to x, the product xy is constant: 6 workers finish a job in 10 days, so 4 workers (same rate) need 6 · 10/4 = 15 days. Map scales are ratios: on a 1 : 50 000 map, 4 cm represents 4 · 50 000 cm = 2 km.

Scaling in geometry. If lengths scale by k, areas scale by k² and volumes by k³. Figures in the length ratio 2 : 3 have areas in the ratio 4 : 9 and volumes in the ratio 8 : 27.

Try it

A recipe for 4 people uses 300 g of flour. How much for 10 people?

Answer. 300 · 10/4 = 750 g.

2. Percent

A percent is a ratio out of 100: 35% of 80 is 0.35 · 80 = 28, and 45 out of 60 is 75%. Percent change = (new − old)/old. Successive changes multiply. A price that rises 20% and then falls 20% ends at 1.2 · 0.8 = 0.96 of the original, a net loss of 4%. Two discounts of 20% and 10% together give 0.8 · 0.9 = 0.72, i.e. 28% off, not 30%. To undo a change divide: a price of 100 after a 20% decrease was 100/0.8 = 125 before.

Profit. Cost 80, sale price 100: profit 20, which is 25% of the cost but 20% of the sale price. Always say which base is used. Interest. Compound interest at 10% for 2 years multiplies by 1.1² = 1.21 (1000 becomes 1210), while simple interest gives only 1200.

Averages and mixtures. A weighted average weights each group by its size: 20 students averaging 70 and 30 averaging 80 give (1400 + 2400)/50 = 76. To mix 30% and 60% solutions into 40%, the amounts are in the ratio (60 − 40) : (40 − 30) = 2 : 1.

Try it

A shirt costs $40 after a 20% discount. What was the original price?

Answer. 40/0.8 = $50.

3. A longer problem

Problem. A store marks up goods by 40% over cost, then offers a 25% discount on the marked price. What is the profit percent on cost? If the cost was $60, what is the selling price?

Solution. Marked price = 1.4 × cost; selling price = 0.75 × 1.4 = 1.05 × cost: a 5% profit. With cost 60 the selling price is $63.

Reflection. Multiplying factors (1.4 and 0.75) is the cleanest approach to chained percentage changes; adding percentages (40 − 25 = 15) is the classic mistake.

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. (1) Adding successive percentages. (2) Using the wrong base. (3) Mixing up direct and inverse proportion. (4) Forgetting to square or cube a scale factor for area and volume.

Checks. Estimate: does the answer have the right size? Substitute back.

How to practise. Convert every percent to a multiplier. In Unlimited practice, rotate the ratio and percent families.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Percent of. 15% of 80 is 12. Percent change. From 40 to 52 is a 30% increase (12/40). Up then down. A 20% increase followed by a 20% decrease multiplies by 1.2 · 0.8 = 0.96, a 4% net decrease. Successive discounts of 20% and 10% multiply by 0.8 · 0.9 = 0.72, a 28% total discount, not 30%.

Ratios. Splitting 64 in the ratio 3 : 5 gives 24 and 40. If a : b = 2 : 3 and b : c = 4 : 5, scale b to 12: a : b : c = 8 : 12 : 15.

Try it

A price rises 25% and then falls 20%. What is the net change?

Answer. 1.25 · 0.8 = 1, no change.

Try it

If a : b = 3 : 4 and b : c = 2 : 7, find a : b : c.

Answer. b : c = 2 : 7 = 4 : 14, so a : b : c = 3 : 4 : 14.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. A shirt is on sale for 20% off and costs 48 dollars after the discount. What was the original price, and what price after a further 10% off the sale price? Solution. Original = 48/0.8 = 60. A further 10% off gives 48 · 0.9 = 43.2.

Reflection. Divide to undo a percentage; do not add the percentage back.

Problem p1. If 5 workers build a wall in 12 days, how many days do 8 workers need?

HintWorkers × days is constant.
Answer15/2
Method: Inverse proportion

5·12 = 60 worker-days, and 60/8 = 7.5 days.

Method: Rates

One worker does 1/60 of the wall per day, 8 do 8/60 = 2/15 per day, so 15/2 days.

Problem p7. If x : y = 3 : 4 and y : z = 6 : 5, find x : z as a fraction x/z.

HintMake the y parts equal.
Answer9/10
Method: Common y

x : y = 9 : 12 and y : z = 12 : 10 (scaling 6 : 5 by 2), so x : z = 9 : 10.

Method: Multiply

x/z = (x/y)(y/z) = (3/4)(6/5) = 18/20 = 9/10.
