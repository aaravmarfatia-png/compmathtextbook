1. Loci

A locus is the set of all points satisfying a geometric condition. Standard loci: points equidistant from two points form the perpendicular bisector; points equidistant from two intersecting lines form the two angle bisectors; points at distance d from a point form a circle; points at distance d from a line form two parallel lines; points P with ∠APB = 90° form a circle with diameter AB (minus A and B).

Worked example 1. The locus of points P with PA² + PB² = k for fixed A and B is a circle centred at the midpoint M of AB, since PA² + PB² = 2PM² + AB²/2 (Apollonius). So PM² = (k − AB²/2)/2. For A = (−1, 0), B = (1, 0), k = 10: PM² = (10 − 2)/2 = 4, a circle of radius 2.

Worked example 2. In a circle of radius 5 consider all chords of length 8. Each is at distance √(25 − 16) = 3 from the centre, so the midpoints of these chords form the circle of radius 3 about the centre.

2. Constructions with compass and straightedge

With a straightedge and a compass you can: bisect a segment and construct its perpendicular bisector; bisect an angle; construct a perpendicular to a line at a point; draw a parallel; construct a regular triangle, square, hexagon, and pentagon; divide a segment into n equal parts (by similar triangles).

Constructible numbers. Starting from 1 you can build all numbers obtained by +, −, ×, ÷ and square roots. Hence you can construct √2, (1 + √5)/2 and the length of a regular pentagon's diagonal, but not ∛2 (doubling the cube), the trisection of a general angle, or a square with the same area as a circle (π is not algebraic).

Worked example. Construct a segment of length √5: draw a right triangle with legs 1 and 2; the hypotenuse is √5. To construct √ab, draw a semicircle on a segment of length a + b and the perpendicular at the joining point.

3. A potpourri of problems

The tallest pole. A ladder of length 13 leans against a wall with its foot 5 away: it reaches 12. If the foot is pulled out by 1 (to 6), the top drops to √(169 − 36) = √133 ≈ 11.53.

The coin rotation puzzle. A coin rolls around an identical coin without slipping: it makes 2 full turns for one trip around (not 1).

Folding and cutting. A square piece of paper of side 8 is folded so that a corner touches the midpoint of the opposite side: the crease length can be found by similar triangles.

Heron and trapezoids. The area of a trapezoid with parallel sides a, b and legs c, d can be found by sliding one leg so that a triangle with sides (a − b), c, d appears: Heron gives its height.

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. Forgetting that a locus can have two parts. Including excluded points (like A and B for the right-angle circle). Trying to trisect an angle with a straightedge and compass.

Checks. Test a locus with two or three specific points.

Practice. Describe the locus in words before using coordinates.

Worked examples with Try-its: Loci

A locus is the set of points satisfying a condition. Equidistant from two points: their perpendicular bisector. At fixed distance r from a point: a circle. At fixed distance from a line: two parallel lines. Equidistant from two lines: the angle bisectors. Apollonius. For A = (0, 0) and B = (3, 0), the points P with PA = 2·PB satisfy x² + y² = 4((x − 3)² + y²), which simplifies to (x − 4)² + y² = 4, a circle centred (4, 0) with radius 2 (it meets the x-axis at 2 and 6, the harmonic pair).

Try it

Describe the locus of points P with PA + PB = 10 for fixed points A, B that are 6 apart.

Answer. An ellipse with foci A, B and semi-major axis 5.

Worked examples with Try-its: Constructions

With straightedge and compass you can bisect angles and segments, construct perpendiculars and parallels, build 60° angles and hence 30°, 15°, and construct lengths √n. A regular n-gon is constructible for n = 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 15, 16, 17 but not for 7, 9, 11 or 13. Trisecting a general angle and doubling the cube are impossible.

Try it

Which of these regular polygons can be constructed: 7-gon, 10-gon, 9-gon, 12-gon?

Answer. 10-gon and 12-gon.

Worked examples with Try-its: A longer problem

Problem. Show that there are 5 consecutive composite numbers. Solution. 6! + 2, …, 6! + 6 = 722, 723, 724, 725, 726 are divisible by 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 respectively (722 = 2 · 361, 723 = 3 · 241, 724 = 4 · 181, 725 = 5 · 145, 726 = 6 · 121).

Reflection. The same idea gives arbitrarily long gaps between primes.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Midpoints of chords through a fixed point. For the unit circle and P = (1, 0), the chord from P to (cos θ, sin θ) has midpoint ((1 + cos θ)/2, (sin θ)/2), which lies on the circle with centre (1/2, 0) and radius 1/2: the locus is the circle on diameter OP. Constructibility. A regular 15-gon can be constructed (3 · 5 with distinct Fermat primes) but a regular 7-gon cannot.

Try it

What is the locus of points equidistant from two parallel lines 6 apart?

Answer. The parallel line midway between them, 3 from each.

Try it

What is the locus of points at distance 2 from a given circle of radius 5 (same centre side)?

Answer. Two concentric circles of radii 3 and 7.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. Find the locus of points P such that PA² + PB² = 10 where A = (−1, 0) and B = (1, 0). Solution. (x + 1)² + y² + (x − 1)² + y² = 2x² + 2y² + 2 = 10, so x² + y² = 4: a circle of radius 2 about the midpoint of AB.

Check. At (2, 0): 9 + 1 = 10 ✓.

Problem p1. Points are 5 from (0,0) and 5 from (6,0). How far apart are the two such points?

HintThey lie on the line x = 3.
Answer8
Method: Algebra

x = 3 and y² = 25 − 9, so the points are (3, ±4), which are 8 apart.

Method: Geometry

The two points and the centres form a rhombus with diagonals 6 and 8.

Problem p2. The locus of points equidistant from (0,0) and (4,2) is a line. Where does it cross the y-axis (give y)?

HintSquare the distances.
Answer5
Method: Algebra

x² + y² = (x − 4)² + (y − 2)² gives 8x + 4y = 20, so at x = 0, y = 5.

Method: Bisector

It passes through the midpoint (2, 1) with slope −2, so at x = 0 it is at y = 1 + 4 = 5.

Problem p3. The points P with PA = 2·PB, where A = (0,0) and B = (3,0), form a circle. What is its radius?

HintSquare both sides and complete the square.
Answer2
Method: Algebra

x² + y² = 4((x − 3)² + y²) gives x² − 8x + y² + 12 = 0, so (x − 4)² + y² = 4 and the radius is 2.

Method: Check

The points (2, 0) and (6, 0) on the x-axis satisfy PA = 2PB.

Problem p4. How many circles are tangent to all three lines of a triangle (the incircle and excircles)?

HintOne inside, one opposite each vertex.
Answer4
Method: Count

The incircle plus three excircles: 4.

Method: Reason

The centres are the intersections of angle bisectors: one internal triple point and three points where one internal and two external bisectors meet.

Problem p5. A ladder of length 10 slides with its ends on perpendicular walls. What is the radius of the circle traced by its midpoint?

HintThe midpoint of a hypotenuse is equidistant from the three vertices.
Answer5
Method: Thales

The midpoint is always 5 from the corner, so the locus is a circle (quarter) of radius 5.

Method: Coordinates

The ends are (a, 0) and (0, b) with a² + b² = 100, so the midpoint (a/2, b/2) has distance √(a² + b²)/2 = 5 from the origin.

Problem p12. A and B are 7 apart. How many points are at distance 5 from A and distance 3 from B?

Answer2

Triangles with sides 5, 3, 7 exist, and the third vertex has two mirror positions.

Problem p15. A and B are 6 apart. The locus of P with PA = 2 PB is a circle. What is its radius?

HintApollonius: radius = k · AB/|k² − 1|.
Answer4
Method: Formula

2 · 6/3 = 4.

Method: Diameter

The circle's diameter has endpoints at the internal division point (distance 2 from B) and the external one (distance 6 from B), so the diameter is 8 and the radius is 4.

At the top of the ellipse the distance to each focus is a = 5, giving a 3-4-5 triangle.
