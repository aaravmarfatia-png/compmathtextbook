Reading

1. Basic angle facts

Angles on a straight line add to 180°. Vertical angles are equal. For parallel lines cut by a transversal, corresponding angles are equal and alternate angles are equal; co-interior angles add to 180°. The angles of a triangle add to 180°; an exterior angle equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles. In an isosceles triangle the base angles are equal.

Worked example 1. In triangle ABC with AB = AC and angle A = 40°, the base angles are (180 − 40)/2 = 70° each.

Worked example 2. In a triangle with angles A, B, C and the angle bisectors from B and C meeting at the incentre I, angle BIC = 180° − B/2 − C/2 = 90° + A/2. For A = 60° this is 120°.

Worked example 3. The orthocentre H of an acute triangle satisfies angle BHC = 180° − A. For A = 50°: 130°.

2. Angles in circles

The inscribed angle is half the central angle. Angles in the same segment are equal. An angle in a semicircle is a right angle. In a cyclic quadrilateral opposite angles add to 180°. The tangent–chord angle equals the inscribed angle on the opposite side.

Worked example 1. In a circle, chord AB subtends an angle of 35° at a point C on the major arc. The angle subtended at the centre is 70°. A point D on the minor arc sees AB at 180° − 35° = 145°.

Worked example 2. A cyclic quadrilateral ABCD has angle BAD = 80° and the diagonal AC makes angle BAC = 30°. Then angle CAD = 50°, and angle BCD = 180° − 80° = 100°. Since angles on the same arc are equal, angle BDC = angle BAC = 30°. In triangle BCD the third angle is 180° − 100° − 30° = 50° = angle CBD, which equals angle CAD (both subtend arc CD) ✓.

3. A method for angle chasing

Name every angle. Give unknown angles letters (x, y), write down every relation (straight line, triangle sum, cyclic, isosceles), and solve the small linear system.

Worked example. In triangle ABC, AB = AC and D is a point on BC with AD = BD, and angle DAC = 21°. Let angle B = x. Then angle C = x (isosceles) and angle BAD = x (since AD = BD, triangle ABD is isosceles with apex D). Angle BAC = 180° − 2x, and BAC = BAD + DAC = x + 21°. So 180 − 2x = x + 21, 3x = 159, x = 53°. The triangle has angles 74°, 53°, 53°. Check: BAD = 53, DAC = 21, total 74 ✓.

Auxiliary lines. Extend a line, join two points, draw a parallel, or add a circle through three points, to create new angle relations.

Adding the circumcircle. If you suspect four points are concyclic, prove it (by angles) and then use the inscribed angles you have gained.

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. Reading an angle off an unreliable diagram. Using the wrong arc for an inscribed angle. Mixing up interior and exterior angles. Contradictory numbers in a problem (always check the figure is consistent).

Checks. Check that every triangle's angles add to 180° and the whole figure is consistent.

Practice. Mark equal angles with the same symbol.

Worked examples with Try-its: Basic tools

Angles in a triangle sum to 180°; an exterior angle equals the sum of the two remote interior angles. In an isosceles triangle with apex 40° the base angles are 70°. An inscribed angle is half the intercepted arc, and angles in the same segment are equal. The angle between a tangent and a chord equals the inscribed angle on the chord. Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°.

Try it

An isosceles triangle has a base angle of 50°. What is its apex angle?

Answer. 80°.

Worked examples with Try-its: Centres

If I is the incentre of a triangle with angle A = 50°, then ∠BIC = 90° + A/2 = 115° (the other two angles are 65° after halving). For the orthocentre H, ∠BHC = 180° − A = 130°. The sum of the angles at the points of a five-pointed star (pentagram) is 180°.

Try it

Triangle with A = 80°: find ∠BIC where I is the incentre.

Answer. 130°.

Worked examples with Try-its: A longer problem

Problem. In triangle ABC, AB = AC and ∠A = 20°. Point D is on AC so that BD bisects angle B. Find ∠BDC. Solution. Base angles are 80°, so ∠ABD = ∠DBC = 40°. In triangle BDC, ∠BDC = 180° − 40° − 80° = 60°.

Reflection. Label every angle you can find, even those not asked for.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Isosceles with apex 20°. In triangle ABC with AB = AC and A = 20°, the base angles are 80°. Let D be on AC with AD = BD. Then ∠ABD = ∠A = 20°, so ∠DBC = 80° − 20° = 60°, and ∠BDC = 20° + 20° = 40° (exterior angle of triangle ABD). The angles of triangle DBC are 60°, 80°, 40°, summing to 180°. Cyclic quadrilateral. If ∠A = 70° then the opposite angle ∠C = 110°.

Try it

The angle between a tangent and a chord is 35°. What is the inscribed angle on the other side of the chord?

Answer. 35°, the tangent-chord angle equals the inscribed angle on the intercepted arc.

Try it

In a triangle, one exterior angle is 110° and one remote interior angle is 40°. Find the other remote angle.

Answer. 70°.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. Triangle ABC has A = 40° and B = 60°. The angle bisectors of A and B meet at I. Find ∠AIB. Solution. ∠IAB = 20° and ∠IBA = 30°, so ∠AIB = 130°. This agrees with the formula 90° + C/2 where C = 80°.

Check. 90° + 40° = 130° ✓.

Problem p1. A triangle has angles 50° and 60°. What is the third angle in degrees?

HintThey sum to 180°.
Answer70
Method: Sum

180 − 110 = 70.

Method: Check

50 + 60 + 70 = 180.

Source: AoPS Volume 1, ch. 10 and 14 (Angles and Angle Chasing)

Problem p2. The two remote interior angles of a triangle are 35° and 45°. How large is the exterior angle at the third vertex?

HintExterior angle theorem.
Answer80
Method: Theorem

35 + 45 = 80.

Method: Straight line

The third interior angle is 100°, so the exterior angle is 180 − 100 = 80.

Source: AoPS Volume 1, ch. 10 and 14 (Angles and Angle Chasing)

Problem p3. Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal and one angle is 65°. A co-interior (same-side interior) angle is what, in degrees?

HintThey are supplementary.
Answer115
Method: Rule

180 − 65 = 115.

Method: Via alternate angles

The alternate angle is 65° and its neighbour on a straight line is 115°.

Problem p4. An isosceles triangle has an apex angle of 40°. How large is each base angle?

HintBase angles are equal.
Answer70

(180 − 40)/2 = 70.

Method: Equation

40 + 2x = 180 gives x = 70.

Problem p5. A tangent and a chord meet at a point on a circle and cut off an arc of 100°. What is the angle between them, in degrees?

HintHalf the arc.
Answer50
Method: Rule

50.

Method: Inscribed angle

It equals the inscribed angle on the same arc, which is half of 100° = 50°.

The angle is an exterior angle of a triangle whose remote angles are inscribed angles of 35° and 25°, giving 60°.

Problem p9. In a regular pentagon ABCDE find angle BAC in degrees.

Answer36
Method: Isosceles

Angle BAC is an inscribed angle on one side of the pentagon, whose arc is 72°, so it is 36°.
