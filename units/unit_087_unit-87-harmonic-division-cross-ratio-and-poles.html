1. Cross ratio and harmonic division

For four collinear points A, B, C, D, the cross ratio is (A, B; C, D) = (AC/BC) ÷ (AD/BD), with signed lengths. Projections preserve it. When the cross ratio is −1, we say C and D divide AB harmonically: C is inside the segment AB and D is outside, with AC/CB = AD/DB.

Worked example. With A = 0 and B = 3 on a number line, the harmonic conjugate of C = 1 is D with AC/CB = 1/2, so AD/DB = 1/2 with D outside: D = −3 (AD = 3, DB = 6). Check cross ratio with signed lengths: AC = 1, BC = −2, AD = 3, BD = 6: (1/(−2)) ÷ (3/6) = (−1/2) ÷ (1/2) = −1 ✓.

Harmonic mean connection. If C and D divide AB harmonically, the distance AB relates to AC and AD by 2/AB = 1/AC + 1/AD with signed distances from A. In the example above AB = 3, AC = 1 and AD = −3, and 1/1 + 1/(−3) = 2/3 = 2/3 ✓.

2. Apollonius circles and harmonic pencils

The locus of points P whose distances to two fixed points A and B are in a constant ratio k ≠ 1, PA/PB = k, is a circle (the Apollonius circle). Its diameter is the segment between the two points dividing AB internally and externally in the ratio k, and those two points are harmonic conjugates with respect to A and B.

Worked example. A = (0, 0), B = (6, 0), k = 2 (PA = 2PB). Points on the line: internal division x with x/(6 − x) = 2, x = 4; external, x/(x − 6) = 2, x = 12. The circle has diameter from (4, 0) to (12, 0): centre (8, 0), radius 4. Check the point (8, 4): PA = √(64 + 16) = √80, PB = √(4 + 16) = √20 and √80/√20 = 2 ✓.

Angle bisectors. The internal and external bisectors of the angle at P meet line AB at the points dividing AB harmonically, which is why they lie on the Apollonius circle.

3. Poles and polars

For a circle with centre O and radius r, the polar of a point P (not O) is the line perpendicular to OP at the point P′ with OP · OP′ = r² (the inverse of P). In coordinates, for the circle x² + y² = r², the polar of (x₀, y₀) is x x₀ + y y₀ = r². If P is outside the circle, its polar is the line through the two points of tangency of the tangents from P.

Worked example. For x² + y² = 4 and P = (4, 0): the polar is 4x = 4, i.e. x = 1. The tangent points have x = 1 and y² = 3: (1, ±√3). Check: the tangent from (4, 0) to (1, √3) has direction (−3, √3), and the radius to (1, √3) is (1, √3): dot product −3 + 3 = 0 ✓.

La Hire's theorem. A point Q lies on the polar of P iff P lies on the polar of Q. Also, the polar of a point is the set of harmonic conjugates: for a line through P meeting the circle at A and B, the point Q on that line with (A, B; P, Q) = −1 lies on the polar of P.

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. Using unsigned lengths in the cross ratio. Confusing inversion with polarity (the polar is a line; the inverse is a point). Taking the polar line through the wrong point.

Checks. Test the harmonic relation numerically.

Practice. Put the points on a number line and use coordinates.

Worked examples with Try-its: Harmonic division

Four collinear points A, B, C, D have cross ratio (A, B; C, D) = (AC · BD)/(BC · AD) using signed lengths. The pair C, D divides AB harmonically when this equals −1, which means C divides AB internally and D externally in the same ratio. Example: A = 0, B = 6, C = 2, D = −6; then AC/CB = 2/4 and AD/DB = 6/12 (taken externally), and the cross ratio is (2 · (−12))/((−4)(−6)) = −1.

Try it

Find D so that A = 0, B = 8, C = 6 and D are harmonic (C inside AB).

Answer. AC/CB = 6/2 = 3; D outside with AD/DB = 3: D = 12 (AD = 12, DB = 4).

Worked examples with Try-its: The angle bisector connection

In triangle ABC the feet of the internal and external bisectors from A cut BC harmonically. With AB = 3, AC = 6 and BC = 9 (placing B = 0, C = 9), the internal foot is at 3 and the external foot at −9, and (B, C; 3, −9) = −1. This explains why the Apollonius circle (all points whose distances to B and C are in a fixed ratio) has the harmonic pair as a diameter.

Try it

Triangle with AB = 4, AC = 12, BC = 8: where does the internal bisector from A meet BC (distance from B)?

Answer. BD = 8 · 4/16 = 2.

Worked examples with Try-its: A longer problem

Problem. Show that if C and D divide AB harmonically and M is the midpoint of AB, then MC · MD = MA². Check with numbers. A = 0, B = 6, C = 2, D = −6, M = 3: MC = 1, MD = 9, product 9 = 3². ✓

Reflection. The proof sets M as the origin and uses the coordinates a, −a, c, d with cd = a², the standard way to prove harmonic facts.

More worked examples: Worked examples

With A = 0, B = 4 and C = 1, the harmonic conjugate D satisfies AD/DB = AC/CB = 1/3 with D outside the segment, so D = −2 (AD = 2 and DB = 6). The cross ratio (A, B; C, D) = ((1 − 0)(−2 − 4))/((1 − 4)(−2 − 0)) = −6/6 = −1. The polar of the point (2, 0) with respect to the unit circle is x = 1/2.

Try it

Find the harmonic conjugate of the midpoint of AB with respect to A and B.

Answer. The point at infinity on line AB.

Try it

A = 0, B = 6, C = 4: find D with (A, B; C, D) = −1.

Answer. AC/CB = 4/2 = 2, so D = 12.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. Show that if (A, B; C, D) = −1 then 2/AB = 1/AC + 1/AD in signed lengths from A. Check with numbers. A = 0, B = 6, C = 4, D = 12: 2/6 = 1/3 and 1/4 + 1/12 = 1/3 ✓.

Reflection. The distance AB is the harmonic mean of AC and AD.

Problem p1. Points A, B, C, D lie at 0, 6, 2 and d on a line, and (A, B; C, D) = −1. Find d.

HintUse the cross ratio formula.
Answer-6
Method: Formula

(2)(d − 6)/((−4)(d)) = −1 gives 2d − 12 = 4d, so d = −6.

Method: Ratios

The internal point C divides AB in ratio AC:CB = 2:4 = 1:2, so the harmonic conjugate D divides AB externally in the same ratio: DA:DB = 1:2, which puts D at −6, 6 units from A.

Problem p7. Triangle ABC has AB = 6, AC = 3 and BC = 7. The internal bisector from A meets BC at D and the external bisector meets line BC at E beyond C. Find CE.

HintEB/EC = AB/AC = 2.
Answer7
Method: Ratio

EB/EC = 2 and EB = EC + 7, so EC = 7.

Method: Harmonic

(B, C; D, E) is harmonic. D has BD = 14/3, DC = 7/3, and E satisfies EB/EC = 2, which gives EC = 7.

Problem p14. If (A, B; C, D) = 2, find (A, B; D, C).

HintSwapping C and D inverts the cross ratio.
Answer1/2
Method: Rule

(A, B; D, C) = 1/(A, B; C, D) = 1/2.

Method: Example

With 0, 2, 3, 6 the swap gives (AD · BC)/(AC · BD) = 6 · 1/(3 · 4) = 1/2.
