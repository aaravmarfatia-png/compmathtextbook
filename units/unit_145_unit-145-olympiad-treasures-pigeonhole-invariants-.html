1. Pigeonhole and extremal counting

Every integer is 2ˢ times an odd number, so {1, …, 10} splits into 5 chains by odd part (1,2,4,8; 3,6; 5,10; 7; 9). Any 6 numbers put two in the same chain, so one divides the other, and the numbers 6 to 10 show 5 is not enough: the answer for N = 10 is 6. n lines in general position make 1 + n + C(n,2) regions: 4 lines give 11; n planes make 1 + n + C(n,2) + C(n,3).

Try it

Regions made by 3 lines in general position?

Answer. 7.

Try it

Smallest k forcing divisibility in {1,…,8}?

Answer. 5.

2. Invariants and games

A move that keeps some quantity fixed pins down the end state. Replacing a, b by a + b − 1 lowers the sum by 1 each time, so from 1..4 the final number is 10 − 3 = 7. Replacing by ab + a + b keeps ∏(x + 1), so from 1,2,3 we end at 4! − 1 = 23. Nim. With piles 3, 4, 5 the XOR is 2, and the only winning move is 3 → 1. Josephus. Eliminating every second person from n = 2ᵐ + L leaves 2L + 1.

Try it

Final number from 1,2,3,4,5 using a + b − 1?

Answer. 15 − 4 = 11.

Try it

Josephus survivor for n = 6?

Answer. L = 2, survivor 5.

3. A worked problem

Problem. Find the survivor for n = 41. Solution. 41 = 32 + 9, so the survivor is 2 · 9 + 1 = 19.

Problem p1. What is the smallest k such that every k-element subset of {1, …, 10} contains two numbers, one dividing the other?

HintWrite each number as 2^s times an odd number and use pigeonhole.
Answer6
Method: Pigeonhole

The 10 numbers fall into 5 chains by their odd part; 6 numbers force two in the same chain, so k = 6.

Method: Antichain

The 5 numbers from 6 to 10 have no divisibility among them, so k > 5; the chain bound gives k = 6.

Source: Technique from Olympiad Treasures ch. 3 and Aigner & Ziegler ch. 10, 25 (original problem)

Problem p5. 5 lines in the plane, no two parallel and no three through a point, divide the plane into how many regions?

HintAdding the kth line adds k new regions.
Answer16
Method: Recurrence

R_n = R_(n−1) + n with R₀ = 1: R_5 = 16.

Method: Formula

1 + n + C(n,2) = 16.

Source: Technique from Olympiad Treasures ch. 3 and Aigner & Ziegler ch. 10, 25 (original problem)

Problem p8. 4 planes in space in general position divide it into how many regions?

HintCount by dimension: 1 + n + C(n,2) + C(n,3).
Answer15
Method: Formula

C(4,0) + C(4,1) + C(4,2) + C(4,3) = 15.

Method: Recurrence

The kth plane is cut by the earlier ones into 1 + (k − 1) + C(k − 1, 2) pieces, each splitting a region; summing gives 15.

Source: Technique from Olympiad Treasures ch. 3 and Aigner & Ziegler ch. 10, 25 (original problem)

Problem p11. In Nim with piles 3, 4, 5, how many different first moves leave the opponent in a losing position?

HintA position is losing exactly when the XOR of the pile sizes is 0.
Answer1
Method: XOR

The XOR of the piles is 2; a move to XOR 0 changes a pile p to p ⊕ 2 and is legal when that is smaller: 1 such piles.

Method: Exhaustive search

Check every move from the position and test whether the result is a losing position: 1 moves work.

Source: Technique from Olympiad Treasures ch. 3 and Aigner & Ziegler ch. 10, 25 (original problem)

Problem p15. The numbers 1, 2, …, 10 are written on a board. A move replaces two numbers a, b by a + b − 1. What number is left after 9 moves?

HintFind a quantity that never changes.
Answer46
Method: Invariant

The sum drops by 1 each move, so the final number is 55 − 9 = 46.

Method: Small case

Try n = 3: 1,2,3 → 2,3 → 4 = 6 − 2. The same pattern gives 46.

Source: Technique from Olympiad Treasures ch. 3 and Aigner & Ziegler ch. 10, 25 (original problem)

Problem p18. The numbers 1, 2, …, 4 are on a board. A move replaces two numbers a, b by ab + a + b. What number is left at the end?

HintConsider the product of (number + 1).
Answer119
Method: Invariant

(ab + a + b) + 1 = (a + 1)(b + 1), so the product of (x + 1) is unchanged: the final number is 5! − 1 = 119.

Method: Small case

For 1, 2, 3: (1,2) → 5; then (5,3) → 23 = 4! − 1; the pattern gives 119 for n = 4.

Source: Technique from Olympiad Treasures ch. 3 and Aigner & Ziegler ch. 10, 25 (original problem)

Problem p21. 7 people stand in a circle numbered 1 to 7. Starting at 1, every second person (2, 4, …) is removed, going round the circle. Who survives?

HintWrite n = 2^m + L; the survivor is 2L + 1.
Answer7
Method: Josephus formula

7 = 4 + 3, so the survivor is 2·3 + 1 = 7.

Method: Simulation

Remove 2, 4, 6, … round after round; the last person left is number 7.

Source: Technique from Olympiad Treasures ch. 3 and Aigner & Ziegler ch. 10, 25 (original problem)
