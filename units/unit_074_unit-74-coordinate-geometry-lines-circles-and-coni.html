1. Parabolas

A parabola is the set of points equally far from a fixed point (the focus) and a fixed line (the directrix). The parabola y² = 4ax has focus (a, 0) and directrix x = −a. The parabola y = x²/4 (that is, x² = 4y) has focus (0, 1) and directrix y = −1. Check with the point (2, 1) on it: its distance to the focus (0, 1) is 2 and to the line y = −1 is also 2 ✓.

Tangents. The tangent to y = x² at x = 1 has slope 2 (the derivative) and passes through (1, 1): y = 2x − 1. A line y = mx + c meets y = x² in one point exactly when x² − mx − c = 0 has a double root: m² + 4c = 0.

2. Ellipses

An ellipse is the set of points whose distances to two foci add up to a constant 2a. In standard form x²/a² + y²/b² = 1 (a > b) with foci (±c, 0), c² = a² − b². For x²/25 + y²/9 = 1: a = 5, b = 3, c = 4, foci (±4, 0). A point such as (0, 3) has distance 5 to each focus, sum 10 = 2a ✓. The area is πab = 15π.

Worked example. The ellipse x²/25 + y²/9 = 1 meets the line y = 3x/5 at x²/25 + (9x²/25)/9 = 1, so 2x²/25 = 1, x = ±5/√2, giving the two intersection points.

Eccentricity. e = c/a = 4/5 measures how elongated the ellipse is; a circle has e = 0.

3. Hyperbolas and the general picture

A hyperbola is the set of points whose distances to two foci differ by a constant 2a. In standard form x²/a² − y²/b² = 1, with foci (±c, 0), c² = a² + b², and asymptotes y = ±(b/a)x. For x²/9 − y²/16 = 1: a = 3, b = 4, c = 5, asymptotes y = ±4x/3. The point (5, 16/3) lies on it, and its distances to the foci (5, 0) and (−5, 0) are 16/3 and √(100 + 256/9) = 34/3; the difference is 6 = 2a ✓.

The rectangular hyperbola xy = 1 has asymptotes the axes. The line x + y = k meets it where x(k − x) = 1, x² − kx + 1 = 0: two points if |k| > 2, one if |k| = 2 (tangent) and none if |k| < 2.

General conics. Every second-degree equation Ax² + Bxy + Cy² + Dx + Ey + F = 0 is a conic (ellipse, parabola, hyperbola or degenerate). The discriminant B² − 4AC tells which: negative for an ellipse, zero for a parabola, positive for a hyperbola.

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. Using c² = a² + b² for an ellipse (the correct relation is c² = a² − b²). Mixing a and b. Forgetting that a hyperbola has two branches.

Checks. Test a point on the curve: the focal distances should add (ellipse) or differ (hyperbola) by 2a.

Practice. Sketch with the axes and asymptotes first.

Worked examples with Try-its: Lines and distances

The distance between (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂) is √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²). The distance from (x₀, y₀) to the line ax + by + c = 0 is |ax₀ + by₀ + c|/√(a² + b²); for (1, 2) and 3x + 4y − 10 = 0 it is |3 + 8 − 10|/5 = 1/5. Perpendicular lines have slopes whose product is −1. The midpoint is the average of the coordinates.

Triangles in coordinates. A right triangle with legs 6 and 8 along the axes has hypotenuse 10 and inradius (6 + 8 − 10)/2 = 2, so its incentre is (2, 2).

Try it

Distance from the origin to the line x + y = 4?

Answer. |−4|/√2 = 2√2.

Worked examples with Try-its: Circles

The circle with centre (h, k) and radius r is (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r². Completing the square turns x² + y² − 6x + 4y − 12 = 0 into (x − 3)² + (y + 2)² = 25, centre (3, −2), radius 5. A line is tangent when its distance to the centre equals r: y = x + k touches x² + y² = 2 when |k|/√2 = √2, so k = ±2.

Chords. The line x + y = 7 meets x² + y² = 25 at (3, 4) and (4, 3) (check: 9 + 16 = 25), so the chord has length √2.

Try it

Find the centre and radius of x² + y² + 2x − 4y − 4 = 0.

Answer. (x + 1)² + (y − 2)² = 9: centre (−1, 2), radius 3.

Worked examples with Try-its: Conics and a longer problem

The ellipse x²/25 + y²/9 = 1 has semi-axes 5 and 3, foci at (±4, 0) because c² = 25 − 9, and area 15π. The parabola y = x²/8 has focus (0, 2) (written x² = 4py with p = 2). The hyperbola x²/a² − y²/b² = 1 has c² = a² + b².

Problem. Find the points where the line y = x meets the ellipse x²/25 + y²/9 = 1, and their distance apart. Solution. x²(1/25 + 1/9) = 1, so x² = 225/34 and the two points are ±(15/√34)(1, 1). Their distance is 2 · √2 · 15/√34 = 30√2/√34 = 30/√17.

Reflection. Substitute one equation into the other and solve the resulting quadratic.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Circle through three points. The points (0, 0), (4, 0), (0, 3) form a right triangle, so the circumcentre is the midpoint of the hypotenuse, (2, 3/2), and the radius is 5/2. Parallel lines. The distance between 3x + 4y = 5 and 3x + 4y = 15 is |15 − 5|/√(9 + 16) = 2. Slopes. The line through (1, 2) and (5, 10) has slope 2, and the perpendicular through (1, 2) has slope −1/2.

Try it

Find the midpoint and length of the segment from (−1, 3) to (5, 11).

Answer. Midpoint (2, 7), length √(36 + 64) = 10.

Try it

Is the line y = x tangent to the circle (x − 3)² + y² = 4?

Answer. Distance from (3, 0) to the line is 3/√2 ≈ 2.12 > 2, so no.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. Find the circle through (0, 0), (1, 0) and (0, 1). Solution. The three points form a right triangle with hypotenuse from (1, 0) to (0, 1), so the centre is the midpoint (1/2, 1/2) and the radius is √2/2. The equation is (x − 1/2)² + (y − 1/2)² = 1/2, and the origin satisfies it: 1/4 + 1/4 = 1/2.

Reflection. Count the conditions: a circle needs three.
