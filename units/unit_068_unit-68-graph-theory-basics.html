1. Degrees, paths and connectivity

A graph is connected if every pair of vertices is joined by a path. The handshake lemma says the sum of degrees equals twice the edges, so the number of odd-degree vertices is even. A degree sequence like (3, 3, 2, 2, 2) is realisable by a simple graph (sum 12, so 6 edges), but (4, 4, 4, 1, 1) is not (three vertices of degree 4 in a 5-vertex graph would be adjacent to everyone, forcing every vertex to have degree at least 3) and (3, 3, 3, 1) is not (the three degree-3 vertices must all be joined to the degree-1 vertex). The Havel–Hakimi procedure checks this: remove the largest degree d and subtract 1 from the next d largest; repeat.

Trees. A tree is a connected graph with no cycle. A tree on n vertices has exactly n − 1 edges and at least two leaves (vertices of degree 1). A forest with k components has n − k edges. Cayley's formula counts labelled trees: nⁿ⁻² (16 for n = 4).

Try it

A tree has 10 vertices. How many edges? A graph with 10 vertices and 8 edges is a forest with how many components if it has no cycles?

Answer. 9 edges; 10 − 8 = 2 components.

2. Bipartite graphs and planarity

A graph is bipartite if its vertices can be split into two groups with all edges between the groups, equivalently if it has no odd cycle. A bipartite graph with parts of size a and b has at most ab edges. A planar graph can be drawn in the plane without crossing; for a connected planar graph, Euler's formula V − E + F = 2 holds, where F counts the regions including the outer one. Check: cube V = 8, E = 12, F = 6; dodecahedron 20 − 30 + 12 = 2, icosahedron 12 − 30 + 20 = 2.

Consequences. A simple planar graph has E ≤ 3V − 6 (and E ≤ 2V − 4 if it is bipartite). Hence K₅ (10 edges, bound 9) and K₃,₃ (9 edges, bound 2 · 6 − 4 = 8) are not planar.

Try it

Can a connected planar graph have 7 vertices and 16 edges?

Answer. 3 · 7 − 6 = 15 < 16, so no.

3. A longer problem

Problem. Prove that in any group of at least two people, two have the same number of acquaintances within the group.

Solution. Let n ≥ 2 people have degrees between 0 and n − 1. If someone has degree 0, nobody has degree n − 1; if someone has degree n − 1, nobody has degree 0. So only n − 1 distinct degree values are available for n people, and by pigeonhole two share a value.

Reflection. The key is noticing that 0 and n − 1 cannot both occur. This pattern — two extreme values being mutually exclusive — is very common.

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. (1) Forgetting loops and multiple edges are excluded in a simple graph. (2) Applying Euler's formula to a disconnected graph. (3) Miscounting the outer region.

Checks. Test claims on small graphs (the triangle, K₄, a path).

How to practise. Draw the graph. In Unlimited practice, rotate the graph-theory families.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Havel–Hakimi. Is (3, 3, 2, 2, 2) a graph's degree sequence? Remove the 3 and subtract 1 from the next three: (2, 1, 1, 2) → sorted (2, 2, 1, 1). Remove 2 and subtract from the next two: (1, 0, 1) → (1, 1, 0). Remove 1 and subtract from the next one: (0, 0). Yes. For (4, 4, 4, 1, 1) the first step gives (3, 3, 0, 0), then (2, −1, 0): impossible. A computer implementation of this test agrees with both answers.

Try it

Is (2, 2, 2, 2) a degree sequence?

Answer. Yes: the 4-cycle.

Try it

A tree has 6 leaves and every other vertex has degree 3. How many vertices does it have?

Answer. A tree on n vertices has n − 1 edges so the degree sum is 2(n − 1). With 6 leaves and k vertices of degree 3: 6 + 3k = 2(6 + k − 1), so k = 4 and n = 10.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. Prove that every tree with at least 2 vertices has at least 2 leaves. Solution. Take a longest path in the tree. Its two end vertices have no neighbours off the path (else the path would be longer) and no neighbours on the path other than the next vertex (else there would be a cycle), so both have degree 1.

Reflection. Extremal path arguments often prove graph facts.

Problem p1. A graph has 7 vertices, each of degree 4. How many edges does it have?

HintHandshake lemma.
Answer14
Method: Handshake

The degree sum is 28 and equals twice the number of edges, so there are 14.

Each edge has two ends; 28 ends means 14 edges.
