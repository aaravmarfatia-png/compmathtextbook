Reading

1. Derivatives

The derivative f′(x) is the slope of the tangent line. Rules: (xⁿ)′ = nxⁿ⁻¹; (f + g)′ = f′ + g′; product rule (fg)′ = f′g + fg′; quotient rule; chain rule (f(g(x)))′ = f′(g(x)) g′(x); (eˣ)′ = eˣ; (ln x)′ = 1/x; (sin x)′ = cos x; (cos x)′ = −sin x.

Worked example 1. The tangent to y = x² at x = 3 has slope 6 and passes through (3, 9): y = 6x − 9. Example 2. The derivative of (x² + 1)³ is 3(x² + 1)² × 2x = 6x(x² + 1)².

2. Maxima and minima

At a local maximum or minimum of a smooth function f′(x) = 0 (a critical point). The second derivative tells which: f″ > 0 means a minimum, f″ < 0 a maximum. On a closed interval also compare the endpoint values.

Worked example. f(x) = x³ − 3x has f′ = 3x² − 3 = 0 at x = ±1; f″ = 6x, so x = −1 is a local maximum (f = 2) and x = 1 is a local minimum (f = −2).

An optimisation. Of all rectangles with perimeter 20, the area x(10 − x) has derivative 10 − 2x = 0 at x = 5, giving 25.

3. Integrals

The integral ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx is the signed area under the curve. The fundamental theorem: if F′ = f then ∫ₐᵇ f = F(b) − F(a). Basic antiderivatives: ∫xⁿ = xⁿ⁺¹/(n + 1), ∫1/x = ln x, ∫eˣ = eˣ.

Worked example 1. ∫₀¹ x² dx = 1/3. Example 2. The area between y = x and y = x² on [0, 1] is ∫(x − x²) = 1/2 − 1/3 = 1/6. Example 3. Rotating y = x about the x-axis on [0, 1] gives a cone with volume π∫₀¹ x² dx = π/3, as the cone formula (π r² h/3 with r = h = 1) says.

Substitution. ∫ 2x cos(x²) dx = sin(x²) + C by u = x².

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. Forgetting the chain rule factor. Forgetting + C. Not checking endpoints of the interval. Treating a critical point as a maximum without testing.

Checks. Differentiate your antiderivative to recover the integrand.

Practice. Verify each formula by differentiating once.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Critical points. For f(x) = x³ − 3x², f′(x) = 3x² − 6x = 0 at x = 0 and x = 2, with f(0) = 0 a local maximum and f(2) = −4 a local minimum. Limit. (eˣ − 1)/x → 1 as x → 0. Integral. ∫₀² x³ dx = 4.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. A cylinder with no top and volume 27π is to be made with the least surface area. Find its radius. Solution. h = 27/r², area S = πr² + 2πrh = πr² + 54π/r. S′ = 2πr − 54π/r² = 0 gives r³ = 27, so r = 3 and h = 3.

Check. S = 9π + 18π = 27π at r = 3.

Problem p1. Find the local maximum value of f(x) = x³ − 6x² + 9x.

HintSolve f'(x) = 0.
Answer4
Method: Derivative

f' = 3x² − 12x + 9 = 3(x − 1)(x − 3), zero at x = 1, 3; f(1) = 4 is the local maximum and f(3) = 0 the local minimum.

Method: Check

f(0.9) = 3.969 and f(1.1) = 3.971, both below 4.

Problem p4. Find the area under y = x² from x = 0 to x = 3.

HintThe integral of x² is x³/3.
Answer9
Method: Antiderivative

3³/3 = 9.

Method: Riemann sums

With 3 equal strips, the left-hand sum is 5 and the right-hand sum is 14, bracketing 9; finer strips converge to 9.

Problem p5. A box without a top is made from a 12 × 12 sheet by cutting a square of side x from each corner and folding. What is the largest possible volume?

HintV = x(12 − 2x)².
Answer128
Method: Derivative

V = 4x³ − 48x² + 144x gives V' = 12x² − 96x + 144 = 12(x − 2)(x − 6), so x = 2 and V = 2 · 64 = 128.

Method: AM-GM

V = (1/4)(4x)(12 − 2x)(12 − 2x). The three factors have sum 24, so by AM-GM their product is at most 8³ = 512, and V ≤ 512/4 = 128, with equality when 4x = 12 − 2x, i.e. x = 2.

Source: Andreescu & Gelca, 2.10; Zeitz ch. 9; AoPS Volume 2 ch. 8
