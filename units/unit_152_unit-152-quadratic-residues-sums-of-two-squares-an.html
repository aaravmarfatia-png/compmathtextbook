1. Quadratic residues and reciprocity

Modulo an odd prime p, a is a quadratic residue if x² ≡ a has a solution. Euler's criterion: a^((p−1)/2) ≡ 1 for residues and ≡ −1 for non-residues. Example: mod 11 the squares are 1, 4, 9, 5, 3, so 5 is a residue (and 2 is not). Quadratic reciprocity says (p/q)(q/p) = (−1)^(((p−1)/2)((q−1)/2)): for odd primes p, q it lets you flip the symbol, so (3/101) = (101/3) = (2/3) = −1 because 101 ≡ 1 (mod 4).

Try it

Is 2 a residue mod 7?

Answer. Yes: 3² = 9 ≡ 2.

Try it

Is 3 a residue mod 11?

Answer. Yes: 5² = 25 ≡ 3.

2. Sums of two squares and integer polynomials

Fermat. An odd prime p is a sum of two squares iff p ≡ 1 (mod 4); in general n is, iff every prime ≡ 3 (mod 4) divides n to an even power. The number of integer pairs (a, b) with a² + b² = n is 4(d₁(n) − d₃(n)): for 25 the divisors are 1, 5, 25 (all ≡ 1), giving 12 pairs. Polynomials. For integer coefficients, a − b divides f(a) − f(b); and rational roots p/q of an integer polynomial satisfy p | constant term, q | leading coefficient. A cubic is determined by four values, found by finite differences.

Try it

Is 21 a sum of two squares?

Answer. No: 3 · 7 has two primes ≡ 3 (mod 4) to odd powers.

Try it

Which rational roots of 2x² − 3x − 2 = 0 are possible, and which are real?

Answer. The roots are 2 and −1/2.

3. A worked problem

Problem. Find the number of integer pairs with a² + b² = 65. Solution. 65 = 5 · 13 has divisors 1, 5, 13, 65 all ≡ 1 (mod 4), so the count is 4 · 4 = 16: (±1, ±8), (±8, ±1), (±4, ±7), (±7, ±4).

Check. 1 + 64 = 65 and 16 + 49 = 65 ✓.

Problem p13. What is the smallest positive integer x with x² ≡ −1 (mod 13)?

HintSince 13 ≡ 1 (mod 4), −1 is a quadratic residue; compute ((p−1)/2)! or search.
Answer5
Method: Search

Test x = 1, 2, …: the first success is x = 5, since 5² + 1 = 26 = 13·2.

Method: Wilson

For p ≡ 1 (mod 4), x = ((p−1)/2)! is a root; reducing gives 5 or 8, the smaller being 5.

Problem p18. The prime 29 is the sum of two positive squares a² + b². What is a + b?

HintSearch for the square that leaves a square.
Answer7
Method: Search

29 = 2² + 5², so a + b = 7.

Method: Gaussian integers

Factor p = (a + bi)(a − bi); the uniqueness of the factorization gives {a, b} = {2, 5}.

Problem p20. f is a polynomial with integer coefficients. What is the largest integer that must divide f(10) − f(4) for every such f?

Problem p22. A polynomial f of degree at most 3 satisfies f(0) = 1, f(1) = 3, f(2) = 9, f(3) = 19. What is f(4)?

HintUse finite differences or Lagrange interpolation.
Answer33
Method: Finite differences

Take differences until the third difference is constant, then extend the table: f(4) = 33.

Method: Lagrange

f(x) = 2*x**2 + 1, so f(4) = 33.
