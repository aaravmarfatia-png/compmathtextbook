Reading

1. Coordinates and complex numbers

Rotating by 90° about the origin sends (x, y) to (−y, x), which is multiplication by i. The circumcentre is the intersection of perpendicular bisectors: for the right triangle (0,0), (8,0), (0,6) it is the midpoint (4,3) of the hypotenuse. The shoelace formula gives the area ½|Σ(xᵢyᵢ₊₁ − xᵢ₊₁yᵢ)|: the rectangle (0,0), (4,0), (4,3), (0,3) has area 12.

Try it

Rotate (3, 1) by 90° about the origin?

Answer. (−1, 3).

Try it

Distance from (0,0) to 3x + 4y = 12?

Answer. 12/5.

2. Lengths and lattice points

Law of cosines: with angle 60° and sides 5, 8, BC² = 25 + 64 − 40 = 49, BC = 7; with angle 120° and sides 3, 5 we get 9 + 25 + 15 = 49 as well. Euler: OH² = 9R² − (a² + b² + c²), so OH = 0 in an equilateral triangle. Pick: A = I + B/2 − 1; the triangle (0,0), (6,0), (0,4) has A = 12, B = 12 and I = 7. A segment with differences (dx, dy) has gcd(dx, dy) + 1 lattice points.

Try it

Lattice points on the segment (0,0)–(6,9)?

Answer. gcd = 3, so 4.

Try it

Interior points of the triangle (0,0), (4,0), (0,4)?

Answer. A = 8, B = 12, so I = 8 − 6 + 1 = 3.

3. A worked problem

Problem. Find the circumcentre of (0,0), (6,0), (2,4). Solution. The perpendicular bisector of the base is x = 3. Equating distances to (0,0) and (2,4): 9 + y² = 1 + (y − 4)², so 8y = 8, y = 1; the circumcentre is (3, 1).

Check. Distance to (0,0) is √10 and to (2,4) is √(1 + 9) = √10 ✓.

4. In the books

Kedlaya. Ch. 1 develops the plane from the distance formula; 1.4 gives the area of a polygon by the shoelace formula, [P₁…Pₙ] = ½(x₁y₂ − x₂y₁ + … + xₙy₁ − x₁yₙ). Section 2.3 identifies points with complex numbers; 3.4 proves that every orientation-preserving similarity is z ↦ az + b, a translation if a = 1 and a spiral similarity otherwise (so a 90° rotation about c is z ↦ i(z − c) + c).

Aigner & Ziegler, ch. 12. The chapter on Euler's formula applications also proves Pick's theorem, A = I + B/2 − 1. Olympiad Treasures 2.8 (“Dr. Trig Learns Complex Numbers”) is a problem section on complex numbers in trigonometry.

Problem p1. Rotate the point (5, 2) by 90° counter-clockwise about (1, 1). What is the sum of the coordinates of the image?

HintShift so the centre is the origin, rotate by (x, y) → (−y, x), then shift back.
Answer5
Method: Translate and rotate

Relative position (4, 1) becomes (-1, 4); adding the centre gives (0, 5), sum 5.

Method: Complex numbers

Multiply (5 + 2i) − (1 + 1i) by i and add the centre back: 0 + 5i, so the sum is 5.

Source: Technique from Kedlaya ch. 1–3 and Aigner & Ziegler ch. 12 (original problem)

Problem p11. Triangle ABC has AB = 5, AC = 8 and angle A = 60°. Find BC.

HintLaw of cosines.
Answer7
Method: Law of cosines

BC² = 5² + 8² − 2·5·8·cos 60° = 49, so BC = 7.

Method: Coordinates

Put A at the origin and AC on the x-axis; B is at 5(cos 60°, sin 60°); the distance from B to C is 7.

Source: Technique from Kedlaya ch. 1–3 and Aigner & Ziegler ch. 12 (original problem)

Problem p15. Triangle ABC has sides 13, 14, 15. If O is the circumcentre and H the orthocentre, find OH². (fraction)

HintEuler: OH² = 9R² − (a² + b² + c²).
Answer265/64
Method: Euler's formula

R = 65/8, so OH² = 9·4225/64 − 590 = 265/64.

Method: Coordinates

Place B and C on the x-axis, compute O and H = A + B + C − 2O and the squared distance: 265/64.

Problem p21. How many lattice points lie strictly inside the triangle with vertices (0,0), (6,0), (0,4)?

HintPick's theorem: A = I + B/2 − 1.
Answer7
Method: Pick's theorem

A = 12, B = 6 + 4 + gcd(6,4) = 12, so I = 12 − 6 + 1 = 7.

Method: Count rows

For each x from 1 to 5 count the integers y with 0 < y < 4(1 − x/6); the total is 7.

Source: Technique from Kedlaya ch. 1–3 and Aigner & Ziegler ch. 12 (original problem)
