Reading

1. Extremes from simple inequalities

Many 'largest' or 'smallest' geometry questions reduce to AM-GM or the triangle inequality. Rectangles: with perimeter 20 the area x(10 − x) is largest when x = 5 (a square), area 25. Triangles: among triangles with a fixed base and fixed perimeter, the isosceles triangle has the greatest area. Inscribed in a circle: the regular n-gon has the greatest area.

Worked example. A right triangle with hypotenuse 10 has area ½ab with a² + b² = 100. Since 2ab ≤ a² + b² = 100, the area is at most 25, at a = b = 5√2.

2. Shortest paths

The shortest path between two points is a straight segment. If the path must touch a line, reflect one endpoint (Unit 104). On a solid, unfold faces into a plane and measure straight.

Worked example (cube). An ant walks along the surface of a unit cube from one corner to the opposite corner. Unfold two adjacent faces into a 2 × 1 rectangle; the diagonal is √(4 + 1) = √5 ≈ 2.236, shorter than going along three edges (3).

Worked example (cylinder). A cylinder has circumference 12 and height 5. Unrolled it is a 12 × 5 rectangle; a path from a point to the point directly across along the surface half way round has length √(6² + 5²) = √61 ≈ 7.81.

3. The Fermat point

The point P that minimises PA + PB + PC for a triangle with all angles below 120° is the Fermat point: the three segments make angles of 120° with one another. For an equilateral triangle of side 1 the Fermat point is the centre, with total distance 3 × (1/√3) = √3 ≈ 1.732.

Why. Rotate the triangle by 60° around A; the sum PA + PB + PC becomes the length of a broken line that is shortest when straight.

Worked example. If one angle is 120° or more, the Fermat point is that vertex. For the 3-4-5 triangle the minimum total distance is √((a² + b² + c²)/2 + 2√3 K), with a² + b² + c² = 50 and K = 6, so it is √(25 + 12√3) ≈ 6.77. (For an equilateral triangle of side 1 the same formula gives √(3/2 + 3/2) = √3 ✓.)

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. Reflecting both endpoints. Unfolding the wrong faces; try more than one net and take the shortest. Assuming the extremum occurs at a symmetric figure without checking the boundary.

Checks. Compare with a nearby non-optimal path.

Practice. For each problem try the three tools: AM-GM, reflection, unfolding.

Worked examples with Try-its: Shortest paths by unfolding

To find the shortest route along the surface of a box, unfold the faces into a plane and measure a straight line. On a 3 × 4 × 5 box between opposite corners the three possible unfoldings give √((3 + 4)² + 5²) = √74, √((3 + 5)² + 4²) = √80 and √((4 + 5)² + 3²) = √90, so the shortest path has length √74. On a unit cube the corresponding path across two faces has length √5.

Try it

A 1 × 1 × 2 box: find the shortest surface path between opposite corners.

Answer. Options √((1 + 1)² + 2²) = √8 and √((1 + 2)² + 1²) = √10; the shortest is √8.

Worked examples with Try-its: Reflection and Fermat point

To minimise a path that touches a line, reflect one endpoint across the line. The point inside a triangle (all angles under 120°) that minimises the sum of distances to the vertices is the Fermat point, where the sides subtend 120°. For an equilateral triangle of side 1 the minimum sum is √3. Among shapes of equal perimeter, the circle has the greatest area.

Try it

What is the sum of distances from the centre of an equilateral triangle of side 6 to its vertices?

Answer. 3 · (6/√3) = 6√3.

Worked examples with Try-its: A longer problem

Problem. A fly sits on the middle of one 1 × 1 end face of a 1 × 1 × 3 box (centre of the 1 × 1 face) and a spider at the centre of the opposite end. Find the shortest path along the surface. Solution. Unfold: the path goes ½ across the first end, 3 along a side, and ½ across the last end, totalling 4 in a straight line.

Reflection. Straight lines on the unfolded net are shortest paths on the surface.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Reflection principle. To minimise PA + PB for P on the x-axis with A = (0, 3), B = (8, 5), reflect A to (0, −3); the minimum is |A′B| = √(64 + 64) = 8√2, attained where the line A′B meets the axis, at x = 3. Perimeter. Among rectangles with area 36 the square has the least perimeter, 24.

Try it

Reflect A = (1, 2) in the line x = 4.

Answer. (7, 2).

Try it

Find the minimum value of √(x² + 1) + √((4 − x)² + 4) for real x.

Answer. The distance from (0, 1) to (4, −2) with a reflection: √(16 + 9) = 5.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. A point P is on the line y = x. Find the least value of PA + PB where A = (1, 0) and B = (0, 3). Solution. The points A and B are on opposite sides of y = x? A has y < x and B has y > x, so the straight segment AB crosses the line and the minimum is |AB| = √10.

Check. |AB| = √(1 + 9) = √10 ≈ 3.16.

Practice problems (25)

Problem p1. A rectangle is inscribed in a circle of radius 5. What is the largest possible area?

HintThe diagonal is the diameter 10.
Answer50
Method: AM-GM

With sides a, b: a² + b² = 100 and ab ≤ (a² + b²)/2 = 50, with equality for the square.

Method: Trig

If the diagonal makes angle θ with a side, the sides are 10 cos θ and 10 sin θ, so the area is 100 sin θ cos θ = 50 sin 2θ ≤ 50.

Source: Engel, Problem-Solving Strategies, ch. 12 (Geometry)

Problem p2. A right triangle has hypotenuse 10. What is its largest possible area?

Hintab ≤ (a² + b²)/2.
Answer25
Method: AM-GM

a² + b² = 100 gives ab ≤ 50, so the area ab/2 ≤ 25.

Method: Median

The altitude to the hypotenuse is at most the median, 5, so the area is at most (1/2)(10)(5) = 25.

Source: Engel, Problem-Solving Strategies, ch. 12 (Geometry)

Problem p3. A ray of light goes from A = (1, 2) to the x-axis and then to B = (7, 6). What is the shortest total path length?

HintReflect B in the x-axis.
Answer10
Method: Reflection

The image B' = (7, −6). The distance from A is √(6² + 8²) = 10.

Method: Check

The path hits the axis at the point where the line from A to B' crosses y = 0, which is x = 5/2.

Problem p5. How many triangles with positive integer sides have perimeter 15 (order ignored)?

HintTriangle inequality.
Answer7
Method: By longest side

c = 7 gives (1, 7, 7), (2, 6, 7), (3, 5, 7), (4, 4, 7): 4. c = 6 gives (3, 6, 6), (4, 5, 6): 2. c = 5 gives (5, 5, 5): 1. Total 7.

Problem p10. A(0, 2) and B(6, 4) are above the x-axis. A path goes from A to a point on the x-axis and then to B. Find the square of the shortest possible length.

Answer72
