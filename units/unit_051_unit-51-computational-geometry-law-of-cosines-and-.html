1. The law of cosines

For a triangle with sides a, b, c and opposite angles A, B, C: c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C. It generalises the Pythagorean theorem (cos 90° = 0) and is used when two sides and the included angle, or all three sides, are known. For sides 5 and 8 enclosing 60°, c² = 25 + 64 − 40 = 49, c = 7. Solving for the angle: cos C = (a² + b² − c²)/(2ab). For the 7-8-9 triangle the angle opposite 9 has cos C = (49 + 64 − 81)/112 = 2/7, so C ≈ 73.4°. In the 13-14-15 triangle the angle between 13 and 14 has cosine (169 + 196 − 225)/(2 · 13 · 14) = 5/13, so its sine is 12/13 and the area is ½ · 13 · 14 · 12/13 = 84 ✓.

Classifying a triangle by its sides. The largest angle is opposite the longest side, and it is acute, right or obtuse according as c² is less than, equal to or greater than a² + b². For 4, 5, 7: 49 > 41, and cos C = −0.2, so the triangle is obtuse. For 5, 6, 7 the largest angle has cosine 1/5.

Try it

Find the third side of a triangle with sides 3 and 5 enclosing 120°.

Answer. c² = 9 + 25 − 2 · 15 · (−1/2) = 49, so c = 7.

2. The law of sines and the circumradius

a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C = 2R, where R is the circumradius. It is used when two angles and a side, or two sides and a non-included angle, are known. For the 13-14-15 triangle the angle opposite 14 has sine 56/65 (from area = ½ · 13 · 15 · sin A = 84), so 2R = 14/(56/65) = 65/4 and R = 65/8 ✓ (agrees with Unit 26).

The ambiguous case (SSA). Given a = 6, b = 8 and A = 30°: sin B = 8 · sin 30°/6 = 2/3. Both B = 41.8° and B = 138.2° are possible (each leaves a positive third angle since 30° + 138.2° < 180°), so there are two triangles. If sin B came out larger than 1 there would be none; if b sin A = a (tangent case) exactly one right-angled triangle.

Try it

In a triangle A = 40°, B = 60° and a = 10. Find b.

Answer. b = 10 sin 60°/sin 40° = 8.66/0.6428 ≈ 13.47.

3. A longer problem

Problem. A boat sails 12 km on a bearing, turns through 50° and sails 9 km. How far is it from the start?

Solution. The turn through 50° makes the interior angle 130° (the angle between the legs measured inside the triangle is 180° − 50°). d² = 144 + 81 − 2 · 12 · 9 · cos 130° = 225 + 216 · 0.6428 = 363.8, d ≈ 19.07 km.

Reflection. The most common error in navigation problems is to use 50° in the formula instead of the interior angle 130°. Draw the triangle.

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. (1) Using the exterior angle. (2) Calculator in the wrong mode. (3) Ignoring the second solution in the ambiguous case. (4) Dividing wrongly: cos C = (a² + b² − c²)/(2ab).

Checks. The largest side faces the largest angle; angles sum to 180°.

How to practise. Sketch every triangle. In Unlimited practice, rotate the law-of-cosines and law-of-sines families.

More worked examples: Worked examples

A triangle with sides 5 and 8 enclosing 60° has third side √(25 + 64 − 40) = 7, area ½ · 5 · 8 · sin 60° = 10√3 and circumradius R = 7/(2 sin 60°) = 7/√3. For the reverse problem, a triangle with sides 5, 7, 8 has cos θ = (25 + 64 − 49)/(2 · 5 · 8) = 1/2 for the angle opposite 7, so that angle is 60°.

Try it

Sides 3, 5 and 7: find the largest angle.

Answer. cos θ = (9 + 25 − 49)/30 = −1/2, so θ = 120°.

Try it

In triangle ABC, A = 30°, B = 45°, a = 6. Find b.

Answer. b = 6 sin 45°/sin 30° = 6√2.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. The sides of a triangle are 7, 8 and 9. Find its area and inradius. Solution. s = 12 and Heron gives √(12 · 5 · 4 · 3) = √720 = 12√5. The inradius is area/s = √5.

Check. cos A (opposite 7) = (64 + 81 − 49)/144 = 2/3, so sin A = √5/3 and the area is ½ · 8 · 9 · √5/3 = 12√5 ✓.

Practice problems (25)

Problem p1. A triangle has sides 7, 8, 9. Find the cosine of the angle opposite the side 7 as a fraction.

HintLaw of cosines.
Answer2/3
Method: Law of cosines

cos = (8² + 9² − 7²)/(2·8·9) = 96/144 = 2/3.

Method: Coordinates

Put the vertex V where the sides 8 and 9 meet at (0,0), the end of the side 9 at P = (9,0) and the end of the side 8 at Q = (8c, 8s) with c = cos θ. Then PQ² = (9 − 8c)² + 64s² = 49, so 81 − 144c + 64 = 49 and c = 96/144 = 2/3.

Source: Chen, Euclidean Geometry in Mathematical Olympiads, ch. 5

Problem p2. In triangle ABC, angle A = 30° and BC = 5. Find the circumradius.

Hinta/sin A = 2R.
Answer5
Method: Law of sines

2R = 5/sin 30° = 10, so R = 5.

Method: Inscribed angle

The chord BC subtends a 30° inscribed angle, so the central angle is 60° and triangle OBC is equilateral with side R = 5.

Problem p5. A triangle has sides 4, 5, 6. Find the square of its circumradius as a fraction.

HintR = abc/(4·Area).
Answer64/7
Method: Area formula

Heron gives area 15√7/4. R = 120/(4·15√7/4) = 8/√7, so R² = 64/7.

Problem p6. Find the area of the triangle with vertices (1,1), (5,2) and (3,7).

HintShoelace formula.
Answer11
Method: Shoelace

½|1(2 − 7) + 5(7 − 1) + 3(1 − 2)| = ½|−5 + 30 − 3| = 11.

Method: Vectors

From (1,1) the other vertices are at displacements (4,1) and (2,6). The area is half the determinant: |4·6 − 1·2|/2 = 11.

Problem x18. A triangle has sides 6, 8 and 10. Find the square of the median to the side of length 6.

Hintm² = (2b² + 2c² − a²)/4.
Answer73
Method: Median formula

(2 · 64 + 2 · 100 − 36)/4 = 73.

Method: Parallelogram law

Doubling the median completes a parallelogram with diagonals 6 and 2m; the law gives 36 + 4m² = 2(64 + 100), so m² = 73.
