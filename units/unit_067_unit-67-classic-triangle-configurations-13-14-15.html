Reading

1. The 13-14-15 triangle: a catalogue

The triangle with sides 13, 14, 15 is the most famous 'nice' scalene triangle: its area is 84 (Heron: s = 21, √(21 · 8 · 7 · 6) = 84), and almost every associated length is rational. Placing B = (0, 0), C = (14, 0), A = (5, 12) splits it into the 5-12-13 triangle (left) and the 9-12-15 triangle (right). Altitudes: onto 14 it is 12, onto 15 it is 2 · 84/15 = 56/5, onto 13 it is 168/13. Inradius 4, circumradius 65/8, semiperimeter 21, tangent lengths 7, 6, 8.

Centres. Centroid (19/3, 4), incentre (6, 4), circumcentre (7, 33/8), orthocentre (5, 15/4). Notice that the incentre has integer coordinates and lies at the same height 4 as the centroid. Excircle radii 12, 14, 10.5, and 1/r = 1/rₐ + 1/r_b + 1/r_c holds.

Why it appears so often. Two Pythagorean triples (5, 12, 13) and (9, 12, 15) share the leg 12, so gluing along it gives integer sides. The same trick generates other 'Heronian' triangles (integer sides and area): glue two copies of 3-4-5 along the leg 4 to get the isosceles 5-5-6 (area 12), or glue 9-12-15 and 5-12-13 along 12 to get 13-14-15.

Try it

Find the length of the altitude from B to side AC in the 13-14-15 triangle.

Answer. 2 · 84/15 = 56/5 = 11.2.

2. Facts to use

Angle bisector from A meets BC at distance 6.5 from B; its length is √146.25 ≈ 12.09. Median from A has length √148. Cosines: cos B = 5/13, cos C = 3/5, cos A = 33/65, with sines 12/13, 4/5 and 56/65. Check that the angles sum to 180°: arccos(5/13) ≈ 67.38°, arccos(3/5) ≈ 53.13°, arccos(33/65) ≈ 59.49°, total 180.00°.

Integer relatives. The 13-14-15 triangle has area 84, which equals 6 × 14: the area is 6 times the side 14, because the altitude to 14 is 12 and ½ · 12 = 6. Other Heronian triangles: 5-5-6 (area 12), 7-15-20 (area 42), 17-25-28 (area 210).

Try it

Find cos B in the 13-14-15 triangle where B is the angle at vertex B between sides 13 and 14.

Answer. (169 + 196 − 225)/(2 · 13 · 14) = 140/364 = 5/13.

3. A longer problem

Problem. In the 13-14-15 triangle ABC (BC = 14), the incircle touches BC at D. Find AD.

Solution. BD = s − b = 21 − 15 = 6, so D = (6, 0) in the coordinates above; A = (5, 12), so AD = √(1 + 144) = √145.

Reflection. Having a coordinate model of the standard triangle makes every such question a short distance computation.

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. (1) Placing the triangle so that the altitude foot falls outside BC (for 13-14-15 with BC = 14 the foot is inside). (2) Mixing up which side is a, b, c. (3) Forgetting that tangent lengths are s − a, etc.

Checks. Verify every length with the distance formula.

How to practise. Memorise the triangle's six or seven key numbers. In Unlimited practice, rotate the standard-triangle families.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Euler's distance formula. The distance between the circumcentre O and incentre I satisfies OI² = R² − 2Rr. For the 13-14-15 triangle R = 65/8 and r = 4, so OI² = 4225/64 − 65 = 65/64, matching the coordinates O = (7, 33/8) and I = (6, 4): (1)² + (1/8)² = 65/64 ✓. Other facts. The altitude to the side 14 is 12, the area is 84, and the three altitudes are 12, 56/5 and 168/13. The 3-4-5 triangle has inradius 1 and circumradius 5/2. Because OI² cannot be negative, Euler's formula implies R ≥ 2r in every triangle.

Try it

Use Euler's formula to find OI² for a 3-4-5 triangle.

Answer. R = 5/2, r = 1: OI² = 25/4 − 5 = 5/4.

Try it

What is the inradius of the 5-12-13 triangle?

Answer. (5 + 12 − 13)/2 = 2.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. The 13-14-15 triangle ABC has BC = 14. The altitude from A meets BC at H. Find the distance from H to the point where the incircle touches BC. Solution. With B = (0, 0), the foot of the altitude is H = (5, 0), and the incircle touches BC at (s − b, 0) = (6, 0), so the distance is 1.

Reflection. The foot of the altitude and the touch point both come from the same coordinate model.

Problem p1. Find the area of the triangle with sides 13, 14, 15.

HintHeron's formula.
Answer84
Method: Heron

s = 21, K = √(21·8·7·6) = √7056 = 84.

Method: Altitude

Drop the altitude 12 on the side 14 (a 5-12-13 and 9-12-15 split): (1/2)·14·12 = 84.

Source: Chen, Euclidean Geometry in Mathematical Olympiads, ch. 4

Problem p2. Find the inradius of the 13-14-15 triangle.

Hintr = K/s.
Answer4
Method: Formula

r = 84/21 = 4.

Method: Half-angle

The tangent length from B is 6 and the inradius is 4, so tan(B/2) = 4/6 = 2/3. Check: cos B = (13² + 14² − 15²)/(2·13·14) = 5/13, and tan(B/2) = sin B/(1 + cos B) = (12/13)/(18/13) = 2/3.

Source: Chen, Euclidean Geometry in Mathematical Olympiads, ch. 4

Method: Check

65/8 = 8.125, larger than half the longest side 7.5, as required.

Problem p16. With B = (0, 0), C = (14, 0), A = (5, 12), find the y-coordinate of the circumcentre.

HintIt lies on x = 7 at distance R = 65/8 from B.
Answer33/8
Method: Distance

y² = (65/8)² − 7² = 4225/64 − 3136/64 = 1089/64, so y = 33/8.

Method: Equal distances

From (7, y) the distances to B and A agree: 49 + y² = (7 − 5)² + (y − 12)², giving 49 + y² = 4 + y² − 24y + 144, so 24y = 99 and y = 33/8.

Source: Chen, Euclidean Geometry in Mathematical Olympiads, ch. 4

Problem p17. With B = (0, 0), C = (14, 0), A = (5, 12), find the y-coordinate of the orthocentre.

HintThe altitude from A is x = 5; the altitude from B is perpendicular to AC.
Answer15/4
Method: Altitudes

AC has direction (9, −12), so the altitude from B is 9x − 12y = 0, i.e. y = 3x/4. At x = 5 this gives 15/4.

Method: Euler

H = A + B + C − 2O = (19, 12) − (14, 33/4) = (5, 15/4).

Source: Chen, Euclidean Geometry in Mathematical Olympiads, ch. 4
