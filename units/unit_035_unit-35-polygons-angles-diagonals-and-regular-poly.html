Reading

1. Angles in polygons

A polygon with n sides can be cut from one vertex into n − 2 triangles, so its interior angles sum to (n − 2) · 180°: 180° for n = 3, 360° for n = 4, 540° for n = 5, 720° for n = 6, 1080° for n = 8, 1440° for n = 10 and 1800° for n = 12. The exterior angles (one at each vertex, turning in the same direction) always sum to 360°, whatever n is.

Regular polygons. All sides and angles equal. Each interior angle is (n − 2) · 180°/n: 108° for a pentagon, 120° for a hexagon, 135° for an octagon. Each exterior angle is 360°/n, so 36° for a decagon. To find n from an angle, use the exterior angle: if the interior angle is 156° the exterior angle is 24° and n = 360/24 = 15.

Convex or not. The formulas for the angle sum hold for any simple polygon, convex or not; the exterior-angle fact needs the signed (turning) version. Always check the number of sides before using the formula.

Try it

Each interior angle of a regular polygon is 168°. How many sides?

Answer. Exterior 12°, n = 30.

2. Diagonals

A convex n-gon has n(n − 3)/2 diagonals: from each vertex there are n − 3 diagonals (not to itself or its two neighbours), and each diagonal is counted from both ends. The counts for n = 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12 are 2, 5, 9, 20, 35, 54, and a 20-gon has 170.

Triangulations. Any convex n-gon can be cut into n − 2 triangles by n − 3 non-crossing diagonals; the number of different triangulations is the Catalan number C(n − 2): 14 for the hexagon.

Regions made by all diagonals. If no three diagonals meet at a point, the diagonals divide the interior of a convex n-gon into C(n, 4) + C(n − 1, 2) regions: 1, 4, 11, 25 for n = 3, 4, 5, 6. Each intersection point of two diagonals corresponds to a choice of 4 vertices, which is why C(n, 4) appears.

Try it

How many diagonals does a convex 15-gon have?

Answer. 15 · 12/2 = 90.

3. Regular polygons: lengths and areas

Place a regular n-gon in a circle of radius R. Its area is (n/2)R² sin(360°/n), because it consists of n triangles with two sides R and apex angle 360°/n. For n = 8 and R = 1 the area is 2√2. A regular hexagon of side s splits into 6 equilateral triangles and has area (3√3/2)s²: for s = 2 that is 6√3.

The pentagon and the golden ratio. In a regular pentagon the ratio of the diagonal to the side is φ = (1 + √5)/2 ≈ 1.618 (verified from the coordinates of the vertices on a unit circle). This is why the pentagram contains a nest of similar pentagons and pentagrams, each smaller by a factor of φ².

Octagon. A regular octagon of side 1 has area 2 + 2√2 (cut the corners off a square of side 1 + √2).

Apothem. The distance from the centre to the midpoint of a side is the apothem a, and the area of a regular polygon is ½ · perimeter · apothem.

Try it

Find the area of a regular hexagon with side 4.

Answer. (3√3/2) · 16 = 24√3.

4. Counting with polygons

Triangles from vertices. The vertices of a convex 12-gon form C(12, 3) = 220 triangles. Among them, the equilateral ones use every fourth vertex: 4 of them. To count isosceles triangles, fix the apex and choose a symmetric pair of base vertices.

Interior angles in a sequence. If a convex polygon's angles are in arithmetic progression, use the sum formula to determine the middle value: for 5 angles with common difference 10°, the sum is 540°, so the middle angle is 108° and the angles are 88°, 98°, 108°, 118°, 128°.

Try it

A convex hexagon has five angles of 110°. What is the sixth?

Answer. 720 − 550 = 170°.

5. A longer problem

Problem. A regular polygon has interior angle 150°. (a) How many sides? (b) How many diagonals? (c) What is the area if the side is 2?

Solution. (a) Exterior 30°, so 12 sides. (b) 12 · 9/2 = 54. (c) A regular 12-gon of side 2 has area 3 · s² · (2 + √3) = 12(2 + √3) = 24 + 12√3: check by cutting it into 12 triangles with apex 30° and apothem (s/2)/tan 15° = 1/tan 15° = 2 + √3, giving area ½ · 24 · (2 + √3).

Reflection. The exterior angle did the first part at once. For the area, the apothem formula ½ · perimeter · apothem is the most reliable route.

6. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. (1) Using n · 180 instead of (n − 2) · 180. (2) Counting diagonals as n(n − 3) without halving. (3) Mixing circumradius and side. (4) Forgetting that the exterior angle is 180° minus the interior angle.

Checks. Test formulas on the square and the hexagon. The exterior angles must sum to 360°.

How to practise. Draw the polygon and mark one triangle. In Unlimited practice, rotate the polygon-angle and diagonal families.

Problem p1. Find the sum of the interior angles of a hexagon, in degrees.

Hint(n − 2)·180.

Problem p2. Find each interior angle of a regular 12-sided polygon, in degrees.

HintExterior angle is 360/12.
Answer150
Method: Exterior angle

Each exterior angle is 360°/12 = 30°, so each interior angle is 180° − 30° = 150°.

The interior sum is 10·180° = 1800°, shared among 12 equal angles: 150° each.

Problem p7. A regular hexagon has side 6. Find its area. (Write 54√3 style.)

HintSix equilateral triangles.
Answer54√3
Method: Six triangles

Each equilateral triangle of side 6 has area (√3/4)·36 = 9√3. Six of them: 54√3.

Method: Trapezoids

Cut the hexagon along its long diagonal into two trapezoids. Each has height 3√3 and parallel sides 6 and 12, so area (6 + 12)/2 · 3√3 = 27√3. Together: 54√3.

Source: Rusczyk, Art of Problem Solving Vol. 1, ch. 13

Problem p8. A regular hexagon has side 10. What is the distance between two opposite sides?

HintTwo apothems.
Answer10√3
Method: Apothem

The apothem (centre to a side) is 5√3, the height of an equilateral triangle of side 10. The distance across is twice that: 10√3.

Method: Trapezoid height

Cut the hexagon into two trapezoids; each has height 5√3, and together they span the distance 10√3.

Source: Rusczyk, Art of Problem Solving Vol. 1, ch. 13

Problem p9. In a regular pentagon, find the angle between the two diagonals from one vertex, in degrees.

HintThe three angles at a vertex are equal.
Answer36
Method: Equal angles

The interior angle is 108°, and the two diagonals divide it into three equal angles of 36° (each is an inscribed angle on an arc of 72°). The angle between the diagonals is 36°.

Method: Inscribed angles

Each side of a regular pentagon cuts a 72° arc of the circumscribed circle. The angle between the diagonals cuts one such arc, so it is 72°/2 = 36°.

Source: Rusczyk, Art of Problem Solving Vol. 1, ch. 13

Problem p10. How many diagonals does a regular octagon have, and what is each of its interior angles in degrees? Answer as diagonals, angle.

HintUse n(n − 3)/2 and 180 − 360/n.
Answer20, 135
Method: Formulas

8·5/2 = 20 diagonals; the interior angle is 180° − 360°/8 = 135°.

Method: Reasoning

Each vertex has 5 diagonals, 8·5/2 = 20. The angle sum is 6·180° = 1080°, so each angle is 135°.

Problem x23. 25 objects are placed into 6 boxes. What is the largest number n for which some box must contain at least n objects?

HintPigeonhole principle.
Answer5
Method: Pigeonhole

⌈25/6⌉ = 5.

Method: Contradiction

If every box had at most 4 objects, there would be at most 6 · 4 = 24 < 25 objects in all.
