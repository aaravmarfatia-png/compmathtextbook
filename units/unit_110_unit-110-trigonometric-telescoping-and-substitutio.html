1. Product of cosines by doubling

Since sin 2x = 2 sin x cos x, multiplying cos x cos 2x cos 4x … cos(2^{n−1} x) by sin x telescopes: sin x · Π cos(2^k x) = sin(2^n x)/2^n. So Π_{k=0}^{n−1} cos(2^k x) = sin(2^n x)/(2^n sin x).

Worked example. cos(π/7) cos(2π/7) cos(4π/7). With x = π/7 and n = 3: sin(8π/7)/(8 sin(π/7)) = −sin(π/7)/(8 sin(π/7)) = −1/8. Numerically cos 25.71° × cos 51.43° × cos 102.86° = 0.9010 × 0.6235 × (−0.2225) = −0.125 ✓.

2. Telescoping sums with trigonometry

The identity cot(x/2) − cot x = 1/sin x lets you telescope sums of 1/sin(2^k x). Check at x = π/2: cot(π/4) − cot(π/2) = 1 − 0 = 1 = 1/sin(π/2) ✓. Also tan x = cot x − 2 cot 2x. And sin(k + 1) − sin(k − 1) = 2 cos k sin 1, which telescopes sums of cos k.

Worked example. Since 2 sin(½) cos k = sin(k + ½) − sin(k − ½), the sum telescopes: Σ_{k=1}^{n} cos k = [sin(n + ½) − sin ½]/(2 sin ½).

Angle addition. tan(a − b) = (tan a − tan b)/(1 + tan a tan b). Taking a = k + 1 and b = k gives tan 1 = (tan(k+1) − tan k)/(1 + tan(k+1) tan k), which turns Σ tan k tan(k+1) into a telescoping sum.

3. Trigonometric substitutions

When you see √(1 − x²), put x = sin θ (then √(1 − x²) = cos θ for θ in [−π/2, π/2]). For √(1 + x²) or 1 + x², put x = tan θ. For √(x² − 1), put x = sec θ.

Worked example. Maximise x√(1 − x²) for 0 ≤ x ≤ 1. With x = sin θ the expression is sin θ cos θ = ½ sin 2θ ≤ ½, attained at θ = 45°, x = √2/2.

Worked example 2. If x² + y² = 1 then 3x + 4y has maximum 5: write x = cos θ, y = sin θ so 3 cos θ + 4 sin θ = 5 cos(θ − φ).

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. Dividing by sin x when it could be zero. Taking the wrong sign for a square root of a trigonometric expression. Using x = sin θ when |x| might exceed 1.

Checks. Evaluate the closed form numerically for a small n.

Practice. Learn the doubling and cot identities until automatic.

Worked examples with Try-its: Telescoping with trigonometry

Repeated use of sin 2x = 2 sin x cos x gives cos x · cos 2x · cos 4x = sin 8x/(8 sin x). With x = 20° this yields cos 20° cos 40° cos 80° = sin 160°/(8 sin 20°) = 1/8, since sin 160° = sin 20°. Another telescoping identity: 1/sin x = cot(x/2) − cot x, so 1/sin θ + 1/sin 2θ + 1/sin 4θ = cot(θ/2) − cot 4θ (checked numerically for θ = 10°).

Try it

Evaluate cos 36° · cos 72°.

Answer. 1/4.

Worked examples with Try-its: Substitutions

When x² + y² = 1 put x = cos θ and y = sin θ. For expressions with √(1 + x²) try x = tan θ; for √(1 − x²) try x = sin θ. These substitutions turn algebraic problems into trigonometric identities. For example, with x = tan θ the expression 2x/(1 + x²) equals sin 2θ.

Try it

If x = tan 15°, what is 2x/(1 + x²)?

Answer. sin 30° = 1/2.

Worked examples with Try-its: A longer problem

Problem. Show that tan 20° · tan 40° · tan 80° = √3. Check. The identity tan x · tan(60° − x) · tan(60° + x) = tan 3x with x = 20° gives tan 20° tan 40° tan 80° = tan 60° = √3. Numerically 0.36397 · 0.83910 · 5.67128 ≈ 1.7321.

Reflection. Triple-angle identities are the natural tool when angles come in 20° steps.

More worked examples: Worked examples

A product. sin 10° · sin 50° · sin 70° = 1/8 (numerically 0.125000). It follows from cos 20° cos 40° cos 80° = 1/8 because sin 70° = cos 20°, sin 50° = cos 40° and sin 10° = cos 80°. Substitution. If x = 2 cos θ then x³ − 3x = 2 cos 3θ, which solves x³ − 3x = 1 with θ = 20°.

Try it

Evaluate cos 20° · cos 40° · cos 80°.

Answer. 1/8.

Try it

If x = tan θ, what is (1 − x²)/(1 + x²)?

Answer. cos 2θ.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. Show that x³ − 3x − 1 = 0 has the root 2 cos 20°. Solution. With x = 2 cos θ the left side is 2 cos 3θ − 1, which is zero when cos 3θ = 1/2, i.e. 3θ = 60° and θ = 20°.

Check. 2 cos 20° ≈ 1.879, and 1.879³ − 3 · 1.879 ≈ 6.634 − 5.638 ≈ 1.

Problem p1. Find 4 cos 36° cos 72°.

HintUse sin 2θ = 2 sin θ cos θ.
Answer1
Method: Doubling

cos 36° cos 72° = sin 144°/(4 sin 36°) = 1/4, since sin 144° = sin 36°, so the expression is 1.

Method: Numeric

cos 36° ≈ 0.8090 and cos 72° ≈ 0.3090, product ≈ 0.25.

Source: Andreescu & Gelca, 1.9-1.10

Problem p2. If tan θ = 2, find sin θ cos θ as a fraction.

Hintsin θ cos θ = tan θ/(1 + tan² θ).
Answer2/5
Method: Formula

2/(1 + 4) = 2/5.

Method: Triangle

The right triangle with legs 2 and 1 has hypotenuse √5, so sin θ cos θ = (2/√5)(1/√5) = 2/5.

Source: Andreescu & Gelca, 1.9-1.10

Problem p3. Find sin²1° + sin²2° + … + sin²89° as a fraction.

HintPair k° with (90 − k)°.
Answer89/2
Method: Pairing

sin²k° + sin²(90° − k)° = sin²k° + cos²k° = 1. The 88 terms with k ≠ 45 form 44 pairs and sin² 45° = 1/2, so the sum is 44 + 1/2 = 89/2.

Method: Numeric

The sum is 44.5.

Source: Andreescu & Gelca, 1.9-1.10

Problem x18. The roots of x² − 8x + 11 = 0 are r and s. Find r² + s².

HintUse r + s and rs.
Answer42
Method: Vieta

r + s = 8 and rs = 11, so r² + s² = (r + s)² − 2rs = 64 − 22 = 42.

Method: Recurrence

Let pₙ = rⁿ + sⁿ. Then p₀ = 2, p₁ = 8 and p₂ = 8 · p₁ − 11 · p₀ = 64 − 22 = 42.

Source: Andreescu & Gelca, 1.9-1.10
