Reading

1. Tangents

A tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact. The two tangent segments from an outside point P to a circle are equal, and if O is the centre and r the radius, the tangent length is √(PO² − r²): for PO = 13, r = 5 it is 12. The line from P through O bisects the angle between the tangents.

Two circles. For circles with radii r₁ and r₂ whose centres are d apart, a common external tangent has length √(d² − (r₁ − r₂)²) and a common internal tangent has length √(d² − (r₁ + r₂)²). With r₁ = 3, r₂ = 5, d = 8 (touching externally) the external tangent is √(64 − 4) = 2√15 = 2√(r₁r₂). With d = 10 the internal tangent has length √(100 − 64) = 6.

Try it

From a point 17 units from the centre of a circle of radius 8 a tangent is drawn. How long is it?

Answer. √(289 − 64) = 15.

2. Chords

A perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects it. A chord at distance 3 from the centre of a circle of radius 5 has length 2√(25 − 9) = 8. Chords equidistant from the centre have equal length. Equal chords subtend equal arcs and equal angles at the centre.

Two circles intersecting. The line joining the two intersection points is the common chord; it is perpendicular to the line of centres. Two circles of radius 5 with centres 6 apart have a common chord of length 2√(25 − 9) = 8.

Try it

A chord of length 24 is in a circle of radius 13. How far is it from the centre?

Answer. √(169 − 144) = 5.

3. The radical axis

The power of a point P with respect to a circle with centre O and radius r is PO² − r². For two circles, the set of points with equal power with respect to both is a straight line, the radical axis. For x² + y² = 25 and (x − 6)² + y² = 9, equal power means x² + y² − 25 = (x − 6)² + y² − 9, which simplifies to 12x − 36 = 16, i.e. x = 13/3. At (13/3, 0) the power with respect to each circle is −56/9 (negative, so inside both).

Properties. The radical axis is perpendicular to the line of centres. If the circles intersect, it is the line through the intersection points (the common chord). For three circles the three radical axes meet at a point, the radical centre (or are parallel).

Using it. To prove that three points are collinear, show that they all have equal power with respect to two circles. To prove tangent lengths equal from a point on the radical axis, use that its power is the squared tangent length.

Try it

Find the radical axis of x² + y² = 4 and x² + y² − 6x = 0.

Answer. Equal power: x² + y² − 4 = x² + y² − 6x, so 6x = 4 and x = 2/3.

4. A longer problem

Problem. Two circles with radii 5 and 3 have centres 7 apart. (a) Do they intersect? (b) Find the length of their common chord. (c) Find the length of a common external tangent.

Solution. (a) The circles intersect in two points exactly when |5 − 3| < d < 5 + 3, i.e. 2 < 7 < 8, which holds. (b) Place the centres at (0, 0) and (7, 0). Radical axis: x² + y² − 25 = (x − 7)² + y² − 9 gives 14x = 65, x = 65/14. Then y² = 25 − (65/14)² = 25 − 4225/196 = 675/196, so the half-chord is √675/14 = 15√3/14 and the chord is 15√3/7. (c) √(49 − 4) = √45 = 3√5.

Reflection. The radical axis converts a two-circle problem into a line-and-circle problem.

5. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. (1) Using the distance between centres instead of the tangent length. (2) Mixing up internal and external tangent formulas. (3) Forgetting the power is negative inside a circle. (4) Not squaring correctly when computing half-chords.

Checks. Check the right angle at the point of tangency with the Pythagorean theorem. Check chord lengths with the triangle formed by the centre.

How to practise. Draw the radii to points of tangency. In Unlimited practice, rotate the tangent and chord families.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Radical axis. For the circles x² + y² = 25 and (x − 6)² + y² = 9 subtract the equations x² + y² − 25 = 0 and (x − 6)² + y² − 9 = 0: the difference is 12x − 52 = 0, so the radical axis is x = 13/3. At that x we get y² = 25 − 169/9 = 56/9, so the circles meet in two points. Power of a point. A point P outside a circle at distance d from the centre has power d² − r² = (tangent length)².

Try it

A point is 13 from the centre of a circle of radius 5. How long is the tangent from it?

Answer. 12.

Try it

Chords AB and CD meet inside a circle at P with PA = 4, PB = 3, PC = 2. Find PD.

Answer. PD = 4 · 3/2 = 6.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. Two circles of radius 5 have centres 6 apart. Find the length of their common chord. Solution. The radical axis is the perpendicular bisector of the centres, at distance 3 from each. Half the chord is √(25 − 9) = 4, so the chord has length 8.

Reflection. For equal circles the radical axis is the perpendicular bisector.

Problem p1. A point P is 13 from the centre of a circle of radius 5. How long is a tangent from P to the circle?

HintThe radius to the tangent point is perpendicular to the tangent.
Answer12
Method: Right triangle

The triangle O, tangent point, P is right-angled with legs 5 and tangent length t and hypotenuse 13: t = √(169 − 25) = 12.

Method: Power of a point

The power of P is 13² − 5² = 144 = t², so t = 12.

Problem p4. The circles x² + y² = 25 and (x − 8)² + y² = 9 have a radical axis (the set of points with equal power for both circles), which is a vertical line x = c. Find c.

HintSubtract the two equations.
Answer5
Method: Subtract

Equal powers means x² + y² − 25 = (x − 8)² + y² − 9. Expanding, −25 = −16x + 64 − 9, so 16x = 80 and x = 5.

Method: Check

The centres are 8 apart and the radii add to 5 + 3 = 8, so the circles touch at (5, 0). The radical axis of touching circles is the common tangent there, the line x = 5.

Problem x19. Chords AB and CD of a circle meet at P inside it with AP = 8, PB = 9 and CP = 12. Find PD.

HintIntersecting chords theorem.
Answer6
Method: Theorem

AP · PB = CP · PD, so 8 · 9 = 12 · PD and PD = 6.

Method: Similar triangles

Triangles APC and DPB are similar, so AP/DP = CP/BP, giving the same equation 8 · 9 = 12 · PD.
