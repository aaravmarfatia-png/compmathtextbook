Reading

1. Inversion in a circle

Inversion about a circle of centre O and radius r sends a point P ≠ O to the point P′ on ray OP with OP · OP′ = r². Points inside the circle go outside and vice versa; points on the circle stay fixed. In the unit circle (2, 0) goes to (1/2, 0) and (1, 1) goes to (1/2, 1/2); in a circle of radius 5, the point (3, 4) (at distance 5) is fixed. Applying inversion twice returns the original point.

What it does to lines and circles. A line through O maps to itself. A line not through O maps to a circle through O: the line x = 1 maps to the circle with centre (1/2, 0) and radius 1/2 (every image point (1/(1 + t²), t/(1 + t²)) was verified to lie on it). Conversely a circle through O maps to a line not through O, and a circle not through O maps to a circle. Angles are preserved (though orientation reverses), and tangent circles map to tangent circles or lines.

Try it

Find the image of (3, 4) under inversion in the unit circle.

Answer. (3/25, 4/25).

2. Using inversion

Inversion straightens circles through the centre of inversion into lines, turning hard circle problems into easier line problems. Example. Two circles tangent at O invert to two parallel lines; a chain of circles tangent to both becomes a chain of equal circles between parallel lines (Pappus chain). Ptolemy via inversion. Invert about one vertex of a cyclic quadrilateral: the other three vertices land on a line and the length relation becomes collinearity (A′B′ + B′C′ = A′C′), which is Ptolemy's equality.

Distance formula. If A′ and B′ are the inverses of A and B, then A′B′ = r² · AB/(OA · OB). Check: with r = 1, A = (2, 0), B = (0, 3): AB = √13, OA · OB = 6, so A′B′ = √13/6; and A′ = (1/2, 0), B′ = (0, 1/3) have distance √(1/4 + 1/9) = √13/6 ✓.

Try it

Under inversion in the unit circle, what is the image of the circle x² + y² = 4?

Answer. The circle of radius 1/2 about the origin.

3. A longer problem

Problem. Invert the line x + y = 2 in the unit circle. Describe the image.

Solution. The line does not pass through the origin; its nearest point to O is (1, 1), at distance √2. The image is a circle through O with diameter from O to the inverse of (1, 1), which is (1/2, 1/2): so the circle has centre (1/4, 1/4) and radius √2/4, i.e. x² + y² = (x + y)/2.

Reflection. The foot of the perpendicular from O to the line inverts to the far end of the diameter of the image circle.

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. (1) Forgetting that O itself has no image. (2) Thinking that a circle's centre maps to the centre of its image. (3) Using OP + OP′ instead of the product. (4) Forgetting that inversion changes lengths according to the distance formula.

Checks. Compute the inverse of a couple of points and check the product OP · OP′.

How to practise. Always invert at a point where many circles meet. In Unlimited practice, rotate the inversion families.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Inversion in the unit circle sends z to 1/z̄: the point 2 goes to 1/2, and 1 + i goes to (1 + i)/2. The line x = 2 (not through the centre) goes to a circle through the origin with diameter from 0 to 1/2. Distances change: for P, Q with images P′, Q′ we have P′Q′ = r² · PQ/(OP · OQ), so for r = 1, P = 2 and Q = 3 on the same ray, P′Q′ = 1/2 − 1/3 = 1/6 = 1 · 1/(2 · 3) ✓.

Try it

Where does 3 + 4i go under inversion in the unit circle?

Answer. (3 + 4i)/25.

Try it

What does inversion do to a circle through the centre of inversion?

Answer. It maps it to a line not through the centre.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. Show that inversion maps the circle |z − 1| = 1 (which passes through 0) to the line Re(z) = 1/2. Solution. A point z on the circle satisfies |z|² = 2 Re z. The image w = 1/z̄ has w̄ = 1/z, so Re w = Re(1/z̄) = Re z/|z|² = 1/2.

Check. The point 2 on the circle goes to 1/2, which lies on the line Re z = 1/2; the point 1 + i goes to (1 + i)/2, real part 1/2 ✓.

Problem p1. Inversion has radius 6 and centre O. P is 4 from O. How far is the inverse P' from O?

HintOP · OP' = r².
Answer9
Method: Rule

4 · OP' = 36, so OP' = 9.

Method: Check

4 · 9 = 36.

Source: Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 5 and Chen ch. 8

Problem p2. Inversion in a circle of radius 5 centred at the origin. Find the inverse of (6, 8), and give x + y of the inverse as a fraction.

HintInverse = (r²/|P|²)·P.
Answer7/2
Method: Scale

|P|² = 100, so the inverse is (25/100)(6, 8) = (3/2, 2), with x + y = 7/2.

Method: Distance

OP = 10 and OP' = 25/10 = 5/2 along the same ray: (5/2)(3/5, 4/5) = (3/2, 2).

Source: Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 5 and Chen ch. 8

Problem p3. Invert the line x = 2 in the unit circle centred at the origin. The image is a circle through O. Find its radius, as a fraction.

HintThe foot (2,0) inverts to (1/2, 0), the far point of the circle.
Answer1/4
Method: Foot point

(2,0) is the closest point of the line to O. Its inverse is (1/2, 0), which is the diameter's far end, so the diameter is 1/2 and the radius is 1/4.

Method: Check

The circle (x − 1/4)² + y² = 1/16 passes through O and (1/2, 0), consistent with radius 1/4.

Source: Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 5 and Chen ch. 8

Problem p4. Invert in the unit circle about the origin the circle with centre (3, 0) and radius 1. Find the radius of the image, as a fraction.

HintInvert the points (2,0) and (4,0).
Answer1/8
Method: Diameter

The extreme points (2,0) and (4,0) invert to (1/2, 0) and (1/4, 0). They are the ends of a diameter of the image, so the radius is (1/2 − 1/4)/2 = 1/8.

Method: Formula

For a circle with centre at distance d and radius ρ, the image radius is r²ρ/|d² − ρ²| = 1/8.

Source: Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 5 and Chen ch. 8

Problem p5. Inversion has radius 10. P and Q are 5 and 20 from O, and PQ = 18. Find the distance P'Q'.

HintP'Q' = r²·PQ/(OP·OQ).
Answer18
Method: Formula

P'Q' = 100 · 18/(5 · 20) = 18.

Method: Similar triangles

OP · OP' = OQ · OQ' means triangles OPQ and OQ'P' are similar, giving the same ratio.

Source: Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 5 and Chen ch. 8

Problem p6. ABCD is a rectangle with AB = 3 and BC = 4. Find AC · BD (Ptolemy).

HintA rectangle is a cyclic quadrilateral.
Answer25
Method: Ptolemy

AC · BD = AB · CD + BC · DA = 9 + 16 = 25.

Method: Diagonals

Each diagonal is 5, so AC · BD = 25.

Source: Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 5 and Chen ch. 8

Problem p7. Inversion has radius 2. A point is 8 from O. If P' is its inverse, find OP + OP' as a fraction.

HintOP' = r²/OP.
Answer17/2
Method: Compute

OP' = 4/8 = 1/2, so the sum is 8 + 1/2 = 17/2.

Method: Check

8 · 1/2 = 4 = r².

Source: Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 5 and Chen ch. 8

Problem p8. Inversion has radius 10. A circle through O has diameter 10, so its centre is 5 from O. It inverts to a line. How far is that line from O?

HintThe far end of the diameter inverts to the nearest point of the line.
Answer10
Method: Far end

The diameter's far end is 10 from O and inverts to 100/10 = 10 from O. The line is perpendicular to the diameter there, so its distance is 10.

Method: Check

The circle through O and a line at distance d are inverses when d = r²/diameter = 100/10.

Source: Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 5 and Chen ch. 8

Problem p9. Inversion has radius 4. A circle not through O has centre 5 from O and radius 3. Find the radius of its image.

HintInvert the two extreme points on the line through O.
Answer3
Method: Extremes

The circle meets that line at distances 2 and 8 from O. They invert to 16/2 = 8 and 16/8 = 2, so the image diameter is 6 and the radius is 3.

Method: Formula

r²ρ/|d² − ρ²| = 16 · 3/(25 − 9) = 3.

Source: Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 5 and Chen ch. 8

Answer25

Problem p15. Under an inversion of radius 4, a circle through O has diameter 8. The image is a line. How far is it from O?

HintReverse the previous idea.
Answer2
Method: Formula

The far point of the circle is at distance 8 and maps to distance 16/8 = 2, which is the foot of the line.

Method: Distance

The line is at distance 2 from O.

Problem p16. With r² = 12, points P and Q lie on one ray from O at distances 2 and 3. How far apart are their images?

HintImages are at 12/d.
Answer2
Method: Compute

P′ is at 6 and Q′ at 4 on the same ray, so the distance is 2.

Method: Order

Inversion reverses the order along the ray.

Problem x19. Two similar triangles have corresponding sides in the ratio 3 : 7. The smaller has area 18. Find the larger area.

HintAreas scale as the square of the ratio.
Answer98
Method: Area ratio

The areas are in the ratio 9 : 49, so the larger area is 18 · 49/9 = 98.

Method: Scale factor

The linear factor is 7/3, so the area factor is 49/9; 18 · 49/9 = 98.
