Reading

1. Linear equations

To solve a linear equation, collect the unknown on one side by performing the same operation on both sides. 3(x − 2) + 4 = 2x + 5 gives 3x − 2 = 2x + 5, so x = 7. With fractions, multiply every term by the least common denominator. A linear equation can have one solution, none (a contradiction like 0 = 3) or infinitely many (an identity like 0 = 0).

Systems. Two equations in two unknowns can be solved by substitution (solve one for a variable and substitute) or elimination (add multiples to cancel one variable). For x + y = 20 and 5x + 10y = 135 (nickels and dimes): multiply the first by 5: 5x + 5y = 100, subtract: 5y = 35, y = 7, x = 13.

Try it

Solve (x + 1)/2 − (x − 3)/3 = 2.

Answer. 3(x + 1) − 2(x − 3) = 12, so x + 9 = 12 and x = 3.

2. Translating words into equations

Define the unknown first (with units!), write what is known in terms of it, then form an equation from a statement of equality. Ages: a father is 3 times as old as his son; in 10 years he will be twice as old: 3s + 10 = 2(s + 10), so s = 10. Consecutive integers: n + (n + 1) = 2023 gives n = 1011.

Mixtures: how much 20% solution must be mixed with 50% solution to make 10 L of 35%? 0.2x + 0.5(10 − x) = 3.5 gives x = 5. Percent: a price up 20% then down 20% ends at 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96, i.e. 96% of the original.

Try it

The sum of three consecutive even numbers is 78. Find them.

Answer. n + (n + 2) + (n + 4) = 78, so n = 24: 24, 26, 28.

3. Rates: distance, work, current

Distance = rate × time. Two trains 300 miles apart travel toward each other at 50 and 70 mph; they meet after 300/120 = 2.5 hours. Average speed is total distance over total time, not the average of speeds: driving a distance at 60 mph and back at 40 mph gives 2/(1/60 + 1/40) = 48 mph.

Work rates add. If A does a job in 6 hours and B in 3, together they do 1/6 + 1/3 = 1/2 of the job per hour, so 2 hours. Currents. A boat travelling 12 mph in still water: downstream speed 12 + c, upstream 12 − c. If a downstream trip takes 3 hours and the same distance upstream takes 5 hours, then 3(12 + c) = 5(12 − c), so c = 3 mph.

Try it

A can paint a room in 4 hours, B in 6. How long together?

Answer. 1/4 + 1/6 = 5/12 per hour, so 12/5 = 2.4 hours.

4. A longer problem

Problem. Two cars start 280 miles apart. Car A leaves at 9:00 at 50 mph toward B; car B leaves at 10:00 at 60 mph toward A. When and where do they meet?

Solution. At 10:00 car A has gone 50 miles, so the gap is 230. Closing speed 110: 230/110 = 23/11 hours ≈ 2 h 5.5 min after 10:00, i.e. about 12:05. Car A has travelled 50 + 50 · 23/11 = 50 · 34/11 ≈ 154.5 miles.

Reflection. The head start was handled by recomputing the gap at the moment the second car starts, a standard simplification.

5. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. (1) Not defining the unknown. (2) Averaging speeds. (3) Treating percent changes as additive. (4) Forgetting to answer the question asked (e.g. giving x when the question wants x + 2).

Checks. Substitute the answer into the words of the problem.

How to practise. Write 'let x = …' every time. In Unlimited practice, rotate the word-problem families.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Solving. 3x + 7 = 22 gives x = 5. Consecutive integers. If three consecutive integers sum to 63, they are 20, 21, 22 (the middle one is 63/3 = 21). Work. If A finishes a job in 6 hours and B in 3 hours, together they do 1/6 + 1/3 = 1/2 of the job per hour and finish in 2 hours.

Mixtures. To make a 30% solution from 10 litres of a 20% solution and x litres of a 50% solution: 0.2 · 10 + 0.5x = 0.3(10 + x), so 2 + 0.5x = 3 + 0.3x and x = 5. Average speed. Driving a round trip at 60 and 40 km/h gives an average of 2 · 60 · 40/100 = 48 km/h (the harmonic mean), not 50.

Try it

A can paint a room in 4 hours and B in 12 hours. How long together?

Answer. 1/4 + 1/12 = 1/3 per hour, so 3 hours.

Try it

Two numbers sum to 30 and differ by 8. What are they?

Answer. 19 and 11.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. A train leaves at 9:00 going 60 km/h. A second train leaves the same station at 10:00 on the same track going 90 km/h. When does the second catch up? Solution. At 10:00 the first train is 60 km ahead; the second gains 30 km/h, so it needs 2 hours: it catches up at 12:00, 180 km from the station.

Check. First train: 3 hours × 60 = 180 km. Second train: 2 hours × 90 = 180 km. ✓

Problem p1. Solve 2x + 3y = 12 and x − y = 1. Give x, y.

HintSubstitute x = y + 1.
Answer3, 2
Method: Substitution

x = y + 1, so 2y + 2 + 3y = 12, 5y = 10, y = 2, x = 3.

Method: Elimination

Multiply the second equation by 3 and add: 2x + 3y + 3x − 3y = 12 + 3, so 5x = 15, x = 3, then y = 2.

Problem p4. How many litres of a 10% salt solution must be added to 10 litres of a 40% solution to make a 20% solution?

HintSalt amounts add.
Answer20
Method: Salt balance

0.1x + 4 = 0.2(x + 10) gives 0.1x + 4 = 0.2x + 2, so x = 20.

Method: Ratio

The final 20% is 20 points from 40% and 10 points from 10%, so the amounts are in ratio 1 : 2 (40% : 10%), giving 10 L : 20 L.

Problem p10. Find k so that the system x + 2y = 5 and 3x + ky = 7 has no solution.

Answer6
Method: Slopes

Problem x22. Find the sum of all real solutions of |2x − 9| = 5.

HintSplit into two cases.
Answer9
Method: Cases

2x − 9 = 5 gives x = 7, and 2x − 9 = −5 gives x = 2. Sum 9.

Method: Symmetry

The solutions are symmetric about the point where 2x − 9 = 0, i.e. x = 9/2, so their sum is 9.

Source: Rusczyk, Art of Problem Solving Vol. 1, ch. 3
