Reading

1. Tangent lengths and excircles

In a triangle with semiperimeter s the tangent length from vertex A to the incircle is s − a. The excircle opposite A has radius r_a = K/(s − a). For the 13-14-15 triangle s = 21, K = 84: the tangent from A is 8 and r_a = 84/8 = 21/2.

Try it

Tangent length from B in the 13-14-15 triangle (side CA = 15 opposite B)?

Answer. s − b = 6.

Try it

r_c for the 13-14-15 triangle, c = 15?

Answer. 84/6 = 14.

2. Centres and distances

Orthocentre. AH = 2R|cos A| and OM = R|cos A| (so AH = 2·OM). Euler. OI² = R² − 2Rr: for 13-14-15 this is (65/8)² − 2 · (65/8) · 4 = 65/64. The nine-point circle has radius R/2. Medians. mₐ² = (2b² + 2c² − a²)/4. Brahmagupta. K = √((s − a)(s − b)(s − c)(s − d)) for a cyclic quadrilateral.

Try it

AH for a triangle with R = 5 and A = 60°?

Answer. 2 · 5 · ½ = 5.

Try it

Radius of the nine-point circle when R = 10?

Answer. 5.

3. A worked problem

Problem. Two circles with radii 13 and 15 have centres 14 apart. How long is their common chord? Solution. The triangle with sides 13, 14, 15 has area 84; its altitude to the side of length 14 is 12, so the chord has length 24.

Check. 4K/d = 336/14 = 24 ✓.

4. In the books

What the Lemmas file is. The “Lemmas in Olympiad Geometry” PDF is an 8-page list of lemmas by Navneel Singhal, not the book by Andreescu and others. Its lemmas include: the circle with diameter AH, the circumcircle of BHC and the median AM are concurrent (lemma 1); OI is the Euler line of the contact triangle (lemma 4); the medial triangle and the Nagel-point triangle share an incircle (lemma 14). It states them without proofs.

Kedlaya. 6.2 radical axis (the common chord of two circles lies on it), 7.2 incenter and excenters, 7.3 circumcenter and orthocenter. IMO Compendium 2.3.1 lists the triangle formulas (Euler's OI² = R² − 2Rr and the distances from the orthocenter).

Honest note. The numeric formulas here (tangent lengths s − a, r_a = K/(s − a), AH = 2R cos A, OI²) are standard and appear in the sources above in some form; the Singhal lemmas are not used directly in the problems.

Problem p1. In triangle ABC with BC = 13, CA = 14, AB = 15 the incircle touches BC at D. Find the tangent length AE from A to the incircle (E on AC). (fraction)

HintThe tangent length from A is s − a.
Answer8
Method: Tangent lengths

With s = 21 the tangent from A has length s − a = 8.

Method: Equations

x + y = 15, y + z = 13, z + x = 14 for the three tangent lengths; the tangent from A is 8.

Problem p4. Triangle ABC has sides BC = 13, CA = 14, AB = 15. Find the radius r_a of the excircle opposite A. (fraction)

Hintr_a = K/(s − a).
Answer21/2
Method: Area formula

K = 84, s − a = 8, so r_a = 21/2.

Method: Area decomposition

K = (r_a/2)(b + c − a) = r_a(s − a), giving r_a = 21/2.

Problem p7. Triangle ABC has BC = 12, CA = 17, AB = 19. Find the length of the median from A. (fraction)

HintApollonius: 4mₐ² = 2b² + 2c² − a².
Answer17
Method: Apollonius

mₐ² = (2·289 + 2·361 − 144)/4, so mₐ = 17.

Method: Parallelogram

Complete ABDC to a parallelogram; the diagonals satisfy AD² + BC² = 2AB² + 2AC², and mₐ = AD/2 = 17.

Problem p10. In triangle ABC with BC = 13, CA = 14, AB = 15, H is the orthocentre. Find AH. (fraction)

HintAH = 2R|cos A|.
Answer39/4
Method: Formula

R = 65/8 and cos A = 3/5, so AH = 39/4.

Method: Coordinates

Place BC on the x-axis; H lies on the altitude from A at height x_A(BC − x_A)/y_A; AH = 39/4.

Problem p16. Triangle ABC has sides 13, 14, 15 with circumradius R and inradius r. Find OI², where O and I are the circumcentre and incentre. (fraction)

HintEuler: OI² = R² − 2Rr.
Answer65/64
Method: Euler's formula

R = 65/8 and r = 4, so OI² = R² − 2Rr = 65/64.

Method: Power of a point

The power of I with respect to the circumcircle is R² − OI² = 2Rr, so OI² = 65/64.

Problem p19. A cyclic quadrilateral has sides 7, 24, 15, 20 in order. Find its area.

HintBrahmagupta: K = √((s − a)(s − b)(s − c)(s − d)).

Method: Brahmagupta

s = 33, so K = √(26·9·18·13) = 234.

The diagonal is a diameter and the quadrilateral is two right triangles: ½·7·24 + ½·15·20 = 234.

Problem p22. Two circles of radii 13 and 15 have centres 14 apart and meet at two points. Find the length of their common chord. (fraction)

HintThe common chord is twice the altitude from an intersection point onto the line of centres in the triangle with sides 13, 15, 14.
Answer24
Method: Triangle altitude

The triangle of the two centres and one intersection point has area 84; the altitude to side 14 is 2K/14, so the chord is 4K/14 = 24.

Method: Radical axis

The chord lies on the radical axis, perpendicular to the line of centres; solving for the foot gives half-chord 12.

Source: Technique from Singhal's note, Kedlaya ch. 6–7 and the IMO Compendium §2.3 (original problem)
