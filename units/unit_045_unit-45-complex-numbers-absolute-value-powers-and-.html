Reading

1. The complex plane

A complex number is a + bi with i² = −1. We plot it at (a, b). The conjugate of z = a + bi is a − bi, the modulus (absolute value) is |z| = √(a² + b²), and z · z̄ = |z|². For z = 3 + 4i, |z| = 5 and z z̄ = 25. Division uses the conjugate: (3 + 4i)/(1 − 2i) = (3 + 4i)(1 + 2i)/5 = (−5 + 10i)/5 = −1 + 2i. Moduli multiply: |z₁z₂| = |z₁||z₂|, so |(1 + i)⁸| = (√2)⁸ = 16, and indeed (1 + i)⁸ = 16.

Powers of i repeat with period 4: i, −1, −i, 1. Hence i²⁰²³ = i³ = −i (2023 = 4 · 505 + 3), and the sum 1 + i + i² + … + i²⁰²³ over 2024 terms is 0, because every block of four sums to 0.

Quadratics. x² + 2x + 5 = 0 has roots −1 ± 2i by the quadratic formula. Complex roots of real polynomials come in conjugate pairs.

Try it

Compute (2 + i)² and |2 + i|.

Answer. (2 + i)² = 4 + 4i − 1 = 3 + 4i; |2 + i| = √5.

2. Polar form, De Moivre and roots of unity

Write z = r(cos θ + i sin θ) = r cis θ, with r = |z| and θ the argument. Multiplying complex numbers multiplies the moduli and adds the arguments; so powers obey De Moivre's theorem: (r cis θ)ⁿ = rⁿ cis nθ. The number −1 + i√3 has modulus 2 and argument 120°; (1 + i)/(1 − i) = i since the arguments are 45° and −45°, giving 90°.

Roots of unity. The solutions of zⁿ = 1 are cis(2πk/n) for k = 0, …, n − 1, which are the vertices of a regular n-gon on the unit circle. Their sum is 0 for n ≥ 2 (checked numerically for n = 5), and their product is (−1)ⁿ⁺¹. The cube roots of unity are 1, ω, ω² with ω = cis 120°, satisfying 1 + ω + ω² = 0.

Taking roots. To solve z² = i, write i = cis 90°: z = ±cis 45° = ±(1 + i)/√2. For z³ = 8 we get z = 2 cis(120°k): 2, −1 + i√3, −1 − i√3.

Try it

Find all solutions of z⁴ = 1.

Answer. 1, i, −1, −i.

3. A longer problem

Problem. Let ω = cis 72° be a primitive 5th root of unity. Show that (1 − ω)(1 − ω²)(1 − ω³)(1 − ω⁴) = 5 and find |1 − ω|.

Solution. The polynomial (zⁿ − 1)/(z − 1) = 1 + z + z² + z³ + z⁴ = Π(z − ωᵏ) for k = 1, …, 4. Setting z = 1 gives 5 = Π(1 − ωᵏ). Also |1 − ω| = 2 sin 36° ≈ 1.1756, the side of a regular pentagon inscribed in the unit circle.

Reflection. Factoring zⁿ − 1 over its roots and evaluating at a convenient point is the standard trick.

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. (1) Forgetting that i² = −1 when multiplying. (2) Dividing without the conjugate. (3) Adding arguments in degrees and radians together. (4) Missing roots in zⁿ = c (there are always n).

Checks. |z₁z₂| = |z₁||z₂| should hold; roots of unity should sum to zero.

How to practise. Plot every number. In Unlimited practice, rotate the complex arithmetic and roots-of-unity families.

More worked examples: Worked examples

|3 − 4i| = 5 and (2 + i)(2 − i) = 5 = |2 + i|²: multiplying by the conjugate gives the squared modulus. For z = 1 + i = √2 e^{iπ/4}, z⁸ = (√2)⁸e^{2πi} = 16. If |z| = 1 then 1/z = z̄. The three cube roots of unity add to zero, and ∏(x − ωᵏ) = xⁿ − 1 for the n-th roots of unity ωᵏ.

Try it

Compute (1 − i)⁴.

Answer. (1 − i)² = −2i, so (−2i)² = −4.

Try it

How many solutions has z⁶ = 1 and what is their sum?

Answer. Six solutions; the sum is 0.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. Find all complex z with z³ = −8. Solution. −8 = 8e^{iπ}, so z = 2e^{i(π + 2πk)/3}: 2e^{iπ/3} = 1 + i√3, 2e^{iπ} = −2, and 2e^{5iπ/3} = 1 − i√3. Check: (−2)³ = −8 ✓ and |1 + i√3| = 2.

Reflection. The roots lie on a circle of radius 2 at 120° spacing.

Problem x22. What is the remainder when 6^45 is divided by 13?

HintFind the pattern of remainders of successive powers.
Answer5
Method: Cycle of remainders

The remainders of 6, 6², 6³, … are 6, 10, 8, 9, 2, 12, 7, 3, 5, 4, 11, 1, and then they repeat from position 1 with period 12. Exponent 45 lands on 5.

Method: Repeated squaring

Write 45 = 101101 in binary. The squares 6^(2^i) mod 13 are 6, 10, 9, 3, 9, 3; multiplying the ones for the 1-bits gives 5.

Source: Rusczyk, Art of Problem Solving Vol. 2, ch. 9
