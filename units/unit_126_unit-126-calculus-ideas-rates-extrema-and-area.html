1. Rates of change

If a quantity y depends on time t, dy/dt is its rate of change. In related-rates problems several quantities are linked by an equation; differentiate that equation with respect to t.

Worked example. A 13-foot ladder leans against a wall; the foot is 5 ft from the wall and slides away at 2 ft/s. With x the foot's distance and y the height, x² + y² = 169 so x dx/dt + y dy/dt = 0. At x = 5, y = 12: 5 × 2 + 12 dy/dt = 0, so dy/dt = −5/6 ft/s: the top is dropping slowly.

Average and instantaneous. Average rate = (f(b) − f(a))/(b − a); instantaneous = f′(t).

2. Extrema

To maximise or minimise a quantity, express it as a function of one variable, set the derivative to 0, and check endpoints.

Worked example (open box). Cut squares of side x from the corners of a 12 × 12 sheet and fold up the sides: V(x) = x(12 − 2x)². Then V′ = (12 − 2x)² − 4x(12 − 2x) = (12 − 2x)(12 − 6x), zero at x = 2 (x = 6 gives volume 0). V(2) = 2 × 8² = 128, the maximum.

Check. V(1) = 100 and V(3) = 108 are both smaller than 128 ✓.

Fences and cans. A can with volume fixed has least surface area when its height equals its diameter.

3. Area and accumulation

The total change of a quantity equals the integral of its rate. Distance = ∫ speed dt; area under y = f(x) is ∫ f dx.

Worked example. The area under y = x² from 0 to 3 is [x³/3]₀³ = 9. Example. A car's speed (in m/s) is v(t) = 2t for 0 ≤ t ≤ 5; the distance is ∫₀⁵ 2t dt = 25 m.

Average value. The average of f on [a, b] is (1/(b − a))∫ f. For x² on [0, 3] the average is 9/3 = 3.

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. Substituting numbers before differentiating (in related rates). Forgetting units. Forgetting to check that the critical point is a maximum, not a minimum.

Checks. Compare neighbouring values.

Practice. Write the relation between variables, differentiate, and only then substitute.

Worked examples with Try-its: Rates

A derivative is an instantaneous rate of change. If position is s = t² + t, the velocity is 2t + 1, which equals 7 at t = 3. Related rates. If a circle's radius grows at 2 cm/s, its area A = πr² grows at dA/dt = 2πr · dr/dt = 20π cm²/s when r = 5.

Try it

A square's side grows at 3 cm/s. How fast is its area growing when the side is 10?

Answer. 2 · 10 · 3 = 60 cm²/s.

Worked examples with Try-its: Extrema and area

For a rectangle of perimeter 40 the area x(20 − x) is maximised at x = 10 (derivative 20 − 2x). The area under a curve is an integral: ∫₀^π sin x dx = 2. The mean value of f on [a, b] is the integral divided by (b − a).

Try it

Find the area under y = x² from 0 to 3.

Answer. 9.

Worked examples with Try-its: A longer problem

Problem. A ball is thrown so that its height is h(t) = 20t − 5t². Find the maximum height and when the ball lands. Solution. h′ = 20 − 10t = 0 at t = 2, h(2) = 20. It lands when 20t − 5t² = 0, that is t = 4.

Reflection. The vertex of the parabola is the time-halfway point of the flight.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Rates. If the height of a balloon is h = t³ metres, its speed at t = 2 is 3t² = 12 m/s. Area. ∫₁² 3x² dx = 2³ − 1³ = 7. Extrema. The rectangle of fixed perimeter 40 with greatest area is the 10 × 10 square.

Try it

Find the average value of x² on [0, 3].

Answer. (1/3) · 9 = 3.

Try it

Water leaves a tank at a rate of 5 litres per minute for 8 minutes. How much leaves?

Answer. 40 litres.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. A ladder 10 m long leans against a wall and its foot slides away at 1 m/s. How fast is the top sliding down when the foot is 6 m from the wall? Solution. x² + y² = 100, so x x′ + y y′ = 0. With x = 6, y = 8 and x′ = 1: 6 + 8y′ = 0 and y′ = −3/4 m/s.

Check. The top moves down at 0.75 m/s.

HintPower rule: the derivative of xⁿ is n xⁿ⁻¹.
Answer21
Method: Power rule

(f(2 + h) − f(2))/h for h = 0.001 is 21.012, which is close to the exact value 21.

Problem p4. Find the minimum value of x^2 − 6x + 5.

HintSet the derivative to zero.
Answer-4
Method: Derivative

f′(x) = 2x − 6 = 0 gives x = 3, and f = -4 there. Since the parabola opens upward this is the minimum.

Method: Completing the square

x^2 − 6x + 5 = (x − 3)² − 4, so the minimum is -4.

Problem p6. Find the maximum value of f(x) = x^3 − 12x on the interval from -3 to 3.

HintCompare the endpoints with the critical points.
Answer16
Method: Critical points

f′(x) = 3x^2 − 12 = 0 gives x = -2, 2. Comparing f at -3, 3, -2, 2 gives 9, -9, 16, -16, so the largest is 16.

Method: Table

Evaluating f at every integer from -3 to 3 gives 9, 16, 11, 0, -11, -16, -9; the maximum is 16.

Problem p14. A rectangle has perimeter 40. What is its largest possible area?

HintWrite the area as one variable and differentiate.
Answer100
Method: Derivative

With width w the area is w(20 − w) = 20 w − w². The derivative 20 − 2w = 0 at w = 10, which is a square, with area 100.

Method: AM-GM

w + h = 20, so wh ≤ ((w + h)/2)² = 10² = 100, with equality when w = h.

Source: Zeitz ch. 9

Problem p16. Find the limit as x → 3 of (x² − 9)/(x − 3).

HintFactor the numerator.
Answer6
Method: Factor

(x − 3)(x + 3)/(x − 3) = x + 3, which tends to 6.

Method: Derivative

This is the derivative of x² at 3, which is 2 · 3 = 6.

Problem p19. Find the y-intercept of the tangent line to y = x^3 at x = 2.

HintTangent line: y − f(a) = f′(a)(x − a).
Answer-16
Method: Tangent

f(2) = 8 and f′(2) = 12, so y = 8 + 12(x − 2); at x = 0 this gives -16.

Method: Linear fit

The line passes through (2, 8) with slope 12, so b = y − m·x = 8 − 12 · 2 = -16.

Source: Zeitz ch. 9
