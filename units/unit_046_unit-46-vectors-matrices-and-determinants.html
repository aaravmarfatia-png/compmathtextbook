Reading

1. Vectors

A vector has a magnitude and direction; in coordinates it is a list like (1, 2, 3). Add componentwise. The dot product is a · b = Σaᵢbᵢ: (1, 2, 3) · (4, 5, 6) = 4 + 10 + 18 = 32, and it equals |a||b| cos θ, so two vectors are perpendicular exactly when their dot product is 0. The length of (2, 3, 6) is √49 = 7. The cross product in three dimensions gives a vector perpendicular to both: (1, 2, 3) × (4, 5, 6) = (−3, 6, −3).

Area from vectors. The area of the triangle with vertices (0, 0), (3, 1), (1, 4) is ½|3 · 4 − 1 · 1| = 5.5; the parallelogram has area |ad − bc| for the vectors (a, b) and (c, d). The same quantity is a 2 × 2 determinant.

Try it

Find the angle between (1, 0) and (1, 1).

Answer. cos θ = 1/√2, so θ = 45°.

2. Matrices

A matrix is a rectangular array. Multiplication is row-by-column: [[1, 2], [3, 4]] × [[0, 1], [1, 0]] = [[2, 1], [4, 3]], which swaps the columns, while the product in the other order is [[3, 4], [1, 2]] (swaps the rows). Matrix multiplication is not commutative. The identity matrix leaves any matrix unchanged. A matrix with non-zero determinant has an inverse.

Linear maps. The matrix [[0, −1], [1, 0]] rotates the plane by 90° counterclockwise; applied twice it gives −I. Systems of equations can be written as Ax = b: 2x + y = 5, x + 3y = 10 has solution (1, 3). Powers of [[1, 1], [1, 0]] produce Fibonacci numbers: the tenth power has 55 in the corner.

Try it

Compute [[1, 2], [0, 1]]².

Answer. [[1, 4], [0, 1]].

3. Determinants

For a 2 × 2 matrix [[a, b], [c, d]] the determinant is ad − bc: for [[1, 2], [3, 4]] it is −2. It gives the signed area scale factor of the transformation, and the matrix is invertible exactly when the determinant is not zero. Determinants multiply: det(AB) = det A · det B (checked on the example). For a 3 × 3 determinant expand along a row: det[[1, 2, 3], [4, 5, 6], [7, 8, 10]] = 1(50 − 48) − 2(40 − 42) + 3(32 − 35) = 2 + 4 − 9 = −3.

Properties. Swapping two rows changes the sign; a row of zeros or two equal rows gives 0; adding a multiple of one row to another changes nothing. Use row operations to simplify before expanding.

Try it

Find det [[2, 3], [4, 5]].

Answer. 10 − 12 = −2.

4. A longer problem

Problem. Find the area of the parallelogram spanned by (3, 1) and (1, 4), the inverse of [[2, 1], [1, 3]], and solve 2x + y = 5, x + 3y = 10 using it.

Solution. Area = |3 · 4 − 1 · 1| = 11. The determinant of [[2, 1], [1, 3]] is 5 and the inverse is (1/5)[[3, −1], [−1, 2]]. Multiplying by (5, 10): (1/5)(15 − 10, −5 + 20) = (1, 3).

Reflection. The inverse formula for a 2 × 2 matrix, (1/det)[[d, −b], [−c, a]], is worth memorising.

5. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. (1) Treating AB = BA. (2) Using the wrong signs in the cofactor expansion. (3) Forgetting that the inverse needs a non-zero determinant. (4) Mixing up rows and columns in a product.

Checks. Multiply a matrix by its claimed inverse to get the identity. Check dimensions.

How to practise. Do every determinant two ways. In Unlimited practice, rotate the matrix and determinant families.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Dot product. (1, 2) · (3, 4) = 11, and the angle between (1, 0) and (1, 1) satisfies cos θ = 1/√2, so θ = 45°. Area. The parallelogram spanned by (3, 1) and (1, 2) has area |3 · 2 − 1 · 1| = 5. Matrix product. [[1, 2], [3, 4]] × [[0, 1], [1, 0]] = [[2, 1], [4, 3]]: multiplying by the swap matrix on the right swaps the columns. Inverse. [[2, 1], [1, 1]] has determinant 1 and inverse [[1, −1], [−1, 2]].

Try it

Find the area of the triangle with vertices (0, 0), (5, 1), (2, 4).

Answer. ½|5 · 4 − 1 · 2| = 9.

Try it

Are the vectors (2, 3) and (−3, 2) perpendicular?

Answer. Dot product −6 + 6 = 0, so yes.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. Find the matrix A with A × [[1, 2], [3, 4]] = [[5, 6], [7, 8]]. Solution. A = [[5, 6], [7, 8]] × [[1, 2], [3, 4]]⁻¹. The determinant of the second matrix is −2 and its inverse is [[−2, 1], [3/2, −1/2]], so A = [[5·(−2) + 6·(3/2), 5·1 + 6·(−1/2)], [7·(−2) + 8·(3/2), 7·1 + 8·(−1/2)]] = [[−1, 2], [−2, 3]]. Check: [[−1, 2], [−2, 3]] × [[1, 2], [3, 4]] = [[5, 6], [7, 8]] ✓.

Reflection. Multiply by the inverse on the same side as the unknown.
