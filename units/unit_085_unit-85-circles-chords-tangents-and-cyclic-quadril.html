Reading

1. Tangential quadrilaterals and Pitot's theorem

A quadrilateral with an inscribed circle (tangent to all four sides) satisfies Pitot's theorem: the sums of opposite sides are equal, a + c = b + d. The reason: the tangent lengths from each vertex are equal, so each side is a sum of two tangent lengths and both sums equal the total of all four tangent lengths. Conversely, a convex quadrilateral with a + c = b + d has an incircle.

Worked example. A tangential quadrilateral has sides 5, 6, 7 and d (in order). Then 5 + 7 = 6 + d, so d = 6. Its area is r × s, where s is the semiperimeter: s = 12.

Cyclic and tangential. A quadrilateral that is both has area √(abcd) (Brahmagupta's formula with s − a = c, etc.).

2. Chords, secants and tangents

Intersecting chords: PA · PB = PC · PD. Tangent–secant: PT² = PA · PB. Two secants from an outside point: PA · PB = PC · PD. The angle between a tangent and a chord equals the inscribed angle on the other side of the chord. The angle between two chords meeting inside the circle is half the sum of the intercepted arcs; for two secants meeting outside, half the difference.

Worked example. Two chords AB and CD meet at P inside the circle, with arcs AC = 70° and BD = 50° (the arcs opposite each other across the intersection). Then angle APC = ½(70° + 50°) = 60°.

Worked example 2. Two secants from P meet the circle at A, B and C, D with arcs BD = 100° (far) and AC = 40° (near). The angle at P is ½(100° − 40°) = 30°.

3. Cyclic quadrilateral tests

To show four points concyclic, show any one of these: opposite angles add to 180°; an exterior angle equals the opposite interior angle; two angles subtending the same segment on the same side are equal; the power-of-a-point equation PA · PB = PC · PD holds for lines meeting at P. Ptolemy's equation AC · BD = AB · CD + AD · BC also characterises cyclic quadrilaterals.

Worked example. In triangle ABC, the feet D and E of the altitudes from A and B lie on a circle with diameter AB, since angles ADB and AEB are right angles. So ABDE is cyclic, and angle CDE = angle BAC (exterior angle of the cyclic quadrilateral).

Miquel's theorem. Points D, E, F on the sides BC, CA, AB of a triangle: the circles AEF, BFD, CDE meet at one point.

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. Mixing the near and far arcs. Using the sum when it should be a difference. Forgetting that Pitot's theorem needs the sides taken in order.

Checks. Test your angle on a symmetric case.

Practice. Mark each angle with the arc it intercepts.

Worked examples with Try-its: Power of a point

If two chords AB and CD of a circle meet at P, then PA · PB = PC · PD; for chords cut into 3 and 4, and 2 and 6, 3 · 4 = 2 · 6 = 12. For a point outside, the tangent length t satisfies t² = PA · PB for any secant. A tangent from a point at distance 13 from the centre of a circle of radius 5 has length √(169 − 25) = 12.

Try it

A secant from P meets a circle at distances 4 and 9 from P. How long is the tangent from P?

Answer. √36 = 6.

Worked examples with Try-its: Cyclic quadrilaterals

A quadrilateral is cyclic exactly when opposite angles sum to 180°. Ptolemy: for a cyclic quadrilateral with sides a, b, c, d (in order) and diagonals p, q we have pq = ac + bd. For a 3 × 4 rectangle the diagonals are both 5, and 5 · 5 = 3 · 3 + 4 · 4 = 25. For two circles with centres 13 apart and radii 3 and 8, the external common tangent has length √(13² − 5²) = 12 and the internal one has length √(13² − 11²) = √48.

Try it

In cyclic quadrilateral ABCD, angle A = 80° and angle B = 95°. Find angles C and D.

Answer. C = 180° − 80° = 100° and D = 180° − 95° = 85°.

Worked examples with Try-its: A longer problem

Problem. In a circle, chords AB and CD intersect at P with AP = 2, PB = 6 and CP = 3. Find PD and the product of the two chords' lengths. Solution. PD = 2 · 6/3 = 4, so CD = 7 and AB = 8, product 56.

Reflection. The product of the two segments is the same for every chord through P.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Inscribed angle. A chord that subtends a central angle of 100° subtends an inscribed angle of 50° on the major arc and 130° on the minor arc. Tangent length. A secant from P meeting the circle at distances 3 and 12 has tangent length √36 = 6. Incircle. A right triangle with sides 6, 8, 10 has inradius (6 + 8 − 10)/2 = 2.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. Chord AB of length 8 is 3 units from the centre of a circle. Find the radius. Solution. The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord, so r² = 4² + 3² = 25 and r = 5.

Practice problems (25)

Problem p1. Chords AB and CD of a circle meet at P inside it. PA = 4, PB = 6, PC = 3. Find PD.

HintPA · PB = PC · PD.
Answer8
Method: Power of a point

4 · 6 = 3 · PD, so PD = 8.

Method: Similar triangles

Triangles PAC and PDB are similar (equal inscribed angles), so PA/PD = PC/PB, giving PD = 4 · 6/3 = 8.

Source: Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 2 and Chen ch. 1

Problem p2. From a point P outside a circle, a secant meets the circle at A (nearer) and B with PA = 4 and AB = 5. Find the tangent length PT.

HintPT² = PA · PB.
Answer6
Method: Power

PB = 9, so PT² = 36 and PT = 6.

Method: Check

Triangles PTA and PBT are similar, giving PT/PB = PA/PT, the same relation.

Source: Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 2 and Chen ch. 1

Problem p3. In a cyclic quadrilateral ABCD angle A = 70°. Find angle C in degrees.

HintOpposite angles are supplementary.
Answer110
Method: Theorem

A + C = 180°, so C = 110°.

Method: Arcs

Angle A subtends arc BCD of 140° and angle C subtends the remaining arc of 220°, giving 110°.

Source: Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 2 and Chen ch. 1

Problem p4. An isosceles trapezoid has parallel sides 14 and 4 and legs 13. Find the length of a diagonal.

HintIt is cyclic, so use Ptolemy.
Answer15
Method: Ptolemy

d · d = 14 · 4 + 13 · 13 = 225, so d = 15.

Method: Coordinates

The legs each stick out (14 − 4)/2 = 5, so the height is √(169 − 25) = 12. A diagonal spans horizontally 5 + 4 = 9 and vertically 12, so d = √(81 + 144) = 15.

Source: Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 2 and Chen ch. 1

Problem p5. A right triangle with legs 6 and 8 is inscribed in a circle. Find the circle's radius.

HintThe hypotenuse is a diameter.
Answer5
Method: Thales

The hypotenuse is 10 and is a diameter, so R = 5.

Method: Formula

R = abc/(4K) = 6 · 8 · 10/(4 · 24) = 5.

Source: Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 2 and Chen ch. 1

Problem p6. In a circle of radius 5, a chord is 3 from the centre. Find the chord's length.

HintRight triangle with half-chord.
Answer8
Method: Pythagoras

The half-chord is √(25 − 9) = 4, so the chord is 8.

Method: Power

The midpoint M of the chord is at distance 3 from the centre, and the half-chord squared equals 5² − 3² = 16.
