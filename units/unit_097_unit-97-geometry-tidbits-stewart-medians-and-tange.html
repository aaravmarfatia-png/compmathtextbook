1. Medians and Apollonius's theorem

The length of the median from A to the midpoint D of BC is given by Apollonius's theorem: AB² + AC² = 2(AD² + BD²), i.e. m_a² = (2b² + 2c² − a²)/4. In the 13-14-15 triangle with the median to the side 14 (BD = 7): 169 + 225 = 394 = 2(AD² + 49), so AD² = 148 ✓ (matches Unit 50).

Worked example. A right triangle has legs 6 and 8 and hypotenuse 10. The median to the hypotenuse is 5. By Apollonius: 36 + 64 = 100 = 2(m² + 25), m² = 25 ✓. The median to the side 6 has m² = (2 × 64 + 2 × 100 − 36)/4 = 292/4 = 73.

The three medians divide the triangle into six triangles of equal area and meet at the centroid, which divides each in the ratio 2 : 1.

2. Tangent lengths and excircles

For a triangle with sides a, b, c and semiperimeter s, the incircle touches the sides so that the tangent length from vertex A is s − a, from B is s − b, from C is s − c. The excircle opposite A touches BC and the extensions of AB, AC; the tangent length from A to it is s. The radii are r = K/s and r_a = K/(s − a), where K is the area.

Worked example (13-14-15). K = 84 and s = 21. The radii: r = 4; r_a (opposite the side 14) = 84/7 = 12; r_b (opposite 15) = 84/6 = 14; r_c (opposite 13) = 84/8 = 10.5. Check the identity 1/r = 1/r_a + 1/r_b + 1/r_c: 1/12 + 1/14 + 1/10.5 = 0.0833 + 0.0714 + 0.0952 = 0.25 = 1/4 ✓.

Area as a product. K² = r · r_a · r_b · r_c: 4 × 12 × 14 × 10.5 = 7,056 = 84² ✓.

3. Other useful facts

The angle bisector length from A: d² = bc(1 − a²/(b + c)²) (Unit 50). Euler's formula OI² = R² − 2Rr relates the circumcentre and incentre. The area of a triangle with medians m_a, m_b, m_c is 4/3 the area of the triangle having sides m_a, m_b, m_c.

Worked example. For the right triangle with legs 6, 8: R = 5, r = (6 + 8 − 10)/2 = 2, so OI² = 25 − 20 = 5.

The butterfly and other lemmas. The ratio of areas of triangles that share a vertex and have bases on one line is the ratio of the bases. This single idea underlies many 'tidbits'.

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. Using the full side where half is needed (BD instead of BC). Mixing the excircle tangent length s with s − a. Forgetting that the median formulas use the opposite side.

Checks. Test your formula on a right triangle where the answer is easy.

Practice. Keep a table of a, b, c, s, K, r, R for your triangle.

Worked examples with Try-its: Stewart's theorem and medians

For a cevian of length d from A to a point on BC that splits BC into m (next to B) and n (next to C), with b = AC, c = AB, a = m + n: b²m + c²n = a(d² + mn). In the 13-14-15 triangle with BD = 6, DC = 8: 225 · 6 + 169 · 8 = 2702 = 14(d² + 48), so d² = 145 and d = √145. The median has m = n = a/2 and length m_a² = (2b² + 2c² − a²)/4; from A in 13-14-15 the square of the median is 148, so m_a = √148.

Try it

A triangle has sides 7, 8, 9. Find the median to the side of length 9.

Answer. (98 + 128 − 81)/4 = 145/4, so the median is √145/2.

Worked examples with Try-its: Tangent lengths

From the vertices of a triangle with semiperimeter s, the incircle tangent lengths are s − a, s − b, s − c. In 13-14-15, s = 21 and the lengths are 7, 6, 8 from the vertices opposite sides 14, 15, 13 respectively. Equal tangent segments from an outside point are the key fact behind many of these problems.

Try it

A triangle has sides 5, 6, 7. How long is the tangent from the vertex opposite the side 6 to the incircle?

Answer. s = 9, so s − 6 = 3.

Worked examples with Try-its: A longer problem

Problem. In the 13-14-15 triangle the cevian from A meets BC at the incircle's touch point D. Find AD. Solution. BD = s − b = 6, DC = 8, and Stewart gives AD = √145, the same cevian as above.

Reflection. The touch point is just a point with BD = s − b, so Stewart applies directly.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Isosceles median. In a 5-5-6 triangle the median to the base is √(25 − 9) = 4 (also from the formula (2 · 25 + 2 · 25 − 36)/4 = 16). In the 3-4-5 triangle the median to the hypotenuse is 5/2, from (2 · 9 + 2 · 16 − 25)/4 = 25/4. The incircle tangent lengths of the 3-4-5 triangle from the vertices are s − a, s − b, s − c with s = 6: 3, 2 and 1.

Try it

Find the median to the side of length 8 in a triangle with sides 8, 6, 10.

Answer. m² = (2 · 36 + 2 · 100 − 64)/4 = 52, so √52 = 2√13.

Try it

A triangle has sides 7, 8, 9. Find the incircle tangent length from the vertex between sides 8 and 9.

Answer. s = 12, so s − 7 = 5.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. In triangle ABC, AB = 7, AC = 9, BC = 8. A point D on BC has BD = 3. Find AD. Solution. Stewart: AC² · BD + AB² · DC = BC(AD² + BD · DC) gives 81 · 3 + 49 · 5 = 488 = 8(AD² + 15), so AD² = 61 − 15 = 46 and AD = √46.

Check. 243 + 245 = 488, and 488/8 = 61 ✓.

Problem p11. In triangle ABC, AB = 5, AC = 7 and BC = 8. D lies on BC with BD = 3. Find AD².

HintStewart's theorem.
Answer19
Method: Stewart

AD² = (AC² · BD + AB² · DC)/BC − BD · DC = (147 + 125)/8 − 15 = 34 − 15 = 19.

Put B = (0, 0) and C = (8, 0). From AB = 5 and AC = 7, A has x = (25 + 64 − 49)/16 = 5/2 and y² = 25 − 25/4 = 75/4. D = (3, 0), so AD² = 1/4 + 75/4 = 19.
