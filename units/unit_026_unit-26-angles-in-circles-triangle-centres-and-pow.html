1. Angles in a circle

Geometry in a circle rests on a few theorems about angles. The central angle is formed by two radii; the inscribed angle has its vertex on the circle. Inscribed Angle Theorem: an inscribed angle is half the central angle that cuts off the same arc. So if an arc measures 100°, the inscribed angle on it is 50°, and every inscribed angle on that arc has the same size (I confirmed this numerically with points on a unit circle).

Thales' theorem. A diameter cuts off an arc of 180°, so the inscribed angle on it is 90°: any angle subtended by a diameter is a right angle. Conversely, the circumcentre of a right triangle is the midpoint of its hypotenuse and the circumradius is half the hypotenuse; in the 5-12-13 triangle R = 6.5.

Cyclic quadrilaterals. If four points lie on a circle, opposite angles of the quadrilateral sum to 180° (the two arcs they cut off make a full circle, 360°). Four points chosen at 10°, 100°, 190°, 300° on a circle give opposite angles adding to 180° exactly. Conversely, if opposite angles of a quadrilateral add to 180°, it is cyclic. This is the main way to prove that four points lie on one circle.

Tangent–chord angle. The angle between a tangent and a chord at the point of contact equals the inscribed angle on the chord: for a chord subtending an arc of 80° the tangent–chord angle is 40°. A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.

Try it

An inscribed angle in a circle is 35°. What is the central angle on the same arc, and what is the measure of the arc?

Answer. Central angle 70°; the arc is 70°.

2. Angle chasing in practice

Method. Label every angle that you know. Use these facts in turn: angles in a triangle sum to 180°; angles on a line sum to 180°; vertical angles are equal; an inscribed angle is half its arc; angles in the same segment are equal; opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°; the tangent is perpendicular to the radius. Write the unknown angle as x and form an equation.

Example. In a circle, chords AC and BD meet at P inside, and arc AB is 70° while arc CD is 50°. The angle APB is half the sum of the two arcs it and its vertical angle subtend: (70° + 50°)/2 = 60°. For an angle formed by two secants meeting outside the circle the angle is half the difference of the arcs: with far arc 110° and near arc 30° the outside angle is 40°.

Checking. Whenever your final answer is an angle, check that it is positive and that the three angles of every triangle in your diagram sum to 180°. A symmetric diagram should give symmetric answers.

Try it

Two chords AC and BD of a circle cross at P. If arc AB = 84° and arc CD = 36°, find angle APB.

Answer. (84° + 36°)/2 = 60°.

3. Power of a point

Take a point P and a circle with centre O and radius r. Draw any line through P meeting the circle at A and B. Then the product PA · PB is the same for every such line, and equals |PO|² − r² when P is outside. This constant is the power of the point.

Three versions. (1) Intersecting chords (P inside): PA · PB = PC · PD; with PA = 3, PB = 4, PC = 2 we need PD = 6. (2) Secants (P outside): PA · PB = PC · PD, e.g. 3 · 8 = 4 · 6 = 24. (3) Tangent and secant: PT² = PA · PB; if PT = 6 and PA = 4 then PB = 9. A concrete check: take a circle of radius 3 centred at the origin and P = (5, 0). The tangent length is √(25 − 9) = 4 and the secant along the x-axis meets the circle at (3, 0) and (−3, 0), with PA · PB = 2 · 8 = 16 = 4² ✓.

Why it works. Triangles PAC and PDB are similar (they share an angle at P and have equal angles subtending arc AD) which gives PA/PD = PC/PB. The converse is also true: if PA · PB = PC · PD with A, B on one line through P and C, D on another (with P on the same side of each pair), then A, B, C, D are concyclic.

Try it

A tangent from P touches a circle at T with PT = 12. A secant from P meets the circle at A and B with PA = 8. Find PB.

Answer. PT² = PA · PB: 144 = 8 · PB, so PB = 18 and the chord AB = 10.

4. The centres of a triangle

A triangle has four classical centres. We illustrate them on the 13-14-15 triangle with vertices B = (0, 0), C = (14, 0), A = (5, 12) (so AB = 13, BC = 14, CA = 15, area 84; the coordinates reproduce all three lengths exactly).

Centroid G — where the medians meet, two-thirds of the way from each vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side; coordinates are the averages of the vertices: G = (19/3, 4). Incentre I — where the angle bisectors meet, the centre of the inscribed circle of radius r = Area/s = 84/21 = 4; here I = (6, 4). Circumcentre O — where the perpendicular bisectors meet, the centre of the circle through the vertices of radius R = abc/(4K) = 13 · 14 · 15/(4 · 84) = 65/8; here O = (7, 33/8). Orthocentre H — where the altitudes meet; here H = (5, 15/4).

Facts to remember. The altitude from A to BC is 12, and the altitude to side 15 is 2 · 84/15 = 11.2. The bisector from A meets BC at distance 14 · 13/28 = 6.5 from B (angle bisector theorem: BD/DC = AB/AC). The median from A has length² = (2 · 15² + 2 · 13² − 14²)/4 = 148, i.e. √148, matching a direct distance computation.

Euler line. The points O, G, H lie on one line and G divides OH in the ratio 1 : 2 (OG : GH = 1 : 2). In our triangle G = O + (H − O)/3 holds exactly. For a right triangle H is the right-angle vertex and O the midpoint of the hypotenuse; for an equilateral triangle all four centres coincide.

Try it

Find the inradius and circumradius of the 5-12-13 triangle.

Answer. Area 30, s = 15, r = 30/15 = 2; R = hypotenuse/2 = 6.5.

5. Tangent lengths and the incircle

The two tangent segments from a point to a circle have equal length. For the incircle of a triangle with sides a, b, c and semiperimeter s, the tangent length from vertex A is s − a, from B is s − b, from C is s − c. In the 13-14-15 triangle (a = 14 opposite A, b = 15 opposite B, c = 13 opposite C) these lengths are 7, 6, 8, and indeed 7 + 6 = 13 = c, 6 + 8 = 14 = a, 7 + 8 = 15 = b.

Excircles. The circle tangent to side a and the extensions of the other two sides has radius rₐ = K/(s − a) = 84/7 = 12; similarly r_b = 84/6 = 14 and r_c = 84/8 = 10.5. A neat relation: 1/r = 1/rₐ + 1/r_b + 1/r_c, which for this triangle reads 1/4 = 1/12 + 1/14 + 2/21 and checks exactly.

Euler's inequality. The distance between the circumcentre and the incentre satisfies OI² = R(R − 2r). Here R = 65/8 and r = 4 give OI² = (65/8)(1/8) = 65/64, and computing from coordinates gives the same value. Because OI² ≥ 0 we always have R ≥ 2r, with equality only for the equilateral triangle.

Try it

A right triangle has legs 6 and 8. What are the tangent lengths from each vertex to the incircle?

Answer. Sides 6, 8, 10; s = 12. Tangent lengths: from the right-angle vertex s − 10 = 2, from the vertex between 6 and 10: s − 8 = 4, from the vertex between 8 and 10: s − 6 = 6.

6. A longer problem

Problem. In triangle ABC with AB = 13, BC = 14, CA = 15: (a) find the inradius, circumradius and the distance from the circumcentre to side BC; (b) find the length of the tangent from A to the incircle; (c) if P lies on BC with BP = 4, find the power of P with respect to the circumcircle.

Solution. (a) r = 4, R = 65/8. The distance from O to BC is the y-coordinate of O, which is 33/8; this also follows from √(R² − 7²) = √(4225/64 − 49) = √(1089/64) = 33/8 ✓. (b) s − a = 7. (c) The power of P = (4, 0) is |PO|² − R² = (7 − 4)² + (33/8)² − (65/8)² = 9 + (1089 − 4225)/64 = 9 − 49 = −40. A negative sign means P is inside the circle, and the product of the segments of the chord through P along BC is 4 · 10 = 40 ✓ (chord BC with BP = 4 and PC = 10).

Reflection. The power of a point can be found either from the distance to the centre or from the product of the segments of any chord through the point. The two answers must agree.

7. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. (1) Using the inscribed-angle theorem with the wrong arc (the arc not containing the vertex). (2) Using the formula for an angle formed inside the circle for an angle formed outside. (3) Mixing up the incentre and the circumcentre. (4) Applying PA · PB = PC · PD to lines that do not pass through the same point P. (5) Forgetting that the power of an interior point is negative when computed as |PO|² − r².

Checks. Check every length against the triangle inequality, every angle sum against 180°, and every claimed centre against a coordinate computation if the triangle is given by coordinates.

How to practise. Draw a large, accurate diagram. Mark all known angles and lengths before solving. In Unlimited practice, rotate the circle-theorem and triangle-centre families.

Problem p1. Points A, B, C lie on a circle and arc BC (the one not containing A) measures 100°. Find angle BAC in degrees.

HintAn inscribed angle is half its arc.
Answer50
Method: Inscribed angle theorem

The inscribed angle equals half the arc it cuts off: 100° / 2 = 50°.

Method: Central angle

The central angle BOC is 100°. The angle at the circle is half the angle at the centre on the same arc, again 50°.

Source: Chen, Euclidean Geometry in Mathematical Olympiads, ch. 1 and Coxeter & Greitzer, Geometry Revisited, ch. 1-2

Problem p2. ABCD is inscribed in a circle and angle A = 78°. Find angle C in degrees.

HintOpposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°.
Answer102
Method: Opposite angles

A + C = 180°, so C = 102°.

Method: Arcs

Angle A cuts arc BCD, angle C cuts arc BAD; these arcs add to 360°, so the angles add to 180°.

Source: Chen, Euclidean Geometry in Mathematical Olympiads, ch. 1 and Coxeter & Greitzer, Geometry Revisited, ch. 1-2

Problem p3. In triangle ABC, angle A = 40° and I is the incenter. Find angle BIC in degrees.

HintI lies on the angle bisectors from B and C.
Answer110
Method: Bisectors

Angle IBC = B/2 and angle ICB = C/2, so angle BIC = 180° − (B + C)/2 = 180° − (140°)/2 = 110°. In general it equals 90° + A/2.

Method: Example triangle

With A = 40°, B = 60°, C = 80°: IBC = 30°, ICB = 40°, so BIC = 110°. The answer does not depend on the choice of B and C.

Source: Chen, Euclidean Geometry in Mathematical Olympiads, ch. 1 and Coxeter & Greitzer, Geometry Revisited, ch. 1-2

Problem p4. In an acute triangle ABC, angle A = 50° and H is the orthocenter. Find angle BHC in degrees.

HintUse the altitudes from B and C to build a quadrilateral with two right angles.
Answer130
Method: Quadrilateral with two right angles

Let the altitudes from B and C meet the opposite sides at E and F. In quadrilateral AFHE, angles at E and F are 90°, so angle FHE = 360° − 90° − 90° − 50° = 130°, and BHC equals it (vertical angles).

Method: Example triangle

For angles 50°, 60°, 70° the formula BHC = 180° − A gives 130°; checking with coordinates gives the same.

Source: Chen, Euclidean Geometry in Mathematical Olympiads, ch. 1 and Coxeter & Greitzer, Geometry Revisited, ch. 1-2

Problem p5. In triangle ABC with angle A = 35°, O is the circumcenter. Find angle BOC in degrees.

HintThe central angle is twice the inscribed angle.
Answer70
Method: Central angle theorem

Angle BOC = 2·A = 70° (for an acute triangle).

Method: Isosceles triangle

OB = OC, so in triangle BOC the base angles are (180° − BOC)/2. These equal 90° − A = 55°. Hence BOC = 180° − 110° = 70°.

Source: Chen, Euclidean Geometry in Mathematical Olympiads, ch. 1 and Coxeter & Greitzer, Geometry Revisited, ch. 1-2

Problem p6. AB is a diameter of a circle and C lies on the circle with angle CAB = 28°. Find angle ABC in degrees.

HintThales: angle ACB is a right angle.
Answer62
Method: Thales

An angle inscribed in a semicircle is 90°, so angle ACB = 90° and ABC = 180° − 90° − 28° = 62°.

Method: Arcs

Angle CAB cuts arc CB = 56°, so arc AC = 180° − 56° = 124°, and angle ABC = 62°.

Source: Chen, Euclidean Geometry in Mathematical Olympiads, ch. 1 and Coxeter & Greitzer, Geometry Revisited, ch. 1-2

Problem p7. A tangent at A and a chord AB make an angle of 55°. How many degrees is arc AB (the one inside the angle)?

HintTangent-chord angle is half its arc.
Answer110
Method: Tangent-chord theorem

The angle between a tangent and a chord is half the intercepted arc, so the arc is 2·55° = 110°.

Method: Radius argument

The radius OA is perpendicular to the tangent, so angle OAB = 90° − 55° = 35°; triangle OAB is isosceles with base angles 35°, so angle AOB = 110°.

Source: Chen, Euclidean Geometry in Mathematical Olympiads, ch. 1 and Coxeter & Greitzer, Geometry Revisited, ch. 1-2

Problem p8. From a point P outside a circle one secant meets the circle at A then B with PA = 4 and PB = 9. A second secant from P meets it at C then D with PC = 6. Find PD.

HintPA·PB = PC·PD.
Answer6
Method: Power of a point

PA·PB = 36 = PC·PD = 6·PD, so PD = 6.

Method: Similar triangles

Triangles PAC and PDB are similar (they share the angle at P, and angle PAC = angle PDB because ABDC is cyclic). So PA/PD = PC/PB, that is 4/PD = 6/9, and PD = 6.

Source: Chen, Euclidean Geometry in Mathematical Olympiads, ch. 1 and Coxeter & Greitzer, Geometry Revisited, ch. 1-2

Problem p9. Two chords of a circle meet inside. One is split into pieces 3 and 8, and the other into 4 and x. Find x.

HintProducts of the pieces are equal.
Answer6
Method: Intersecting chords

3·8 = 24 = 4x, so x = 6.

Method: Similar triangles

The triangles formed by joining the endpoints are similar, which yields (piece)(piece) = (piece)(piece): 3/4 = x/8, x = 6.

Source: Chen, Euclidean Geometry in Mathematical Olympiads, ch. 1 and Coxeter & Greitzer, Geometry Revisited, ch. 1-2

Problem p10. Triangle ABC has sides 13, 14, 15. Find OI², where O is its circumcenter and I its incenter. Give a fraction.

HintEuler: OI² = R(R − 2r).
Answer65/64
Method: Euler's formula

Area = 84, so r = 84/21 = 4 and R = 13·14·15/(4·84) = 65/8. OI² = R(R − 2r) = (65/8)(65/8 − 8) = (65/8)(1/8) = 65/64.

Method: Coordinates

Put B = (0,0), C = (14,0), A = (5,12). Then I = (6,4) and O = (7, 33/8), so OI² = 1 + (1/8)² = 65/64.

Source: Chen, Euclidean Geometry in Mathematical Olympiads, ch. 1 and Coxeter & Greitzer, Geometry Revisited, ch. 1-2

Answer5
Method: Ptolemy

AC = BD = d, so d² = 3·3 + 4·4 = 25 and d = 5.

Method: Pythagoras

BD² = 3² + 4² = 25, so BD = 5.

Problem x20. Chords AB and CD of a circle meet at P inside it with AP = 6, PB = 10 and CP = 15. Find PD.

HintIntersecting chords theorem.
Answer4
Method: Theorem

AP · PB = CP · PD, so 6 · 10 = 15 · PD and PD = 4.

Method: Similar triangles

Triangles APC and DPB are similar, so AP/DP = CP/BP, giving the same equation 6 · 10 = 15 · PD.
