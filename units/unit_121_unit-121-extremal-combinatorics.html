Reading

1. The extremal principle

An extremal question asks for the largest or smallest size of a structure with a property. Two steps are always needed: (1) a bound showing that no structure can exceed the number, and (2) a construction reaching the number. A proof of only one of them is incomplete.

Worked example. What is the largest subset of {1, 2, …, 20} with no two consecutive numbers? The 10 pairs (1, 2), (3, 4), …, (19, 20) each contain at most one chosen number, so at most 10. The odd numbers 1, 3, …, 19 achieve 10. So the answer is 10.

Pigeonhole variant. Any 11 numbers from {1, …, 20} must contain two consecutive ones (same pairs, 10 boxes, 11 numbers).

2. Divisibility-free sets

What is the largest subset of {1, …, 2n} in which no element divides another? Write each number as 2^a × (odd part m). There are n odd parts, so among any n + 1 chosen numbers two share an odd part m, and then one divides the other. Hence at most n. The set {n + 1, …, 2n} reaches n, since two numbers there cannot divide each other (the larger is less than twice the smaller).

Worked example. For {1, …, 10}, n = 5 and the answer is 5, e.g. {6, 7, 8, 9, 10} (confirmed by checking all subsets).

Same technique. Pigeonhole on the odd part is typical for divisibility questions.

3. Graph extremes

Mantel's theorem: a graph on n vertices with no triangle has at most ⌊n²/4⌋ edges, with equality for the complete bipartite graph with parts of size ⌊n/2⌋ and ⌈n/2⌉. For n = 6 the maximum is 9, achieved by K₃,₃ (confirmed by exhaustive search over all graphs on 6 vertices).

Why. If a vertex v has degree d(v), its neighbours are pairwise non-adjacent, so the degrees satisfy d(u) + d(v) ≤ n for every edge uv. Summing over edges gives Σ d(v)² ≤ n · m, and Σ d(v)² ≥ (2m)²/n by Cauchy-Schwarz, so 4m²/n ≤ nm, which gives m ≤ n²/4.

Extremal choices. In a proof you can pick the vertex of largest degree, the longest path, the smallest counterexample, and so on, then show a contradiction or a better choice.

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. Proving a bound without showing it can be reached. Constructing an example and calling it the maximum. Mixing up 'maximal' (cannot be extended) with 'maximum' (the largest of all).

Checks. Test the bound for tiny n by brute force.

Practice. For each extremal problem write 'Bound:' and 'Construction:' as separate headings.

Worked examples with Try-its: Turán-type problems

What is the largest number of edges in a triangle-free graph on n vertices? The answer is ⌊n²/4⌋, achieved by the complete bipartite graph with parts as equal as possible: 6 edges for n = 5 (confirmed by testing all graphs on 5 vertices) and 9 for n = 6 (K₃,₃). Mantel's theorem proves it: adjacent vertices have no common neighbour, so d(u) + d(v) ≤ n for every edge uv.

Try it

How many edges can a triangle-free graph on 8 vertices have at most?

Answer. 16.

Worked examples with Try-its: Monotone subsequences

Erdős–Szekeres. Any sequence of n² + 1 distinct numbers contains an increasing or decreasing subsequence of length n + 1. So 10 distinct numbers always have a monotone subsequence of length 4, while 9 numbers may not: 3, 2, 1, 6, 5, 4, 9, 8, 7 has none of length 4.

Try it

What is the least length of a sequence of distinct numbers that forces a monotone subsequence of length 5?

Answer. 17.

Worked examples with Try-its: A longer problem

Problem. Show that among any 6 people there are 3 mutual friends or 3 mutual strangers. Solution. Fix a person; among the other 5, at least 3 are friends or at least 3 are strangers. Say 3 are friends with her. If any two of those 3 are friends we have a friendly triangle; otherwise those 3 are mutual strangers.

Reflection. This is R(3, 3) = 6, the first Ramsey number.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Triangle-free graphs. On 7 vertices the largest triangle-free graph has ⌊49/4⌋ = 12 edges (K₃,₄). Ramsey. Among any 6 people there are 3 mutual friends or 3 mutual strangers, but 5 people can avoid this: colour the edges of a pentagon one colour and the diagonals another. Monotone subsequences. Any sequence of 10 distinct numbers has a monotone subsequence of length 4.

Try it

What is the largest number of edges in a triangle-free graph on 10 vertices?

Answer. 25.

Try it

How many distinct numbers guarantee a monotone subsequence of length 3?

Answer. 5 (since 2² + 1).

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. Show that among any 5 points in a unit square two are within √2/2 of each other. Solution. Divide the square into four squares of side 1/2, each of diameter √2/2; two of the five points share a small square.

Reflection. This is pigeonhole used as an extremal bound.

Practice problems (25)

Problem p1. What is the largest number of edges in a graph on 9 vertices that contains no K₄?

HintTuran: balanced complete 3-partite graph.
Answer27
Method: Turan

The parts have sizes 3, 3, 3, with 3·3 + 3·3 + 3·3 = 27 edges.

Method: Check

An unbalanced split 4, 3, 2 gives 12 + 8 + 6 = 26, which is smaller.

Source: Sriram, Olympiad Combinatorics, ch. 7

Problem p2. Subsets of {1, 2, …, 8} are chosen so that none contains another. What is the largest possible number of subsets?

HintSperner's theorem.
Answer70
Method: Sperner

C(8, 4) = 70, using all subsets of size 4.

Method: Chains

The 8! maximal chains each contain at most one chosen subset, which proves the bound.

Source: Sriram, Olympiad Combinatorics, ch. 7

Problem p3. A family of 3-element subsets of {1, …, 8} has the property that any two members share an element. What is the largest possible size?

HintErdos-Ko-Rado: C(n − 1, k − 1).
Answer21
Method: EKR

C(7, 2) = 21, taking all triples containing 1.

Method: Reason

Fixing one element and choosing the other two from the remaining 7 gives 21 triples that pairwise intersect.

Source: Sriram, Olympiad Combinatorics, ch. 7

Problem p4. What is the largest number of edges in a graph on 8 vertices that contains no cycle?

HintA forest.
Answer7
Method: Tree

A connected acyclic graph is a tree with n − 1 = 7 edges, and a forest has fewer.

Method: Counting

Each edge reduces the number of components by one without creating a cycle.

Problem p5. What is the largest number of integers that can be chosen from {1, 2, …, 30} with none dividing another?

HintTake 16..30.
Answer15
Method: Bound

The 15 numbers 16 to 30 work since twice any of them exceeds 30. Chains {m, 2m, 4m, …} for odd m ≤ 29 number 15, and each chain gives at most one chosen number, so 15 is maximal.

Method: Chains

There are 15 odd numbers up to 30, giving 15 chains that partition the set.

Source: Sriram, Olympiad Combinatorics, ch. 7

Problem p6. A sequence of 17 distinct numbers always contains a monotone subsequence of what length at least?

Hintn² + 1 numbers give length n + 1.
Answer5
Method: Erdos-Szekeres

17 = 4² + 1, so there is a monotone subsequence of length 5.

Method: Reason

Otherwise label each term by the lengths (i, d) of the longest increasing and decreasing subsequences ending there; there are at most 16 labels but 17 terms, and distinct terms receive distinct labels.

Source: Sriram, Olympiad Combinatorics, ch. 7

Problem p7. What is the largest number of edges in a bipartite graph on 10 vertices?

HintBalanced complete bipartite graph.
Answer25
Method: Product

The maximum of a(10 − a) is 25.

Method: Mantel

This equals Mantel's bound ⌊100/4⌋ = 25.

Problem p9. What is the smallest number of edges in a graph on 10 vertices that forces a triangle?

HintOne more than the maximum triangle-free count.
Answer26
Method: Mantel

A triangle-free graph has at most 25 edges, so 26 edges force a triangle.

Method: Sharpness

The complete bipartite graph K₅,₅ has 25 edges and no triangle.

Problem x18. How many edges does the complete graph K_30 have?

HintEvery pair of vertices is joined.
Answer435
Method: Pairs

C(30, 2) = 435.

Method: Handshake lemma

Each of the 30 vertices has degree 29, so the edges number 30 · 29/2 = 435.

Source: Sriram, Olympiad Combinatorics, ch. 7

Problem x19. 25 objects are placed into 6 boxes. What is the largest number n for which some box must contain at least n objects?

HintPigeonhole principle.
Answer5
Method: Pigeonhole

⌈25/6⌉ = 5.

Method: Contradiction

If every box had at most 4 objects, there would be at most 6 · 4 = 24 < 25 objects in all.
