Reading

1. Matrices and products

A matrix is a rectangular array of numbers. Matrices of the same size add entry by entry. The product of an m × n matrix and an n × p matrix is m × p, with entry (i, j) equal to row i of the first times column j of the second. In general AB ≠ BA.

Worked example. Let A = [[1, 1], [1, 0]]. Then A² = [[2, 1], [1, 1]], A³ = [[3, 2], [2, 1]], A⁴ = [[5, 3], [3, 2]], A⁵ = [[8, 5], [5, 3]]. The entries are Fibonacci numbers: Aⁿ = [[F_{n+1}, F_n], [F_n, F_{n−1}]].

Identity and inverse. I has 1s on the diagonal. A matrix A has an inverse A⁻¹ with AA⁻¹ = I if and only if its determinant is not 0. For a 2 × 2 matrix [[a, b], [c, d]] the inverse is (1/(ad − bc)) [[d, −b], [−c, a]].

2. Determinants

For a 2 × 2 matrix, det = ad − bc. For 3 × 3 expand along a row: det [[a, b, c], [d, e, f], [g, h, i]] = a(ei − fh) − b(di − fg) + c(dh − eg). The determinant is multiplicative: det(AB) = det A · det B.

Worked example. det [[2, 0, 1], [1, 3, 2], [1, 1, 2]] = 2(6 − 2) − 0 + 1(1 − 3) = 8 − 2 = 6.

Geometry. |det| is the factor by which the linear map multiplies areas. The triangle with vertices (0, 0), (4, 1), (1, 3) has area ½|4 × 3 − 1 × 1| = 5.5.

Properties. Swapping two rows changes the sign; two equal rows give determinant 0; adding a multiple of one row to another changes nothing.

3. Linear systems and Cramer's rule

For the system ax + by = e, cx + dy = f with D = ad − bc ≠ 0: x = (ed − bf)/D and y = (af − ec)/D.

Worked example. 2x + 3y = 8, x − y = −1. Here D = 2(−1) − 3(1) = −5; x = (8 × (−1) − 3 × (−1))/(−5) = (−5)/(−5) = 1; y = (2 × (−1) − 1 × 8)/(−5) = (−10)/(−5) = 2. Check: 2 + 6 = 8 and 1 − 2 = −1 ✓.

When D = 0. The equations are either contradictory (no solution) or dependent (infinitely many).

Powers and eigenvalues. For B = [[2, 1], [1, 2]] the eigenvalues are 3 and 1, so Bⁿ = ½[[3ⁿ + 1, 3ⁿ − 1], [3ⁿ − 1, 3ⁿ + 1]]; B³ = [[14, 13], [13, 14]] ✓.

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. Multiplying matrices entry by entry. Assuming AB = BA. Forgetting the alternating signs in cofactor expansion.

Checks. Multiply A by A⁻¹ to confirm you get I.

Practice. Compute a few Fibonacci matrix powers by hand until the pattern is clear.

Worked examples with Try-its: Determinants and area

For a 2 × 2 matrix the determinant is ad − bc. The area of the triangle with vertices (0, 0), (4, 0), (0, 3) is ½|det[[4, 0], [0, 3]]| = 6. A 3 × 3 determinant expands along a row: det[[1, 2, 3], [0, 1, 4], [5, 6, 0]] = 1(0 − 24) − 2(0 − 20) + 3(0 − 5) = 1. A matrix is invertible exactly when its determinant is non-zero.

Try it

Find det[[3, 2], [5, 4]].

Answer. 12 − 10 = 2.

Worked examples with Try-its: Systems and powers

Cramer's rule. For 2x + y = 5 and x + 3y = 10 the determinant is 5, x = (5 · 3 − 10 · 1)/5 = 1 and y = (2 · 10 − 1 · 5)/5 = 3. Powers. The matrix [[1, 1], [1, 0]]ⁿ has Fibonacci numbers as entries: its fifth power is [[8, 5], [5, 3]] (a computer check agrees).

Try it

Solve x + 2y = 4 and 3x − y = 5 using determinants.

Answer. D = 1·(−1) − 2·3 = −7; x = (4·(−1) − 2·5)/(−7) = 2; y = (1·5 − 4·3)/(−7) = 1.

Worked examples with Try-its: A longer problem

Problem. Is the matrix [[2, 4], [3, 6]] invertible? Solution. Its determinant is 12 − 12 = 0, so no: the rows are proportional and the matrix collapses the plane to a line.

Reflection. A zero determinant means information is lost, so no inverse exists.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Determinant of a diagonal matrix. diag(2, 3, 4) has determinant 24. Rotation matrix. The matrix [[0, −1], [1, 0]] rotates the plane by 90°, and its fourth power is the identity. Inverse. [[2, 1], [1, 1]] has determinant 1 and inverse [[1, −1], [−1, 2]]. det[[1, 2], [3, 4]] = −2.

Try it

Find det[[2, 5], [1, 3]].

Answer. 6 − 5 = 1.

Try it

What is det(AB) if det A = 3 and det B = 4?

Answer. 12.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. For what k is the matrix [[k, 2], [8, k]] singular? Solution. det = k² − 16 = 0 gives k = ±4.

Check. For k = 4 the rows (4, 2) and (8, 4) are proportional.

Problem p1. Find the determinant of ((3, 1), (4, 2)).

Hintad − bc.
Answer2
Method: Formula

3 · 2 − 1 · 4 = 2.

Method: Area

The columns (3, 4) and (1, 2) span a parallelogram of area 2.

Problem p2. Let A = ((1, 2), (3, 4)). Find the trace (sum of the diagonal entries) of A².

HintCompute A².
Answer29
Method: Multiply

A² = ((7, 10), (15, 22)), so the trace is 29.

Method: Formula

tr(A²) = (tr A)² − 2 det A = 25 − 2(−2) = 29.

Problem p7. Solve 3x + 2y = 7 and x − y = 1 using Cramer's rule. Find x + y as a fraction.

Hintx = det with column replaced / det.
Answer13/5
Method: Cramer

det = 3(−1) − 2(1) = −5; x = (7(−1) − 2(1))/(−5) = 9/5; y = (3 · 1 − 7 · 1)/(−5) = 4/5. So x + y = 13/5.

Method: Substitution

y = x − 1 gives 5x − 2 = 7, so x = 9/5, y = 4/5.

Source: Andreescu & Gelca, 2.9; AoPS Volume 2 ch. 10-11

Problem p8. A 2 × 2 matrix has trace 5 and determinant 6. What is the larger of its two eigenvalues?

HintThe eigenvalues satisfy λ² − 5λ + 6 = 0.
Answer3
Method: Quadratic

(λ − 2)(λ − 3) = 0, so the larger is 3.

Method: Check

The sum 2 + 3 = 5 is the trace and the product 6 is the determinant.

Problem p9. R is the 90° counterclockwise rotation matrix. R³ is applied to the point (1, 0), giving (a, b). Find a + b.

Rotating (1, 0) by 270° counterclockwise gives (0, −1), so a + b = −1.

(1, 0) → (0, 1) → (−1, 0) → (0, −1).

Problem x19. The roots of x² − 11x + 5 = 0 are r and s. Find r² + s².

HintUse r + s and rs.
Answer111
Method: Vieta

r + s = 11 and rs = 5, so r² + s² = (r + s)² − 2rs = 121 − 10 = 111.

Method: Recurrence

Let pₙ = rⁿ + sⁿ. Then p₀ = 2, p₁ = 11 and p₂ = 11 · p₁ − 5 · p₀ = 121 − 10 = 111.

Source: Andreescu & Gelca, 2.9; AoPS Volume 2 ch. 10-11
