Reading

1. The four rigid motions and dilation

A translation slides everything by a vector; a reflection flips across a line; a rotation turns about a point; a dilation scales from a centre. The first three are isometries: they preserve distances, angles and areas. Reflecting (3, 1) in the line y = x gives (1, 3); in the x-axis (3, −1). Rotating (2, 0) by 90° about the origin gives (0, 2). A dilation by factor 3 about the origin sends (2, 0) to (6, 0) and multiplies areas by 9: the triangle (0, 0), (4, 0), (0, 3) of area 6 becomes one of area 24 under factor 2 and of area 54 under factor 3.

Composition. Two reflections in parallel lines give a translation (by twice the distance between the lines); two reflections in lines meeting at angle θ give a rotation by 2θ about the intersection point. Hence reflecting across lines at 30° gives a 60° rotation. A rotation by 60° takes (1, 0) to (1/2, √3/2).

Try it

Reflect the point (5, 2) across the line x = 3.

Answer. (1, 2).

2. Using transformations to solve problems

Shortest path by reflection. A hiker walks from A to a river (a straight line) and then to B on the same side. To minimise the total length, reflect B across the river to B′ and join A to B′: the intersection with the river is the best crossing point, because the path A → X → B has the same length as A → X → B′. Rotation trick. To show that for a point inside an equilateral triangle the distances to the vertices form a triangle, rotate by 60° about one vertex.

Symmetry. A figure has symmetry under a transformation if it maps onto itself. A rectangle has 2 reflection lines and rotation by 180°; a square has 4 reflection lines and rotations by 90°; a regular n-gon has n reflections and n rotations.

Try it

A is (0, 4), B is (6, 2), and the river is the x-axis. Find the shortest path from A to the river to B.

Answer. Reflect B to (6, −2): distance from (0, 4) = √(36 + 36) = 6√2.

3. A longer problem

Problem. A point P = (4, 1) is reflected across the line y = x, then rotated 90° counterclockwise about the origin, then translated by (−2, 3). Where does it end?

Solution. Reflection: (1, 4). Rotation by 90° (x, y) → (−y, x): (−4, 1). Translation: (−6, 4).

Reflection. The order matters: rotating first, then reflecting, then translating would give (2, 2) instead, which shows that transformations generally do not commute.

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. (1) Applying transformations in the wrong order. (2) Rotating in the wrong direction. (3) Forgetting dilation scales area by k². (4) Reflecting about the wrong axis.

Checks. Distances should be preserved by isometries.

How to practise. Track one test point through every step. In Unlimited practice, rotate the transformation families.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Coordinates. A rotation by 90° anticlockwise about the origin sends (3, 4) to (−4, 3); a reflection in y = −x sends (a, b) to (−b, −a); a dilation by 2 about the origin sends (x, y) to (2x, 2y). The map x ↦ 2x + 3 has the fixed point where x = 2x + 3, namely x = −3. A composition of a dilation by 2 followed by a translation by (1, 0) is the map (x, y) ↦ (2x + 1, 2y), whose fixed point is (−1, 0).

Try it

Where does (2, 5) go under reflection in the line y = x followed by a translation by (1, −1)?

Answer. (5, 2) then (6, 1).

Try it

What is the image of (1, 2) under a dilation by 3 centred at (1, 1)?

Answer. (1, 1) + 3 · (0, 1) = (1, 4).

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. A triangle with vertices (0, 0), (2, 0), (0, 3) is rotated 90° anticlockwise about the origin and then reflected in the x-axis. Find the final vertices. Solution. The rotation sends (x, y) to (−y, x): (0, 0), (0, 2), (−3, 0). The reflection sends (x, y) to (x, −y): (0, 0), (0, −2), (−3, 0). The area is ½ · 2 · 3 = 3 both before and after, as expected for isometries.

Reflection. Apply the maps one at a time and write the result after each step.

Problem p1. Reflect the point (3, 5) across the line y = x and then across the x-axis. Where does it end up? Answer as x, y.

HintSwap, then change the sign of y.
Answer5, -3
Method: Step by step

y = x swaps the coordinates: (5, 3). The x-axis flips y: (5, −3).

Method: Rotation

The two reflections are in lines at 45°, so together they rotate by 90° clockwise: (x, y) → (y, −x) sends (3, 5) to (5, −3).
