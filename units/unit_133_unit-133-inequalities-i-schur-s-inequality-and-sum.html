Reading

1. The SOS method

A sum of squares certificate proves an inequality by writing the difference as a sum of squares. The basic identity is a² + b² + c² − ab − bc − ca = ½[(a − b)² + (b − c)² + (c − a)²]; for a = 3, b = 5, c = 8 it gives ½(4 + 9 + 25) = 19. It shows at once that the sum of squares is at least the sum of products, with equality only when a = b = c.

Nesbitt. a/(b + c) + b/(c + a) + c/(a + b) − 3/2 = ½ Σ (a − b)²/((a + c)(b + c)) (checked with exact fractions), so the sum is at least 3/2 for positive a, b, c. The method: move everything to one side and look for the form Σ S·(a − b)² with non-negative weights S.

Try it

Compute a² + b² + c² − ab − bc − ca for (a, b, c) = (1, 2, 4).

Answer. ½(1 + 4 + 9) = 7.

Try it

When does equality hold in a² + b² + c² ≥ ab + bc + ca?

Answer. Exactly when a = b = c.

2. Schur's inequality

For non-negative a, b, c and t = 1: a(a − b)(a − c) + b(b − a)(b − c) + c(c − a)(c − b) ≥ 0. Expanded, Σa³ + 3abc ≥ Σsym a²b. Equality holds when a = b = c or when two variables are equal and the third is 0. Proof idea: assume a ≥ b ≥ c; then a(a − b)(a − c) ≥ b(a − b)(b − c) and the last term c(c − a)(c − b) is non-negative.

For (a, b, c) = (1, 2, 3) the three terms are 2, −2 and 6, and the total 6 is positive, consistent with the inequality. Schur is the standard tool when AM-GM is too weak because the equality case is not only a = b = c.

Try it

Evaluate Schur's expression for (a, b, c) = (2, 2, 0).

Answer. 2·0·2 + 2·0·2 + 0 = 0 (equality case).

Try it

Is Schur's inequality true for negative numbers?

Answer. No: it needs a, b, c ≥ 0.

3. Mixing and a worked problem

Mixing variables. If a ≤ b − 2, replacing (a, b) by (a + 1, b − 1) keeps the sum and raises the product by b − a − 1 > 0. Repeating shows that three positive integers with sum 20 have largest product 6 · 7 · 7 = 294.

Problem. Minimise (x + 4y)(1/x + 1/y) for x, y > 0. Solution. The product is 1 + 4 + x/y + 4y/x ≥ 5 + 2√4 = 9, attained when x/y = 4y/x, that is x = 2y. Cauchy–Schwarz gives the same minimum, (1 + 2)² = 9.

Try it

Largest product of three positive integers with sum 12?

Answer. 4 · 4 · 4 = 64.

4. In the books

Pham Kim Hung, ch. 8 (“A note on symmetric inequalities”). Section 8.1 proves Schur's inequality (Theorem 10) by assuming a ≥ b ≥ c, putting x = a − b and y = b − c, and noting that every term of the rewritten inequality is non-negative. It is equivalent to (a + b − c)(b + c − a)(c + a − b) ≤ abc. Theorem 11 (general Schur) replaces a, b, c by aᵏ, bᵏ, cᵏ for any positive k, and the book uses the fourth- and sixth-degree cases in later examples. Equality holds at a = b = c and at a = b, c = 0.

Cîrtoaje, ch. 1 and 2. The warm-up set (ch. 1) contains several Schur-type problems, for example the one built from E(a,b,c) = Σ a(a − b)(a − c) (problem 12); ch. 2 starts from special fourth-degree inequalities.

Honest note. Neither book has a chapter called “SOS”. The identity a² + b² + c² − ab − bc − ca = ½Σ(a − b)² used here is standard algebra, and the 25 problems are my own numbers, not the books' problems.

Problem p9. For positive x, y find the minimum of (1x + 4y)(1/x + 1/y).

HintExpand and use AM-GM, or use Cauchy–Schwarz.
Answer9
Method: Cauchy–Schwarz

(1x + 4y)(1/x + 1/y) ≥ (1 + 2)² = 9, with equality when x/y = 1/2.

Method: Expand

The product is 1 + 4 + 4y/x + 1x/y; the last two terms have product 4, so their sum is at least 2·2 = 4. Total at least 9.

Source: Technique from Pham Kim Hung ch. 8 and Cîrtoaje ch. 1–2 (original problem)

Problem p13. Real numbers x, y, z satisfy x + y + z = 3. What is the minimum value of x² + y² + z²? (fraction if needed)

HintCompare with the sum of squares of the differences.
Answer3
Method: SOS

3(x² + y² + z²) − (x + y + z)² = (x − y)² + (y − z)² + (z − x)² ≥ 0, so x² + y² + z² ≥ 9/3 = 3.

Method: Equal split

By symmetry the minimum is at x = y = z = 3/3, giving 3 · (3/3)² = 3.

Source: Technique from Pham Kim Hung ch. 8 and Cîrtoaje ch. 1–2 (original problem)

Problem p17. How many ordered triples (a, b, c) of integers with 0 ≤ a, b, c ≤ 3 make a(a − b)(a − c) + b(b − a)(b − c) + c(c − a)(c − b) equal to 0?

HintSchur's inequality has equality when a = b = c or when two are equal and the third is 0.
Answer13
Method: Equality cases

All equal: 4 triples. Exactly one zero and the others equal and positive: 3 · 3 = 9 triples. Total 13.

Method: Listing

The zero value occurs only for (k, k, k) with k = 0..3, and for the permutations of (k, k, 0) with k = 1..3; that is 4 + 9 = 13.

Source: Technique from Pham Kim Hung ch. 8 and Cîrtoaje ch. 1–2 (original problem)

Problem p20. Three positive integers have sum 20. What is the largest possible product?

HintIf two numbers differ by 2 or more, moving them closer increases the product.
Answer294
Method: Mixing

If a ≤ b − 2, replacing (a, b) by (a + 1, b − 1) changes ab to ab + b − a − 1 > ab. So the numbers differ by at most 1: 6, 7, 7, product 294.

Method: Divide

20 = 3 · 6 + 2, so the numbers are 6, 7, 7 and the product is 294.

Source: Technique from Pham Kim Hung ch. 8 and Cîrtoaje ch. 1–2 (original problem)
