Reading

1. Roots of unity as filters

If ω = e^(2πi/3) then 1 + ω + ω² = 0, so (f(1) + f(ω) + f(ω²))/3 keeps exactly the terms of a polynomial f whose exponents are multiples of 3. For f(t) = (1 + t)⁶ this gives the number of subsets of a 6-set with size divisible by 3: (64 + 2cos(2π))/3 = 22 and directly C(6,0) + C(6,3) + C(6,6) = 1 + 20 + 1 = 22 ✓.

For 4 classes use 1, i, −1, −i. Subsets of an 8-set of size ≡ 1 mod 4: C(8,1) + C(8,5) = 8 + 56 = 64 ✓.

Try it

How many subsets of a 6-set have size divisible by 3?

Answer. 22.

Try it

How many subsets of an 8-set have size ≡ 1 mod 4?

Answer. 64.

2. Generating functions for counting

The coefficient of xⁿ in a product of factors counts ways to choose terms with exponents adding to n. Making change from 1-, 2-, 5-cent coins uses 1/((1 − x)(1 − x²)(1 − x⁵)); for 10 cents the answer is 10. Dice sums come from (x + … + x⁶)ᵏ; two dice make 7 in 6 ways. Cyclotomic fact: Π(1 − ζᵏ) = n, because 1 + x + … + xⁿ⁻¹ = Π(x − ζᵏ).

Try it

Ways to total 7 with two dice (ordered)?

Answer. 6.

Try it

Number of non-negative solutions of a + b + c = 4?

Answer. 15.

3. A worked problem

Problem. How many subsets of {1,…,12} have a size divisible by 3? Solution. The count is (2¹² + 2cos(12π/3))/3 = (4096 + 2)/3 = 1366.

Check. C(12,0) + C(12,3) + C(12,6) + C(12,9) + C(12,12) = 1 + 220 + 924 + 220 + 1 = 1366 ✓.

4. In the books

Straight from the Book, ch. 7. The central idea is that a congruence between integers can be written as a sum of powers of a root of unity, which is exactly the roots-of-unity filter used in this unit. Addendum 7.A develops finite Fourier analysis, up to Gauss sums and the Pólya–Vinogradov inequality.

Ch. 8 (“Formal Series Revisited”). Sections 8.1 to 8.4 cover counting problems, proving identities, recurrence relations and additive properties with generating functions; the problems include Catalan's problem and counting irreducible polynomials of fixed degree mod p. Addendum 8.A proves Lagrange's inversion theorem, with Cayley's tree formula as an application.

Honest note. These chapters go well beyond a first unit. My 25 problems are my own examples of the filter and the coefficient-extraction idea.

Problem p1. How many subsets of a set of 6 elements have a size divisible by 3?

HintAdd the three cube-root-of-unity values of (1 + t)^6.
Answer22
Method: Roots of unity filter

The count is ((1+1)^6 + (1+ω)^6 + (1+ω²)^6)/3 = (2^6 + 2cos(6π/3))/3 = 22.

Method: Recurrence

Track the number of subsets of each size class mod 3 as elements are added; after 6 elements the class 0 count is 22.

Source: Technique from Andreescu & Dospinescu ch. 7–8, Gelca & Andreescu 6.2.2 (original problem)

Problem p5. Find the coefficient of x^4 in (1 + x + x²)^4.

HintChoose how many factors give x² and how many give x.
Answer19
Method: Counting

Choose j factors to give x² and k − 2j to give x: Σ C(4,j)·C(4−j,4−2j) = 19.

Method: Multiply out

Multiplying the polynomial by itself 3 times gives the x^4 coefficient 19.

Source: Technique from Andreescu & Dospinescu ch. 7–8, Gelca & Andreescu 6.2.2 (original problem)

Problem p9. In how many ways can 10 cents be made from 1-, 2- and 5-cent coins (order does not matter)?

HintFix the number of 5-cent coins first.
Answer10
Method: Case on 5-cent coins

If i fives are used, the rest is paid with 1s and 2s in ⌊(10 − 5i)/2⌋ + 1 ways. Summing over i gives 10.

Method: Generating function

The coefficient of x^10 in 1/((1−x)(1−x²)(1−x⁵)) is 10.

Source: Technique from Andreescu & Dospinescu ch. 7–8, Gelca & Andreescu 6.2.2 (original problem)

Problem p13. Let ζ = e^(2πi/5). Find |(1 − ζ)(1 − ζ²)…(1 − ζ^4)|.

HintThe ζᵏ (k = 1,…,4) are the roots of 1 + x + … + x^4.
Answer5
Method: Cyclotomic factorisation

1 + x + … + x^4 = Π(x − ζᵏ); setting x = 1 gives the product = 5.

Method: Numerical check

Multiplying the 4 moduli |1 − ζᵏ| = 2 sin(kπ/5) gives 5.

Source: Technique from Andreescu & Dospinescu ch. 7–8, Gelca & Andreescu 6.2.2 (original problem)

Problem p16. In how many ways can four ordered dice show a total of 10?

HintFind the coefficient of x^10 in (x + x² + … + x⁶)⁴.
Answer80
Method: Generating function

The coefficient of x^10 in (x + … + x⁶)⁴ is 80.

Method: Inclusion–exclusion

Σ(−1)ʲ C(4,j) C(10 − 6j − 1, 3) = 80.

Problem p20. How many subsets of a set of 8 elements have size congruent to 1 mod 4?

HintUse the four fourth roots of unity as a filter.
Answer64
Method: Roots of unity filter

(1/4)Σ i^(−j)(1 + i^j)^8 over j = 0..3 = 64.

Method: Residue recurrence

Update the four class counts (a₀,a₁,a₂,a₃) one element at a time; the class 1 count after 8 elements is 64.

Source: Technique from Andreescu & Dospinescu ch. 7–8, Gelca & Andreescu 6.2.2 (original problem)

Problem p23. How many non-negative integer triples (a, b, c) have a + b + c = 6?

HintUse stars and bars, or fix a first.
Answer28
Method: Stars and bars

C(6 + 2, 2) = 28.

Method: Fix a

For each a from 0 to 6 there are 6 − a + 1 pairs (b, c); the sum is 28.

Source: Technique from Andreescu & Dospinescu ch. 7–8, Gelca & Andreescu 6.2.2 (original problem)
