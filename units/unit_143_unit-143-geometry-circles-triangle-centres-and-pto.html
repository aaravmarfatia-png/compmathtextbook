Reading

1. Triangle quantities

With sides a, b, c, semiperimeter s and area K (Heron): R = abc/(4K), r = K/s. For the 13-14-15 triangle s = 21, K = 84, R = 2730/336 = 65/8, r = 4. A right triangle with legs a, b has an altitude to the hypotenuse of ab/c; for 5-12-13 that is 60/13.

Try it

Inradius of the 5-12-13 triangle?

Answer. K = 30, s = 15, r = 2.

Try it

Circumradius of the 6-8-10 triangle?

Answer. 5, half the hypotenuse.

2. Circles, bisectors and Ptolemy

Power of a point. For two chords through P inside a circle, PA·PB = PC·PD; from outside, tangent² = PA·PB for the secant PAB. If PA = 4 and AB = 5 the tangent has t² = 4 · 9 = 36 so t = 6. Bisector. BD/DC = AB/AC. Ptolemy. In a cyclic quadrilateral the product of the diagonals equals the sum of products of opposite sides.

Try it

PA = 2, PB = 8, PC = 4 (chords): PD?

Answer. 4.

Try it

Triangle BC = 10, AB = 6, AC = 4: BD?

Answer. 6 (BD/DC = 6/4, so BD = 6).

3. A worked problem

Problem. Points A, B, C, D lie on a circle in order, AB = 7, BC = 24, CD = 15, DA = 20. Find BD. Solution. 7² + 24² = 25² and 15² + 20² = 25², so AC = 25 is a diameter. Ptolemy: 25·BD = 7·15 + 24·20 = 585, so BD = 117/5.

Check. 585/25 = 23.4 = 117/5 ✓; BD is shorter than the diameter 25 ✓.

4. In the books

Kedlaya. Ch. 1 builds the plane from coordinates (1.2 distances and circles, 1.4 areas of polygons). Section 2.1 is trigonometry (law of sines, law of cosines, the inradius-circumradius relation r = 4R sin(A/2) sin(B/2) sin(C/2)). Ptolemy appears as Theorem 2.3.1 in the form AC·BD ≤ AB·CD + BC·DA, with equality exactly for a convex cyclic quadrilateral. Ch. 6 covers power of a point (6.1) and the radical axis (6.2); 8.2 is cyclic quadrilaterals.

Problem p1. Triangle ABC has sides 13, 14, 15. Find its circumradius. (fraction)

HintR = abc/(4K) and use Heron's formula for the area K.
Answer65/8
Method: Area formula

s = 21, K = 84, so R = 13·14·15/(4·84) = 65/8.

Method: Law of sines

With C the angle opposite 15, sin C = 2K/(ab) = 12/13 and R = 15/(2 sin C) = 65/8.

Source: Technique from Kedlaya ch. 6–8 and the IMO Compendium §2.3 (original problem)

Problem p8. Chords AB and CD of a circle meet at P inside the circle. PA = 4, PB = 9 and PC = 6. Find PD. (fraction)

HintPower of a point: PA·PB = PC·PD.
Answer6
Method: Power of a point

PD = PA·PB/PC = 4·9/6 = 6.

Method: Similar triangles

Triangles PAC and PDB are similar, so PA/PD = PC/PB giving PD = 6.

Source: Technique from Kedlaya ch. 6–8 and the IMO Compendium §2.3 (original problem)

Problem p12. From a point P outside a circle a secant meets the circle at A and B with PA = 4 and AB = 5. Find the length of a tangent from P.

Method: Similar triangles

The tangent triangle gives t/PA = PB/t so t² = PA·PB = 36.

Problem p15. In triangle ABC, BC = 7, CA = 8 and AB = 9. The bisector of angle A meets BC at D. Find BD. (fraction)

HintAngle bisector theorem: BD/DC = AB/AC.
Answer63/17
Method: Bisector theorem

BD/DC = 9/8, so BD = 7·9/(8 + 9) = 63/17.

Method: Ratio

Divide BC = 7 in the ratio 9 : 8; the part next to B is 63/17.

Source: Technique from Kedlaya ch. 6–8 and the IMO Compendium §2.3 (original problem)

Problem p21. Points A, B, C, D lie on a circle in that order with AB = 7, BC = 24, CD = 15 and DA = 20. Find BD. (fraction)

HintPtolemy: AC · BD = AB · CD + BC · DA, and AC is the diameter.
Answer117/5
Method: Ptolemy

AC = 25, so BD = (7·15 + 24·20)/25 = 117/5.

Method: Coordinates

Put AC on the x-axis and compute B and D from the right angles at B and D; the distance BD is 117/5.

Source: Technique from Kedlaya ch. 6–8 and the IMO Compendium §2.3 (original problem)

Problem p24. A right triangle has legs 5 and 12. Find the altitude to the hypotenuse. (fraction)

HintArea two ways.
Answer60/13
Method: Area two ways

Hypotenuse 13; ½·5·12 = ½·13·h so h = 60/13.

Method: Similar triangles

The altitude splits the triangle into similar ones: h/5 = 12/13, so h = 60/13.
