Reading

1. Digits and remainders

A decimal number is a sum of digits times powers of 10: 4 372 = 4 · 1000 + 3 · 100 + 7 · 10 + 2. Because 10 ≡ 1 (mod 9), every power of ten leaves remainder 1 on division by 9, so a number has the same remainder modulo 9 as the sum of its digits. Similarly modulo 3. For example 123 456 has digit sum 21, so it leaves remainder 3 on division by 9 and is divisible by 3 but not 9. I checked that n and its digit sum agree modulo 9 (and modulo 3) for every n up to 10 000.

Other divisibility rules. Since 10 ≡ −1 (mod 11), a number has the same remainder modulo 11 as its alternating digit sum (units digit minus tens digit plus hundreds digit …). Since 100 ≡ 0 (mod 4) the last two digits decide divisibility by 4, and since 1000 ≡ 0 (mod 8) the last three digits decide divisibility by 8. All these rules were confirmed over thousands of values.

The seven trap. 1001 = 7 · 11 · 13, so any six-digit number of the form abcabc (like 372 372) is divisible by 7, 11 and 13 — it equals abc · 1001. A direct check over all 900 such numbers confirms this. For 7 alone there is the rule: n is divisible by 7 exactly when (n with its last digit deleted) minus twice the last digit is divisible by 7.

Casting out nines. To check a product or sum, compare remainders mod 9. For 123 × 456 = 56 088: 123 ≡ 6, 456 ≡ 6, 6 · 6 = 36 ≡ 0; and 56 088 has digit sum 27 ≡ 0 ✓. A mismatch proves an error; a match does not prove correctness.

Try it

Show that 372 372 is divisible by 7, 11 and 13.

Answer. 372 372 = 372 · 1001 = 372 · 7 · 11 · 13.

2. Last digits and cycles

The last digit of a power repeats in a cycle. Powers of 2 end in 2, 4, 8, 6, 2, 4, 8, 6, … (cycle of length 4); powers of 3 end in 3, 9, 7, 1, …; powers of 7 end in 7, 9, 3, 1, …. To find the last digit of 7²⁰²³, divide the exponent by the cycle length: 2023 = 4 · 505 + 3, so the last digit is the third in the cycle 7, 9, 3, 1, namely 3 (and a direct modular computation agrees). The last digit of 13²⁰²³ is that of 3²⁰²³, which is 7.

Last two digits. The powers of 7 end in 07, 49, 43, 01 and then repeat, so 7²⁰²³ ends in the third of these, 43. The cycle length is 4 again. Always check the cycle by listing the first few powers rather than trusting memory.

Remainders modulo 9. The powers of 2 modulo 9 are 2, 4, 8, 7, 5, 1 and then repeat with period 6. Since 2023 = 6 · 337 + 1, 2²⁰²³ ≡ 2 (mod 9); the digit sum of 2²⁰²³ is therefore ≡ 2 mod 9.

How many digits? The number of digits of N is ⌊log₁₀ N⌋ + 1. For 2¹⁰⁰: 100 log₁₀ 2 ≈ 30.1, so 31 digits (confirmed by direct computation). 2²⁰²³ has 609 digits. For a product like 2⁵⁰ · 5⁵⁰ = 10⁵⁰ there are 51 digits; 5⁵⁰ alone has 35.

Try it

What is the last digit of 2⁹⁹ + 3⁹⁹?

Answer. 99 = 4 · 24 + 3: 2⁹⁹ ends in 8 and 3⁹⁹ ends in 7; 8 + 7 = 15, so the last digit is 5.

3. Repunits

A repunit is a number made of ones: Rₙ = 11…1 (n ones) = (10ⁿ − 1)/9. R₂ = 11, R₃ = 111 = 3 · 37, R₄ = 1111 = 11 · 101, R₅ = 11 111 = 41 · 271, R₆ = 111 111 = 3 · 7 · 11 · 13 · 37 (factorisations confirmed by computer).

Divisibility. Rₐ divides R_{ab} for every a and b (since 10ᵃ − 1 divides 10^{ab} − 1), and gcd(Rₘ, Rₙ) = R_{gcd(m,n)}: for example gcd(R₁₂, R₁₈) = R₆. This is the same pattern as for Fibonacci numbers and for 2ⁿ − 1.

Repunits are never squares (beyond 1). For n ≥ 2, Rₙ ends in 11, so Rₙ ≡ 11 ≡ 3 (mod 4); but squares are 0 or 1 modulo 4. A check up to n = 39 confirms none of them is a perfect square. A similar argument shows no number whose digits are all 1 or all 7 (beyond single digits) is a square.

Uses. Numbers like 7777 = 7 · R₄, 999 = 9 · R₃ and 5 555 555 = 5 · R₇ reduce to repunits. To compute 11…1 modulo something, use Rₙ = (10ⁿ − 1)/9 carefully (division by 9 requires working modulo 9m).

Try it

Show that R₆ is divisible by 7.

Answer. R₆ = 111 111 = 111 · 1001 = 111 · 7 · 11 · 13.

4. Digit sums and digit problems

Digit sums of special numbers. 10⁵⁰ − 1 consists of fifty 9s, so its digit sum is 450. Multiples of 99…9 behave nicely: the digit sum of k · 99 999 for k up to 99 999 is always 45 (tested on many k): this is a surprising symmetry of the product of 10ⁿ − 1 with any k < 10ⁿ.

Digits and equations. Find all three-digit n equal to 11 times its digit sum. Writing n = 100a + 10b + c = 11(a + b + c) gives 89a − b − 10c = 0, whose only solution with digits is a = 1, b = 9, c = 8: n = 198 (a search over all three-digit numbers returns only 198). The key move is to turn the digit condition into an equation in a, b, c and use their ranges (0–9) to bound the solutions.

Reversals. If ab is a two-digit number and ba its reversal, ab − ba = 9(a − b), a multiple of 9. A four-digit palindrome abba equals 1001a + 110b = 11(91a + 10b), so it is divisible by 11; there are 90 of them (9 choices for a and 10 for b, confirmed by listing).

Digit sums and the digital root. The digital root (repeatedly sum digits until one digit remains) equals 1 + (n − 1) mod 9. For 2¹⁰⁰: 2¹⁰⁰ mod 9 = 7, so the digital root is 7.

Try it

What is the sum of all three-digit numbers made from the digits 1, 2, 3 each used once?

Answer. Each digit appears twice in each place: 2 · (1 + 2 + 3) · 111 = 1332.

5. Squares and their endings

Last digits of squares can only be 0, 1, 4, 5, 6, 9. So no square ends in 2, 3, 7 or 8. The last two digits of squares take only 22 possible values out of 100. A square ending in 5 always ends in 25, and a square ending in 1 or 9 has an even tens digit (verified for all n below 1000).

Use. To show that a number such as 2 222 223 is not a square, observe that it ends in 3. To show that a number like 4 444 444 + 1 is not a square, note that it is ≡ 1 + 44 ≡ 1 mod 4, so look instead modulo 8 or 16. The strategy: list the possible endings of squares in a few moduli and pick one that excludes your number.

Squares and 9. Squares have digital roots 1, 4, 7 or 9 (their remainders mod 9 are 0, 1, 4, 7). So a number with digit sum 12, say, cannot be a square (12 ≡ 3 mod 9).

Counting digits of squares. A number with k digits has a square with 2k − 1 or 2k digits; so the square of a 3-digit number has 5 or 6 digits.

Try it

Can a number whose digits are all 2 (such as 22 222) be a square?

Answer. It ends in 2, and squares never end in 2.

6. A longer problem

Problem. (a) Find the last two digits of 7²⁰²³. (b) How many digits has 2²⁰²³? (c) Show that the number R₂₀₂₂ = 111 … 1 (2022 ones) is divisible by 3 but not by 9, and is not a perfect square. (d) What is the digital root of 2²⁰²³?

Solution. (a) The last two digits of powers of 7 cycle 07, 49, 43, 01 with period 4; since 2023 ≡ 3 (mod 4), the answer is 43. (b) log₁₀ 2²⁰²³ = 2023 · 0.30103 ≈ 608.96, so 609 digits. (c) R₂₀₂₂ has digit sum 2022 = 3 · 674, divisible by 3, and 2022 ≡ 6 (mod 9), so it is not divisible by 9. It ends in 11, so it is ≡ 3 (mod 4) and cannot be a square. (d) 2²⁰²³ ≡ 2 (mod 9), so the digital root is 2.

Reflection. Reading a problem for the cheapest invariant (mod 4, mod 9, last digit, digit sum) is often enough. In part (c) the number 2022 matters: with 2023 ones the number would not even be divisible by 3, so always compute the digit sum before claiming divisibility.

7. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. (1) Applying the digit-sum rule for 9 to 11 (or the reverse). (2) Forgetting to reduce the exponent modulo the cycle length. (3) Starting the cycle at the wrong place (the cycle for the exponent 4 gives the fourth entry, not the first). (4) Using R_n = (10ⁿ − 1)/9 modulo m without clearing the division. (5) Believing 'a number is divisible by 9 iff digit sum is' implies it for 18 or 27 alone.

Checks. Compute the first few powers to see the cycle. Check divisibility rules on small examples. Check the number of digits with a rough logarithm.

How to practise. For each problem name the modulus you will use before computing. In Unlimited practice, rotate the last-digit, digit-sum and repunit families.

Answer5
