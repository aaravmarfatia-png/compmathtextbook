1. Rectangular boxes and distances in space

A rectangular box with edges a, b, c has volume abc, surface area 2(ab + bc + ca) and space diagonal √(a² + b² + c²). For a 3 × 4 × 5 box: volume 60, surface area 2(12 + 20 + 15) = 94, diagonal √50 = 5√2. The distance formula in three dimensions is the same as in two with an extra term: the distance from (0, 0, 0) to (3, 4, 12) is √(9 + 16 + 144) = 13. A cube of edge a has diagonal a√3.

Face diagonals and a path on the surface. A face diagonal is √(a² + b²). To find the shortest path between two points on the surface of a box, unfold the faces into a plane and use a straight line: the distance is computed with the Pythagorean theorem on the unfolded rectangle.

Try it

A box is 2 × 3 × 6. Find the length of its space diagonal.

Answer. √(4 + 9 + 36) = 7.

2. Prisms, cylinders, pyramids, cones

Prisms and cylinders: volume = base area × height. A cylinder with r = 3 and h = 5 has volume 45π and total surface area 2πr(r + h) = 48π. Pyramids and cones: volume = ⅓ × base area × height. A square pyramid with base side 6 and height 4 has volume 48. A cone with r = 3 and h = 4 has slant height √(9 + 16) = 5, volume 12π and total surface area πr(r + l) = 24π.

Why ⅓? Three congruent pyramids make a cube when placed with a common apex at one vertex, so each has volume ⅓ of the cube: ⅓ · base · height. The cone is the limiting case of a pyramid.

Frustum thinking. The part of a cone left after slicing off the tip can be handled by subtracting the volume of the small cone (similar to the large one, with volume ratio k³ for scale factor k).

Try it

A cone has radius 6 and height 8. Find its slant height and volume.

Answer. Slant 10, volume ⅓π · 36 · 8 = 96π.

3. Spheres and the Platonic solids

A sphere of radius r has volume (4/3)πr³ and surface area 4πr². With r = 3: volume 36π, surface area 36π (equal numerically, a coincidence of r = 3). A sphere inscribed in a cylinder (touching top, bottom and side) fills exactly 2/3 of the cylinder's volume: (4/3)πr³ ÷ 2πr³ = 2/3.

Regular tetrahedron of edge a: volume a³√2/12; for a = 6 this is 18√2. Regular octahedron of edge √2 (formed by joining face centres of a cube of side 2 — its vertices are (±1, 0, 0), (0, ±1, 0), (0, 0, ±1)): volume (√2/3)a³ = 4/3, one sixth of the cube. Cube diagonals: the sphere circumscribing a cube of side a has radius a√3/2; the inscribed sphere has radius a/2.

Try it

A cube has edge 4. Find the radius of its circumscribed sphere.

Answer. 4√3/2 = 2√3.

4. A longer problem

Problem. A cone with radius 5 and height 12 is cut by a plane parallel to its base at height 4 from the base. Find the volume of the frustum.

Solution. The slant height is 13. The cone above the cut has height 8 and radius 5 · 8/12 = 10/3. The big cone has volume ⅓π · 25 · 12 = 100π; the small one has ⅓π · (100/9) · 8 = 800π/27. The frustum has volume 100π − 800π/27 = 1900π/27.

Reflection. Scaling gives a faster check: the small cone has volume (8/12)³ · 100π = (8/27) · 100π = 800π/27 ✓.

5. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. (1) Forgetting the ⅓ for pyramids and cones. (2) Using the slant height where the vertical height is needed (and the reverse). (3) Confusing lateral and total surface area. (4) Scaling volume by k instead of k³.

Checks. Check units (length, area, volume). Test extreme cases (height 0). Compare a cone with a cylinder of the same base and height: it holds one third.

How to practise. Sketch the solid and mark every known length. In Unlimited practice, rotate the volume and surface-area families.

Answer18√2
Method: Cube

A regular tetrahedron of edge 6 fits in a cube of side 3√2 using alternate vertices. The cube has volume (3√2)³ = 54√2, and four corner pieces each take 54√2/6 = 9√2. The tetrahedron is 54√2 − 36√2 = 18√2.

V = a³/(6√2) = 216/(6√2) = 36/√2 = 18√2.
