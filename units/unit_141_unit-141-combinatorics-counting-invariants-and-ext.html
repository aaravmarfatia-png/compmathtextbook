Reading

1. Counting with structure

Derangements. D₄ = 9, D₅ = 44. Catalan. Cₙ = C(2n, n)/(n + 1): C₃ = 5 paths from (0,0) to (3,3) staying on or below y = x. Pigeonhole. A set with no two elements differing by 1 or 2 takes at most one number from each block of three consecutive integers, so inside {1,…,9} it has at most 3. Mantel. A triangle-free graph on n vertices has at most ⌊n²/4⌋ edges, achieved by a complete bipartite graph.

Try it

D₃?

Answer. 2.

Try it

Largest triangle-free graph on 4 vertices?

Answer. 4 edges (the square).

2. Invariants and parity

Lockers toggled by every divisor: locker k ends open iff k has an odd number of divisors, i.e. is a perfect square, so 10 of 100 remain open. Σ C(n,k)² = C(2n, n): for n = 3, 1 + 9 + 9 + 1 = 20 = C(6,3) ✓. Bell numbers count set partitions: B₃ = 5, B₄ = 15.

Try it

How many lockers out of 36 end open?

Answer. 6.

Try it

Σ C(4,k)²?

Answer. 70.

3. A worked problem

Problem. How many permutations of 1..5 have no fixed point? Solution. 5!(1 − 1 + 1/2 − 1/6 + 1/24 − 1/120) = 120 − 120 + 60 − 20 + 5 − 1 = 44.

Check. D₅ = 4(D₄ + D₃) = 4(9 + 2) = 44 ✓.

4. In the books

Ch. 36 proves Turán's theorem, which contains Mantel's bound of ⌊n²/4⌋ edges for a triangle-free graph (the case I use). Ch. 25 covers pigeon-hole and double counting, ch. 30 Cayley's formula.

Olympiad Treasures 3.3 is “Count Twice!”. Khurmi Example 3.1.1 is the locker (jail doors) problem.

Honest note. The unit is an introduction. Pigeonhole and double counting are covered only briefly.

Problem p1. How many permutations of 1, …, 4 have no fixed point?

HintUse inclusion–exclusion or the recurrence D_n = (n − 1)(D_(n−1) + D_(n−2)).
Answer9
Method: Inclusion–exclusion

D_4 = Σ(−1)^k n!/k! = 9.

Method: Recurrence

D_n = (n − 1)(D_(n−1) + D_(n−2)) with D₂ = 1, D₃ = 2 gives D_4 = 9.

Source: Technique from IMO Compendium §2.5, Aigner & Ziegler ch. 25, 36 (original problem)

Problem p5. How many lattice paths from (0,0) to (4,4) use steps (1,0) and (0,1) and never go above the line y = x?

HintThese are Dyck-path counts (Catalan numbers).
Answer14
Method: Formula

C(8,4)/(4 + 1) = 14.

Method: Recursion

Split by the first return to the diagonal: Cₙ = Σ C_i C_(n−1−i) = 14.

Source: Technique from IMO Compendium §2.5, Aigner & Ziegler ch. 25, 36 (original problem)

Problem p8. In how many ways can 5 different books be split into non-empty unlabelled piles?

HintThese are Bell numbers: Bₙ = Σ C(n − 1, k) B_k.
Answer52
Method: Bell recurrence

B_(n+1) = Σ C(n, k)B_k gives B_5 = 52.

Method: Stirling numbers

Sum the numbers of ways to make k piles for k = 1..5: 52.

Source: Technique from IMO Compendium §2.5, Aigner & Ziegler ch. 25, 36 (original problem)

Problem p11. What is the largest subset of {1, …, 15} in which no two elements differ by 1 or 2?

HintLook at blocks of three consecutive numbers.
Answer5
Method: Blocks

Each block of 3 consecutive numbers holds at most one element; 15 numbers form ⌈15/3⌉ = 5 blocks, and 1, 4, 7, … achieves it.

Method: Construction

Take 1, 4, 7, …: that gives 5 numbers, and no larger set can exist by the block bound.

Source: Technique from IMO Compendium §2.5, Aigner & Ziegler ch. 25, 36 (original problem)

Problem p14. Lockers 1, …, 100 start closed. For k = 1, 2, …, 100, person k toggles every kth locker. How many lockers end open?

HintA locker is toggled once per divisor of its number.
Answer10
Method: Divisors

A locker is open iff its number has an odd number of divisors, i.e. is a perfect square: 10 of them.

Method: Simulation

Carry out the toggles; exactly 10 lockers are open.

Source: Technique from IMO Compendium §2.5, Aigner & Ziegler ch. 25, 36 (original problem)

Problem p17. What is the maximum number of edges in a graph on 5 vertices with no triangle?

HintMantel's theorem: at most ⌊n²/4⌋; a complete bipartite graph achieves it.
Answer6
Method: Bipartite example

K_(2,3) has 2·3 = 6 edges and no triangle.

Method: Degree bound

For an edge uv the neighbours of u and v are disjoint so deg u + deg v ≤ 5; summing over edges gives the bound 6.

Source: Technique from IMO Compendium §2.5, Aigner & Ziegler ch. 25, 36 (original problem)

Problem p24. How many lattice paths from (0,0) to (4,4) with steps right and up pass through (2,2)?

HintSplit the path at the point.
Answer36
Method: Multiply

C(4,2)·C(4,2) = 36.

Method: Count by sections

Paths to (2,2): 6; paths from there to (4,4): 6; product 36.
