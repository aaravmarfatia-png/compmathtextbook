Reading

1. Linear algebra tools

For a 2×2 matrix the eigenvalues satisfy λ₁ + λ₂ = trace and λ₁λ₂ = determinant, so λ₁² + λ₂² = tr² − 2det: for [[3,1],[2,4]] that is 49 − 20 = 29. Powers of [[1,1],[1,0]] contain Fibonacci numbers: M⁵ = [[8,5],[5,3]]. The number of invertible 2×2 matrices mod p is (p² − 1)(p² − p); for p = 2 it is 3 · 2 = 6.

Try it

tr and det of [[2,0],[1,3]]?

Answer. 5 and 6.

Try it

Top-left entry of M⁴?

Answer. F₅ = 5.

2. Series, integrals and binomial sums

Σ n xⁿ = x/(1 − x)²; at x = 1/2 this is 2. The Beta integral gives ∫₀¹ x²(1 − x) dx = 2!1!/4! = 1/12 (also 1/3 − 1/4 = 1/12 ✓). Σ k C(n,k) = n2ⁿ⁻¹ and Σ(−1)ᵏ C(n,k)(n − k)ⁿ = n!: for n = 3, 27 − 3·8 + 3·1 − 0 = 6 ✓.

Answer. 3/4.

Try it

Answer. 32.

3. A worked problem

Problem. Find the determinant of [[2,1],[3,4]]³ without multiplying matrices. Solution. det(M³) = (det M)³ = 5³ = 125.

Check. det M = 8 − 3 = 5 ✓; M² = [[7,6],[18,19]] has determinant 133 − 108 = 25 = 5² ✓.

Problem p8. The matrix [[3,1],[2,4]] has eigenvalues λ₁ and λ₂. Find λ₁² + λ₂².

HintThe sum of the eigenvalues is the trace and the product is the determinant.
Answer29
Method: Trace and determinant

λ₁ + λ₂ = 7 and λ₁λ₂ = 10, so λ₁² + λ₂² = 49 − 20 = 29.

Method: Solve the characteristic equation

λ² − 7λ + 10 = 0 gives the two eigenvalues; their squares add to 29.

Source: Technique from Gelca & Andreescu 2.3 and 3.1–3.2 (original problem)

Problem p11. Find Σ_{n=1}^∞ n/2^n. (fraction)

HintDifferentiate the geometric series Σ xⁿ = x/(1 − x).
Answer2
Method: Differentiate

With x = 1/2: Σ n xⁿ = x/(1 − x)² = 2.

Method: Shift trick

S = Σ n/2^n satisfies 2S − S = Σ 1/2^n·(…) ; solving gives S = 2.

Source: Technique from Gelca & Andreescu 2.3 and 3.1–3.2 (original problem)

Problem p20. Compute Σ k·C(8, k) for k = 0, …, 8.

HintUse k·C(n,k) = n·C(n − 1, k − 1), or differentiate (1 + x)^8.
Answer1024
Method: Differentiate

d/dx(1 + x)^8 at x = 1 is 8·2^7 = 1024.

Method: Symmetry

k and n − k pair up with the same coefficient, so the average k is 8/2 and the total is 8·2^8/2 = 1024.

Source: Technique from Gelca & Andreescu 2.3 and 3.1–3.2 (original problem)
