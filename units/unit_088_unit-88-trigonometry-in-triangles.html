Reading

1. Trigonometric ratios and identities

In a right triangle with acute angle θ, sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse, cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse, tan θ = opposite/adjacent. Values worth knowing: sin 30° = 1/2, sin 45° = √2/2, sin 60° = √3/2; cos 30° = √3/2, cos 60° = 1/2; tan 45° = 1. The identity sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 follows from Pythagoras.

Sum formulas. sin(A + B) = sin A cos B + cos A sin B; cos(A + B) = cos A cos B − sin A sin B; tan(A + B) = (tan A + tan B)/(1 − tan A tan B). Double angle: sin 2A = 2 sin A cos A, cos 2A = cos²A − sin²A = 1 − 2sin²A.

Worked example 1. sin 75° = sin(45° + 30°) = (√2/2)(√3/2) + (√2/2)(1/2) = (√6 + √2)/4 ≈ 0.9659.

Worked example 2. tan 75° = (1 + 1/√3)/(1 − 1/√3) = (√3 + 1)/(√3 − 1) = (√3 + 1)²/2 = 2 + √3.

Worked example 3. cos 36° = (1 + √5)/4 ≈ 0.809. It comes from the regular pentagon (the diagonal–side ratio is the golden ratio).

2. Triangle laws

The laws of sines and cosines (Unit 51) solve triangles. The area is ½ab sin C. In any triangle A + B + C = 180°, so sin C = sin(A + B) and tan A + tan B + tan C = tan A tan B tan C (for non-right triangles).

Worked example 1. A triangle with angles 45° and 60° and the side opposite 45° equal to 10 has the side opposite 60° equal to 10 sin 60°/sin 45° = 10 × (√3/2)/(√2/2) = 5√6 ≈ 12.25.

Worked example 2. In triangle ABC, sin A = 3/5 and cos B = 5/13 (acute angles). Then cos A = 4/5 and sin B = 12/13, so sin C = sin(A + B) = (3/5)(5/13) + (4/5)(12/13) = 15/65 + 48/65 = 63/65.

Heights and distances. From a point 50 m from the base of a tower the angle of elevation of the top is 30°. The tower height is 50 tan 30° = 50/√3 ≈ 28.9 m.

3. Solving trigonometric equations

To solve sin x = 1/2 for 0° ≤ x ≤ 360°: x = 30° or 150°. For cos 2x = 1/2: 2x = 60°, 300°, 420°, 660°, so x = 30°, 150°, 210°, 330°. Quadratic-type equations: 2sin²x − sin x − 1 = 0 gives (2 sin x + 1)(sin x − 1) = 0, so sin x = 1 or −1/2: x = 90°, 210°, 330°.

Bounds. |sin x| ≤ 1 and |cos x| ≤ 1. The maximum of 3 sin x + 4 cos x is √(9 + 16) = 5 (Cauchy–Schwarz, Unit 14), attained when tan x = 3/4.

Worked example. Is sin x + cos x ever equal to 2? No: its maximum is √2 (at x = 45°).

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. Working in degrees on a radian calculator. Forgetting the second solution of sin x = k. Using sin(A + B) = sin A + sin B. Sign errors in cos(A + B).

Checks. Test identities with simple angles like 30° and 60°.

Practice. Memorise the special angle table.

Worked examples with Try-its: Sines, cosines and area

Law of sines: a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C = 2R. Law of cosines: c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C. Area: ½ab sin C. A triangle with sides 5 and 8 enclosing 60° has third side √(25 + 64 − 40) = 7 and area ½ · 40 · (√3/2) = 10√3. In the 7-8-9 triangle cos A = (64 + 81 − 49)/144 = 2/3, and in the 13-14-15 triangle cos A = 33/65.

Try it

Find the third side of a triangle with sides 3 and 5 enclosing 120°.

Answer. √(9 + 25 + 15) = 7.

Worked examples with Try-its: Using the laws

Use the law of sines when you know an angle and its opposite side; use the law of cosines when you know two sides and the included angle, or all three sides. The ambiguous case (two sides and a non-included angle) can give two triangles, one, or none. Always check that the three angles sum to 180°.

Try it

In a triangle a = 10, A = 30° and B = 45°. Find b.

Answer. b = 10 sin 45°/sin 30° = 10√2.

Worked examples with Try-its: A longer problem

Problem. In triangle ABC, a = 7, b = 8 and C = 60°. Find c and the circumradius. Solution. c² = 49 + 64 − 56 = 57, so c = √57. Then R = c/(2 sin 60°) = √57/√3 = √19.

Reflection. The circumradius follows from any side and its opposite angle.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Area. Sides 6 and 7 enclosing 30° give area ½ · 6 · 7 · ½ = 10.5. Circumradius. If a = 10 and A = 30° then R = a/(2 sin A) = 10. Largest angle. In the 4-5-6 triangle the angle opposite 6 has cosine (16 + 25 − 36)/(2 · 4 · 5) = 1/8, about 82.8°.

Try it

Find the area of a triangle with sides 4, 5 and included angle 150°.

Answer. ½ · 4 · 5 · ½ = 5.

Try it

In a triangle with a = 7, b = 8, c = 9, find cos C.

Answer. (49 + 64 − 81)/112 = 2/7.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. Two ships leave a port at the same time, one going 12 km/h on a bearing of 0° and the other 16 km/h on a bearing of 60°. How far apart are they after 2 hours? Solution. The distances are 24 and 32 with 60° between them, so d² = 576 + 1024 − 2 · 24 · 32 · ½ = 832 and d = √832 = 8√13 ≈ 28.8 km.

Check. √832 ≈ 28.84.

Problem p1. A triangle has sides 5 and 8 with a 60° angle between them. Find the third side.

HintLaw of cosines.
Answer7
Method: Cosines

c² = 25 + 64 − 2·5·8·(1/2) = 49, so c = 7.

Method: Coordinates

Place the 8-side along the x-axis and the 5-side at 60°: its end is (5/2, 5√3/2), whose distance from (8, 0) is √((11/2)² + (5√3/2)²) = √(121/4 + 75/4) = 7.

Source: AoPS Volume 1, trigonometry chapters; Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 1

Problem p2. In a triangle, side a = 10 is opposite angle A = 30°. Find the circumradius R.

Hinta/sin A = 2R.
Answer10
Method: Sines

10/(1/2) = 20 = 2R so R = 10.

Method: Central angle

Side a subtends a central angle of 60°, forming an equilateral triangle with the radii, so R = a = 10.

Problem p3. Find the area of a triangle with sides 6 and 10 and included angle 30°.

Hint(1/2) ab sin C.
Answer15
Method: Formula

Method: Height

The height on the side 10 is 6 sin 30° = 3, so the area is (1/2)(10)(3) = 15.

Problem p9. How many solutions does sin x = 1/2 have for x in [0°, 360°)?

Answer2

sin(180° − x) = sin x, so the second solution is 180° − 30° = 150°.

Problem p15. Find the circumradius of the triangle with sides 13, 14 and 15.

HintR = abc/(4K) with K = 84.
Answer65/8
Method: Formula

R = 13 · 14 · 15/(4 · 84) = 2730/336 = 65/8.

The altitude to side 14 is 12, so sin of the angle opposite 13 is 12/15 = 4/5. Then 2R = 13/(4/5) = 65/4, so R = 65/8.

Problem p16. In triangle ABC, a = 8 is opposite A with sin A = 1/2, and sin B = 3/4. Find b.

HintLaw of sines.
Answer12
Method: Law of sines

b/sin B = a/sin A, so b = 8 · (3/4)/(1/2) = 12.

Method: Ratio

sin B is 3/2 times sin A, so b is 3/2 times a.

Problem p17. Evaluate sin 150° + cos 120°.

HintReference angles.
Answer0
Method: Values

sin 150° = 1/2 and cos 120° = −1/2, so the sum is 0.

Method: Unit circle

150° and 30° share the same height, and 120° and 60° have opposite x-coordinates in sign.
