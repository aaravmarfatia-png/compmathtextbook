Reading

1. Dissections

A dissection cuts a figure into pieces that can be rearranged or counted. Area is preserved, so dissections prove area facts and bound the number of pieces. A rectangle of size 13 × 8 (area 104) cannot be rearranged into 10 × 10 (area 100); fake dissection paradoxes hide a thin sliver.

Worked example. A square can be cut into 4 smaller squares (a 2 × 2 grid). Cutting any one square into four adds 3 to the count, so 4, 7, 10, 13, … are possible. A 3 × 3 grid with a 2 × 2 block merged gives 6 squares, and then 6, 9, 12, … are possible. Two squares or three squares are impossible, which is a good small case to examine.

Triangle cut by a line parallel to a side. The little triangle is similar, with area equal to (ratio)² times the whole.

2. Centres of mass

The centre of mass of point masses m_i at positions P_i is (Σ m_i P_i)/(Σ m_i). It lets you replace many pieces by a single heavier point. For equal masses at the vertices of a triangle, the centre is the centroid (A + B + C)/3, which is the intersection of the medians.

Worked example 1. The triangle with vertices (0, 0), (6, 0), (0, 9) has centroid (2, 3).

Worked example 2 (an L-shape). An L-shape consists of a 3 × 1 rectangle [0, 3] × [0, 1] (area 3, centre (1.5, 0.5)) and a 1 × 2 rectangle [0, 1] × [1, 3] (area 2, centre (0.5, 2)). The centre of mass is x = (3 × 1.5 + 2 × 0.5)/5 = 1.1, y = (3 × 0.5 + 2 × 2)/5 = 1.1, as symmetry across the line y = x predicts.

Mass points. To find ratios in a triangle, assign masses so that each cevian balances; the ratios of segments are inverse to the masses at the ends.

3. Solids in boxes

Many solids are best seen inside a cube or box. A regular tetrahedron of edge a√2 is inscribed in a cube of edge a by joining four alternate vertices. The cube minus four corner tetrahedra (each with legs a, a, a and volume a³/6) leaves a³ − 4a³/6 = a³/3. So the regular tetrahedron with edge e has volume e³/(6√2) = e³√2/12.

Worked example. A regular tetrahedron has edge 1. It fits in a cube of edge 1/√2, so its volume is (1/√2)³/3 = 1/(6√2) = √2/12 ≈ 0.1179.

The corner of a cube. The tetrahedron cut off by slicing the corner through the three adjacent vertices has volume 1/6 of the cube, and the sliced face is an equilateral triangle.

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. Averaging positions instead of weighting by area or mass. Putting the centre of mass of a triangle half way along a median (it is 1/3 of the way from the base). Forgetting a hole has negative mass.

Checks. A symmetric figure's centre lies on its axis.

Practice. Embed solids in a cube, then subtract pieces.

Worked examples with Try-its: Dissections

A dissection cuts a figure into pieces that can be rearranged. A cube of side 2 splits into six congruent pyramids with apex at the centre; each has base 4 and height 1, so volume ⅓ · 4 · 1 = 4/3 and six of them give 8. A triangle can be cut into 4 congruent similar triangles by joining the midpoints of its sides.

Try it

What is the volume of a pyramid with a square base of side 6 and height 5?

Answer. ⅓ · 36 · 5 = 60.

Worked examples with Try-its: Centres of mass

The centroid of a system is the weighted average of the pieces' centroids. An L-shape made from a 4 × 4 square (centroid (2, 2), area 16) with a 2 × 2 corner [2, 4] × [2, 4] removed (centroid (3, 3), area 4) has area 12 and centroid ((16 · 2 − 4 · 3)/12, (16 · 2 − 4 · 3)/12) = (5/3, 5/3).

Try it

Two masses of 3 and 1 at positions 0 and 8 on a line: where is the centre of mass?

Answer. (3 · 0 + 1 · 8)/4 = 2.

Worked examples with Try-its: A longer problem

Problem. A sphere is inscribed in a cube of side 2. What fraction of the cube does it fill? Solution. Sphere volume (4/3)π · 1³ = 4π/3, cube 8, ratio π/6 ≈ 0.5236.

Reflection. Name the dimensions of the solids first, then compute.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Box diagonal. A 3 × 4 × 12 box has space diagonal √(9 + 16 + 144) = 13. Centroid. The triangle with vertices (0, 0), (6, 0), (0, 6) has centroid (2, 2). Cube in a sphere. The largest cube inside a sphere of radius 3 has space diagonal 6, so its side is 6/√3 = 2√3.

Try it

A cube has side 4. Find the length of its space diagonal.

Answer. 4√3.

Try it

Find the centre of mass of masses 1, 2, 3 at positions 0, 1, 2 on a line.

Answer. (0 + 2 + 6)/6 = 4/3.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. A rectangular box has face areas 12, 15 and 20. Find its volume. Solution. If the edges are a, b, c then ab = 12, bc = 15, ca = 20, so (abc)² = 12 · 15 · 20 = 3600 and abc = 60. The edges are 3, 4 and 5.

Check. 3 · 4 = 12, 4 · 5 = 20, 3 · 5 = 15 ✓.

Problem p1. A 6 × 4 rectangle is cut into squares of side 2. How many squares are there?

HintCompare areas.
Answer6
Method: Areas

24/4 = 6.

Method: Grid

3 squares along the length and 2 across: 6.

Problem p2. A regular tetrahedron is formed by four alternate vertices of a cube of side 6. Find its volume.

HintIt is one third of the cube.
Answer72
Method: Subtract corners

Cut four right-triangular corner pyramids from the cube, each with volume (1/6)·6³ = 36. The remaining tetrahedron has volume 216 − 4 · 36 = 72.

Method: Fraction

The regular tetrahedron has 1/3 of the cube's volume: 216/3 = 72.

Source: Andreescu & Gelca, 1.4, 1.7, 1.8

Problem p3. Masses of 2 kg at x = 0 and 3 kg at x = 10 sit on a rod. Where is the balance point?

HintWeighted average.
Answer6
Method: Weighted

(2 · 0 + 3 · 10)/5 = 6.

Method: Lever

2 · 6 = 3 · 4, so the torques balance.
