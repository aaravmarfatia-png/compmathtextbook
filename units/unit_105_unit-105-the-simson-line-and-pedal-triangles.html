Unit 105: Unit 105: The Simson line and pedal triangles

Book: Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 2; Chen ch. 1 (25 problems)
Coxeter & Greitzer, Geometry Revisited, chapter 2 (the Simson line) and Chen, Euclidean Geometry in Mathematical Olympiads, chapter 1, worked in coordinates on the circle x² + y² = 25 with A = (5, 0), B = (0, 5), C = (−3, −4).

Reading

1. The Simson line

Let ABC be a triangle and P a point. Drop perpendiculars from P to the three side lines, with feet D, E, F. The Simson line theorem: D, E, F are collinear if and only if P lies on the circumcircle of ABC. The line is the Simson line of P.

Worked example. Take A = (0, 0), B = (4, 0), C = (0, 3). The circumcentre is (2, 1.5) with radius 2.5. The point P = (4, 3) satisfies (4 − 2)² + (3 − 1.5)² = 4 + 2.25 = 6.25, so it is on the circumcircle. The feet: on AB (y = 0): (4, 0); on AC (x = 0): (0, 3); on BC (3x + 4y = 12): P − (12/25)(3, 4) = (2.56, 1.08). The first two lie on 3x + 4y = 12, and (2.56, 1.08) gives 7.68 + 4.32 = 12 ✓. They are collinear.

Why it works. The cyclic quadrilaterals formed with the feet give equal angles that add up to a straight angle (Unit 96 on angle chasing).

2. The pedal triangle

For a point P the triangle formed by the three feet of perpendiculars is the pedal triangle. It degenerates exactly when P is on the circumcircle. Its area is K(R² − OP²)/(4R²), where K is the area of ABC, R the circumradius and O the circumcentre.

Worked example. If P = O, the pedal triangle is the medial triangle and the formula gives K/4 ✓. If P is the orthocentre of an acute triangle, the pedal triangle is the orthic triangle. If P is on the circle, OP = R and the area is 0 ✓ (the Simson line).

Side lengths. The side of the pedal triangle opposite the foot on BC equals AP sin A.

3. Using these ideas

The Simson line helps with problems like 'the feet of three perpendiculars are collinear; prove P lies on the circle', and the area formula turns questions about the maximum area of a pedal triangle into 'put P at O' (largest area K/4 when P is inside the circle).

Worked example. A triangle has area 84 and circumradius 8.125 (the 13-14-15 triangle). For P at distance 5 from O the pedal area is 84 × (8.125² − 25)/(4 × 8.125²) = 84 × (66.02 − 25)/(264.06) = 84 × 0.1553 = 13.05.

The Simson line bisects the segment joining P to the orthocentre H.

4. Mistakes, checks and how to practise

Common mistakes. Dropping perpendiculars to the segments instead of the lines (the feet can be outside the sides). Forgetting that the theorem is an if-and-only-if.

Checks. Always test with a right triangle where coordinates are easy.

Practice. Draw the figure large and compute feet with coordinates once.

Worked examples with Try-its: The Simson line

If P lies on the circumcircle of ABC, the feet of the perpendiculars from P to the three side lines are collinear: this is the Simson line. For the 13-14-15 triangle with P the point diametrically opposite A, namely (9, −15/4), the three feet are collinear (computed exactly with rational numbers). The converse also holds: if the feet are collinear, P is on the circumcircle.

Try it

Where do the feet of the perpendiculars from P lie when P is a vertex of the triangle?

Answer. Two feet coincide with P itself, and the third is the foot of the altitude, so the line is the altitude from that vertex.

Worked examples with Try-its: Pedal triangles

The triangle formed by the three feet is the pedal triangle of P. Its area is |R² − OP²|/(4R²) times the area of ABC. When P = O this gives 1/4: the pedal triangle is the medial triangle, with area 84/4 = 21 in the 13-14-15 triangle. When P is on the circumcircle the area is 0, which is the Simson line again.

Try it

A triangle has area 60 and circumradius 10. What is the area of the pedal triangle of a point at distance 6 from the circumcentre?

Answer. |100 − 36|/400 · 60 = 9.6.

Worked examples with Try-its: A longer problem

Problem. In the 13-14-15 triangle let P be the centroid G. What is the area of its pedal triangle using the formula? Solution. OG² = (7 − 19/3)² + (33/8 − 4)² = 4/9 + 1/64 = 265/576. R² = 4225/64. The ratio is (4225/64 − 265/576)/(4 · 4225/64) = (38025/576 − 265/576)/(4225/16) = (37760/576)/(4225/16) = 37760 · 16/(576 · 4225) = 604160/2433600 = 0.2482, so the area is about 0.2482 · 84 ≈ 20.85.

Reflection. The pedal triangle of G is slightly smaller than the medial triangle (21), as OG is small but non-zero.

More worked examples: Worked examples

Right triangle. Take A = (0, 0), B = (4, 0), C = (0, 3); the circumcircle has centre (2, 3/2) and radius 5/2, and P = (4, 3) lies on it. The feet of the perpendiculars from P to the lines AB and AC are B = (4, 0) and C = (0, 3), and the foot on BC lies on line BC, so all three feet lie on the line BC: this is the Simson line of P, as the theorem predicts.

Try it

What is the Simson line of a point P that coincides with a vertex A?

Answer. The altitude from A.

Try it

Do the feet of the perpendiculars from the circumcentre O to the three sides lie on one line?

Answer. No: they are the midpoints of the sides, which form the medial triangle of positive area. The theorem needs P on the circle.

More worked examples: A second longer problem

Problem. Show that the pedal triangle of the circumcentre is the medial triangle. Solution. The foot of the perpendicular from O to BC is the midpoint of BC, since the perpendicular from the centre bisects a chord. The same holds for the other sides.

Reflection. The pedal triangle of O has area 1/4 of the triangle, matching the formula (R² − OP²)/(4R²) with OP = 0.

Practice problems (25)

Problem p1. Triangle A = (5, 0), B = (0, 5), C = (−3, −4) is inscribed in x² + y² = 25. Find its area.

HintHalf the cross product.
Answer30
Method: Cross product

|(0 − 5)(−4 − 0) − (−3 − 5)(5 − 0)|/2 = |20 + 40|/2 = 30.

Method: Shoelace

(5·5 + 0·(−4) + (−3)·0) − (0·0 + 5·(−3) + (−4)·5) = 25 + 35 = 60, halved to 30.

Source: Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 2; Chen ch. 1

Problem p2. Find the area of the pedal triangle of the circumcentre O = (0, 0) of that triangle, as a fraction.

HintUse the area formula with OP = 0.
Answer15/2
Method: Formula

(25 − 0)/100 · 30 = 15/2.

Method: Medial triangle

The feet from O are the midpoints of the sides, so the pedal triangle is the medial triangle with a quarter of the area: 30/4 = 15/2.

Source: Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 2; Chen ch. 1

Problem p7. Find the area of the pedal triangle of the centroid of ABC, as a fraction.

HintFind the centroid first, then use the area formula.
Answer22/3
Method: Computation

The centroid is ((5 + 0 − 3)/3, (0 + 5 − 4)/3) = (2/3, 1/3), so OP² = 4/9 + 1/9 = 5/9. The formula gives (25 − 5/9)/100 · 30 = (220/9)(30/100) = 22/3.

Method: Check

Computing the three feet with the projection formula and applying the shoelace formula gives the same 22/3.

Source: Coxeter & Greitzer ch. 2; Chen ch. 1

Problem p9. Find the slope of the Simson line of P = (−5, 0), as a fraction.

HintCompute two of the feet.
Answer3
Method: Feet

P is the antipode of A, so PB ⊥ AB and the foot on AB is B = (0, 5). The foot on BC is (−2, −1). The slope through these two feet is (5 − (−1))/(0 − (−2)) = 3.

Method: Check

The third foot lies on the same line, since P is on the circle.

Problem p14. From a point P with PA = 10, the feet of perpendiculars to AB and AC are Q and R, and sin A = 3/5. Find QR.

Answer6

In the circle of diameter PA = 10, the chord QR subtends angle A, so QR = 10 sin A = 6.

Problem x18. Find the area of the triangle with sides 7, 24 and 25.

HintHeron's formula.
Answer84
Method: Heron

s = 28, so K = √(28 · 21 · 4 · 3) = 84.

Method: Altitude

The altitude to the side 25 is 2K/25 = 168/25, so the area is ½ · 25 · 168/25 = 84.
