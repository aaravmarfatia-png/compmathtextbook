1. Roots, coefficients and remainders

If t³ − 6t² + 11t − 6 has roots a, b, c then a + b + c = 6, ab + bc + ca = 11 and abc = 6, so a² + b² + c² = 36 − 22 = 14. The Remainder Theorem says that P(x) divided by x − a leaves P(a). For a divisor (x − a)(x − b) the remainder is the linear polynomial through the points (a, P(a)) and (b, P(b)).

Interpolation. A polynomial of degree at most n is determined by n + 1 values: the quadratic taking 1, 3, 7 at x = 0, 1, 2 is x² + x + 1, whose value at 4 is 21. Newton sums. pₖ = e₁pₖ₋₁ − e₂pₖ₋₂ + e₃pₖ₋₃ gives the power sums of the roots without finding them.

Try it

Find the remainder when x⁵ − 1 is divided by x − 2.

Answer. 31.

Try it

If a, b are roots of t² − 5t + 3, find a² + b².

Answer. 25 − 6 = 19.

2. Roots of unity and cyclotomic structure

The polynomial xⁿ − 1 has the n-th roots of unity as its roots, so gcd(xᵐ − 1, xⁿ − 1) = x^gcd(m,n) − 1: for m = 12 and n = 18 the gcd has degree 6. For P(x) = (2x − 1)ⁿ the sum of the coefficients of even powers is (P(1) + P(−1))/2; for n = 5 that is (1 − 243)/2 = −121. Over the integers mod p, the number of monic irreducible quadratics is (p² − p)/2: 3 for p = 3, 10 for p = 5.

Rational root test. A rational root u/v (lowest terms) of an integer polynomial has u dividing the constant term and v dividing the leading coefficient; x³ − 7x + 6 has the integer roots 1, 2, −3.

Try it

Degree of gcd(x⁸ − 1, x¹² − 1)?

Answer. 4.

Try it

Roots of x³ − 7x + 6?

Answer. 1, 2 and −3.

3. A worked problem

Problem. A monic cubic P has P(1) = 1, P(2) = 2 and P(3) = 3. Find P(4). Solution. P(x) − x vanishes at 1, 2, 3 and is monic of degree 3, so P(x) − x = (x − 1)(x − 2)(x − 3) and P(4) = 4 + 6 = 10.

Check. P(x) = x³ − 6x² + 12x − 6; P(1) = 1, P(2) = 8 − 24 + 24 − 6 = 2, P(3) = 27 − 54 + 36 − 6 = 3, P(4) = 64 − 96 + 48 − 6 = 10 ✓.

Problem p1. The roots of t³ − 6t² + 11t − 6 are a, b, c. Find a² + b² + c².

HintUse Vieta: a² + b² + c² = (a + b + c)² − 2(ab + bc + ca).
Answer14
Method: Vieta

a + b + c = 6 and ab + bc + ca = 11, so a² + b² + c² = 36 − 22 = 14.

Method: Find the roots

The roots are 1, 2, 3, and their squares add to 14.

Problem p5. Find the value at x = 10 of the remainder when x^5 + 3x² − x + 5 is divided by (x − 1)(x − 2).

HintThe remainder is linear; find it from the values of the polynomial at x = 1 and x = 2.
Answer359
Method: Interpolate

P(1) = 8 and P(2) = 47, so the remainder is the line through these points; at x = 10 it equals 359.

Method: Division

Writing the remainder as mx + k with m = 39 and k = -31 gives 359 at x = 10.

Problem p9. A polynomial of degree at most 2 takes the values 1, 3, 7 at x = 0, 1, 2. What is its value at x = 4?

HintUse Lagrange interpolation or finite differences.
Answer21
Method: Lagrange

The unique quadratic is x**2 + x + 1, which equals 21 at x = 4.

Method: Finite differences

The second difference is constant, so extending the table gives 21 at x = 4.

Problem p12. What is the degree of the greatest common divisor of x^12 − 1 and x^18 − 1?

HintThe common roots are the roots of unity whose order divides both 12 and 18.
Answer6
Method: Roots of unity

A complex number is a root of both exactly when its order divides gcd(12, 18) = 6, so the gcd has degree 6.

Method: Euclid for polynomials

Division of x^m − 1 by x^n − 1 leaves x^(m−n) − 1 behind, mirroring the Euclidean algorithm on exponents, so the final gcd is x^6 − 1.

Source: Technique from Andreescu & Dospinescu ch. 10–11, Gelca & Andreescu 2.2 (original problem)

Problem p19. How many monic irreducible quadratics x² + ax + b are there over the integers modulo 3?

HintA monic quadratic is reducible exactly when it has a root.
Answer3
Method: Count reducibles

There are 9 monic quadratics; reducible ones are (x − r)(x − s): 3 choices with r = s plus 3 with r ≠ s, total 6. So 3 irreducible.

Method: Direct

Test every pair (a, b) for a root t in {0, …, 2}; exactly 3 pairs have none.

Source: Technique from Andreescu & Dospinescu ch. 10–11, Gelca & Andreescu 2.2 (original problem)

Problem p22. The roots of t³ − 6t² + 11t − 6 are a, b, c. Find a^5 + b^5 + c^5.

Answer276
Method: Recurrence

With p₁ = 6 and p₂ = 14, repeated use of pₖ = 6·pₖ₋₁ − (11)·pₖ₋₂ + (6)·pₖ₋₃ gives p5 = 276.

Method: Numerically
